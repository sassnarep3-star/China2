Cost breakdown

The conversion penalty decomposed into its actual components, each tied to an official source or explicitly marked unverified.

This is the breakdown the brief asked for. It separates the four things that are routinely conflated as "the cost of getting money out of China." Only one of them is a tax on conversion, and that one is usually zero.

What this means in practice

If the Chinese tax on the income has already been paid, the lawful cost of converting the balance is component 3 plus component 4: the bank's spread and the number of years the quota forces you to spread the transfer over. That is why the cheapest lawful strategy is not to hunt for a tax shelter — it is to (a) make sure component 2 is actually settled, so the authenticity review passes; (b) claim component 8 so it is not paid twice; (c) minimise component 3 by getting competing written quotes; and (d) shorten component 4 by qualifying for the emigrant asset transfer or a genuine trade settlement account, both of which sit outside the quota.

Every "cheap" alternative advertised online attacks component 4 by breaking the law, and in doing so converts a modest, quantifiable cost into component 6 — an unquantified penalty exposure plus, on the U.S. side, component 5. That trade is not worth making, and the site does not recommend it.
