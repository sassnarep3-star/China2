Every method to convert CNY into USD

What each route is, who may use it, what it needs, and what it costs you if you get it wrong.

Twenty-seven mechanisms were located. Only thirteen are lawful on documents alone; seven are lawful only if a specific condition is genuinely met, and several of those conditions could not be verified in this session; seven are unlawful and are listed with the statute that penalises them so that nobody has to guess where the line is.

Read the status column literally. lawful means an official source reviewed here establishes the mechanism. conditional means the mechanism is lawful or not verified — check the method's own notes and the Flagged for Review page before relying on it. unlawful means an official source prohibits it.

Lawful routes

Available now, on documents.

lawful M01 · Documented lawful income: bank FX purchase + SWIFT wire

Take the RMB balance to the bank that holds it, buy USD against proof of the underlying lawful income (including the PRC tax record), then wire the USD to your own US account.

Who can use it
Works for both characterisations: a non-domestic individual under S02 Art. 13(1), or a domestic individual above the annual total under S02 Art. 12.
Capacity
No USD 50,000 purchase-quota ceiling where the money is proved lawful current-account income; the ceiling is the size of the documented income. Overseas property, securities and investment-type insurance are excluded declared purposes (S10).
Published cost
published fee 0.1% of amount, min CNY 100, max CNY 500, plus CNY 120 cable · S11
PRC tax
No PRC tax on the exchange itself. PRC individual income tax was due on the income when earned (IIT Law Art. 2 lists the nine taxable categories - a currency exchange is not one of them). If the underlying income was never taxed, the tax is on the income, not on the conversion.
U.S. tax
The conversion is a disposition of nonfunctional currency under 26 U.S.C. 988(c)(1)(C)(i); as a personal transaction it is excluded by 988(e)(1)-(2) unless the exchange gain exceeds USD 200. Income earned in RMB must be translated when earned.
Penalty if wrong
None if documented. Undocumented or false documents expose you to a fine of up to CNY 50,000 for the individual (S05 Art. 48) and, if the conversion is found to be illegal FX arbitrage, up to 30% of the amount (S05 Art. 40).

Pros

  • No published USD 50,000 ceiling when the income is documented, so a six-figure balance does not need to be spread over years.
  • Conversion of already-taxed income is not itself a PRC taxing event in the instruments reviewed (S02, S05): the tax was on the income, not on the exchange.
  • Clean audit trail for both SAFE and the receiving US bank.

Cons

  • Counter review is discretionary and a thin file is refused; the tax record is the usual sticking point.
  • The bank's spread is not published in advance and is the largest component of cost.
  • Intermediary and receiving-bank deductions are outside the published tariff.
Documents needed
  • Passport (and any China residence permit)
  • Employment contract, invoices or service agreement proving the income
  • PRC individual income tax payment record or tax certificate (完税证明 / 纳税记录), years matching the balance [S02 Art. 13(1); S03]
  • Bank statements showing the credits into the account
  • Signed China-side source-of-funds statement, and the receiving US bank's source-of-funds form
How it works, step by step
  1. Identify which characterisation applies: US citizen residing in China for a continuous year or more is a domestic individual (S05 Art. 52(2)); otherwise a non-domestic individual. This decides whether S02 Art. 12 or Art. 13(1) is the operative article, and both require documents.
  2. Obtain the PRC tax record for every year represented in the balance: download the personal income tax payment record from the national tax app, or request a tax certificate at the tax service hall. This is the document the bank will ask for [S02 Art. 13(1); S03].
  3. Assemble the income file so that the amounts tie exactly to the balance: contract, payslips or invoices, bank credits, tax record.
  4. Book a counter appointment at the branch that holds the account. Ask, in writing, for an executable quote showing the applied CNY/USD rate, the commission, the cable charge and any intermediary deduction [S11 items 2.2.4.1-2.2.4.2].
  5. Complete the FX purchase at the counter, and keep the exchange memo (水单) - it is the evidence that the conversion was authorised, and it is the document needed for any later re-conversion within 24 months [S02 Art. 13(2)].
  6. Instruct the wire to your own US account in the same name. Keep the SWIFT MT103 and the remittance advice.
  7. Confirm the USD arrives net of intermediary charges. The bank's tariff expressly excludes other institutions' charges [S11 Important Note 7].
  8. Report the account on FinCEN Form 114 (FBAR) by 15 April, and Form 8938 with the return if the threshold is met [S15, S18, S19]. Claim any PRC tax already paid by treaty or by section 901/903; the mechanics of that credit were not re-verified in this session.

lawful M02 · Documented income routed through same-name HSBC Global Transfer

Convert inside HSBC China exactly as in M01, then move the USD to your own HSBC US account through Global Transfer to Self instead of an ordinary SWIFT wire.

Who can use it
As M01, but you hold or can open HSBC accounts on both sides and meet the Premier balance condition.
Capacity
Same documentary ceiling as M01. The tariff's availability remark lists Canada, US, UK, Australia and Hong Kong SAR for Global Transfer [S11].
Published cost
published fee USD 0.00 · S11
PRC tax
Same as M01: no PRC tax on the exchange of already-taxed income.
U.S. tax
Same as M01: 988(e)(2) excludes personal-transaction exchange gain up to USD 200.
Penalty if wrong
None if documented; same statutory exposure as M01 for false documents.

Pros

  • No published fixed fee for Private Banking or Premier same-name Global Transfer (S11 2.2.6.1).
  • Fewer correspondent hops, so fewer unexpected deductions.

Cons

  • Requires an eligible HSBC relationship on both sides; Premier requires CNY 500,000 or equivalent on deposit (S11 Important Note 3).
  • The CNY-to-USD spread, which is the dominant cost, is unchanged.
  • A US person opening a Chinese bank account faces enhanced due diligence.
Documents needed
  • Everything in M01
  • HSBC US account in exactly the same name
  • Premier status on both sides: last month's Total Relationship Balance of at least CNY 500,000 or equivalent [S11 Important Note 3]
How it works, step by step
  1. Confirm the two accounts are in exactly the same name and that the China-to-US corridor is enabled for Global Transfer to Self.
  2. Complete the M01 documentation and the CNY-to-USD purchase inside HSBC China.
  3. Send the USD by Global Transfer to Self. Under the tariff effective 31 October 2026 this item is published Free for Private Banking and Premier; for Advance it is CNY 145 indicative per transaction with an actual charge of USD 20 [S11 item 2.2.6.1].
  4. Keep the transfer confirmation; the receiving bank still needs a source-of-funds note.
  5. File FBAR, Form 8938 and any credit claim exactly as in M01.

lawful M03 · Emigration asset transfer (移民财产转移) - approved, staged remittance

Apply once to SAFE for the whole amount, then remit in stages over up to two years through a designated bank, against a tax certificate.

Who can use it
A natural person who emigrated from mainland China (or settled in Hong Kong/Macau) and is moving lawful assets they owned before acquiring the immigration status. For a naturalised US citizen who cancelled Chinese household registration, this is the route designed for exactly this situation.
Capacity
No upper limit stated in the measure. Approval level: up to RMB 500,000 equivalent by the local SAFE branch; above that by SAFE head office [S06 Art. 10]. Staging: no more than half first, then no more than half of the remainder after one year, then the balance after two years; amounts up to RMB 200,000 may go in one transfer after approval [S06 Art. 6].
Published cost
published fee 0.1% of amount, min CNY 100, max CNY 500, plus CNY 120 cable · S06
PRC tax
PRC tax attaches to the realisation of the assets (for example property transfer income at 20% of the gain under IIT Law Art. 3(3)), not to the FX conversion. The tax certificate is a required document.
U.S. tax
A personal-transaction conversion is excluded by 988(e)(2) up to USD 200 of exchange gain. Any gain on the sale of Chinese property is a separate US tax question and was not analysed here.
Penalty if wrong
Applications with false materials or repeat applications for the same property are punished under S05 Art. 40 (illegal FX arbitrage), up to 30% of the amount; the bank is separately liable for processing outside the measure [S06 Art. 13].

Pros

  • The only route in the reviewed instruments that is expressly built to move a large, already-owned balance abroad without a USD 50,000 ceiling.
  • Staged remittance is authorised by the approval, so it is not 'structuring'.
  • Direct remittance to your own overseas account is mandated, which suits a US citizen.

Cons

  • Requires cancelled Chinese household registration, which many naturalised citizens no longer have.
  • One application, then up to two years to get everything out.
  • Head-office approval above RMB 500,000: slow, discretionary, and document-heavy.
  • A tax certificate is mandatory, so untaxed income cannot pass.
Documents needed
  • Written application describing the reason and the source and realisation of the assets [S06 Art. 8(1)]
  • Completed applicant and application forms [S06 Art. 8(2)-(3)]
  • Identity documents, plus the cancelled Chinese household registration certificate from the police and proof of settlement abroad certified by a Chinese embassy or consulate [S06 Art. 8(4)]
  • Proof of property rights: title deed, sale contract, demolition compensation agreement or other evidence [S06 Art. 8(5)]
  • Tax certificate or tax payment voucher issued by the competent tax authority where the assets or income are located [S06 Art. 8(6)]
  • Anything else SAFE requests [S06 Art. 8(7)]; notarisation of un-notarised documents [S06 Art. 8 closing paragraph]
How it works, step by step
  1. Confirm you hold the immigration documents the measure requires: cancelled Chinese household registration (户籍注销证明) and certified proof of settlement abroad [S06 Art. 8(4)]. Without these the route is closed.
  2. Realise the assets in China and pay any PRC tax that arises on the realisation, then obtain the tax certificate or payment voucher from the competent tax authority [S06 Art. 8(6)].
  3. File one application for the whole amount with the SAFE branch at your former household registration location; the property must be your own lawful property and free of disputes [S06 Arts. 3, 5].
  4. If the total is above RMB 500,000 equivalent, the branch forwards it to SAFE head office for approval [S06 Art. 10].
  5. On approval, take the approval letter and the capital-account approval document to the designated bank and buy USD [S06 Art. 10].
  6. Remit directly to your own account in your country of residence. The bank may not hand you FX cash inside China [S06 Art. 11].
  7. For the second and later tranches, return with the earlier approval letter and approval document to the same SAFE branch [S06 Art. 8 closing paragraph].
  8. File FBAR and Form 8938 for the year(s) the account existed, and FinCEN Form 105 only if you physically carry currency [S15, S18, S20].

lawful M04 · Inheritance asset transfer (继承财产转移)

Realise the inherited Chinese estate, apply once to SAFE at the deceased's former household registration location, and remit abroad against a tax certificate.

Who can use it
A foreign citizen (including a US citizen) or a Hong Kong/Macau resident who has lawfully inherited assets located in mainland China.
Capacity
No upper limit stated; same RMB 500,000 approval split and direct-to-own-account rule as M03 [S06 Arts. 10, 11]. Inherited property from several deceased persons must be applied for separately [S06 Art. 6].
Published cost
published fee 0.1% of amount, min CNY 100, max CNY 500, plus CNY 120 cable · S06
PRC tax
PRC tax on the realisation of the estate, not on the exchange; the tax certificate is a required document [S06 Art. 9(5)].
U.S. tax
Inheritances are generally not income to the recipient (IRS Publication 525, 'Gifts and inheritances'), while the conversion is a personal transaction excluded by 988(e)(2) up to USD 200 of gain.
Penalty if wrong
False materials engage S05 Art. 40 (up to 30% of the amount); the bank is liable for processing outside the measure [S06 Art. 13].

Pros

  • Expressly available to foreign citizens, so a US passport is not an obstacle.
  • No USD 50,000 ceiling.
  • May be remitted in one go or in instalments after a single application [S06 Art. 6].

Cons

  • Requires a completed inheritance with notarised documents, which is slow and expensive in China.
  • A tax certificate is mandatory.
  • Approval is discretionary and the file must be complete.
Documents needed
  • Written application including the relationship to the deceased and the source and realisation of the estate [S06 Art. 9(1)]
  • Application forms [S06 Art. 9(2)]
  • Identity documents plus certified proof of settlement abroad for a foreign citizen [S06 Art. 9(3)]
  • Proof of inheritance [S06 Art. 9(4)]
  • The deceased's property-right documents and the tax certificate or payment voucher from the competent tax authority [S06 Art. 9(5)]
  • Notarisation of the agency agreement, the inheritance proof and the deceased's title documents where not already notarised [S06 Art. 9 closing paragraph]
How it works, step by step
  1. Complete the inheritance formalities in China and obtain notarised proof of inheritance.
  2. Realise the estate and settle any PRC tax arising, obtaining the tax certificate.
  3. File the application with the SAFE branch at the deceased's former household registration location; a central sub-branch may accept the file [S06 Art. 5].
  4. Obtain approval (head office above RMB 500,000 equivalent).
  5. Buy USD at the designated bank and remit to your own overseas account; cash payment inside China is prohibited [S06 Art. 11].
  6. Keep the approval documents for any additional remittance.
  7. Report the account and the receipt on FBAR and Form 8938 as applicable [S15, S18].

lawful M05 · Re-conversion of unused RMB against the original exchange memo

Present the original exchange memo (水单) and your ID; the bank converts the unused RMB back to the original currency and you remit it.

Who can use it
A non-domestic individual whose RMB came from converting foreign currency inside China, who still holds the original exchange memo and is inside the 24-month window.
Capacity
The memo is valid for 24 months from the exchange date [S02 Art. 13(2); S04]. Same-day totals up to USD 500 (or USD 1,000 at a port-side location before departure) can be handled on ID alone [S02 Art. 13(2); S04].
Published cost
published fee 0.1% of amount, min CNY 100, max CNY 500, plus CNY 120 cable · S02
PRC tax
None on the conversion: this is the return of previously settled principal, and no tax voucher is required for the category [S02 Art. 13(2); S04].
U.S. tax
Personal transaction under 988(e)(1)-(2): no recognition of exchange gain up to USD 200.
Penalty if wrong
None if the memo is genuine; presenting a false memo falls under S05 Art. 40.

Pros

  • No tax certificate is required for this category, which makes it the cleanest documentary path where it applies.
  • No USD 50,000 quota interaction: it is the return of your own principal.

Cons

  • Only works for RMB that came from FX you brought in, which is usually not the case for salary savings.
  • 24-month expiry.
  • The memo is a single physical document and is commonly lost.
Documents needed
  • Passport
  • Original exchange memo (原兑换水单) for each conversion being reversed
  • Bank statements showing the original conversion
How it works, step by step
  1. Locate the original exchange memo for every conversion you want to reverse; without it only the small ID-only amounts in step 2 are available.
  2. For a small residue, same-day totals up to USD 500 (or USD 1,000 at a port-side location before departure) may be converted on ID alone [S04].
  3. Check the date: the memo is valid for 24 months from the exchange date [S02 Art. 13(2)].
  4. Present the passport and memo at the counter and request conversion back to the original currency.
  5. Remit to your own account, keeping the new memo as the receipt for the outbound leg.
  6. Report on FBAR/8938 as in M01.

lawful M06 · Remit foreign currency already sitting in a Chinese FX account (no CNY conversion)

If part of the balance is already in USD, skip the conversion entirely and remit the existing foreign currency.

Who can use it
Anyone holding USD (or another currency) in a foreign-exchange savings account in China, so that no CNY-to-USD conversion is needed at all.
Capacity
Same-day remittance from a foreign-exchange savings account up to USD 50,000 equivalent on ID alone; above that, with authenticity documents [S02 Art. 14]. Cash-funded remittance up to USD 10,000 same-day on ID [S02 Art. 14].
Published cost
published fee 0.1% of amount, min CNY 100, max CNY 500, plus CNY 120 cable · S02
PRC tax
None: there is no conversion and the funds are your own already-settled foreign currency.
U.S. tax
No 988 disposition occurs if no currency is exchanged; the account is still reportable.
Penalty if wrong
None if the documents are accurate (S05 Art. 48 applies to false or missing documents).

Pros

  • No exchange at all, so no spread on that portion.
  • Simplest documentary path for that portion.

Cons

  • Only helps for the part of the balance not held in RMB.
  • Above USD 50,000 same-day, documents are still required.
Documents needed
  • Passport
  • Bank statements for the FX account
  • Authenticity documents where the same-day amount exceeds USD 50,000
How it works, step by step
  1. Ask the bank whether any part of the balance is already denominated in USD or another convertible currency.
  2. Remit up to USD 50,000 equivalent same-day on ID alone, or present authenticity documents for a larger amount [S02 Art. 14].
  3. If the money is held as FX cash in hand, the same-day ceiling is USD 10,000 on ID [S02 Art. 14].
  4. Keep the remittance advice and report on FBAR/8938.

Sources: S02 · S01 · S11

lawful M07 · Sale proceeds of Chinese property, then remit through an approved channel

Sell the property, pay PRC tax on the gain, then move the money out under whichever of M01, M03 or M04 fits your status. There is no separate 'property proceeds' FX channel.

Who can use it
An owner of Chinese real property who sells it and then remits the proceeds either as documented income, as an emigration transfer, or as an inheritance transfer.
Capacity
Determined by the channel used: M01 (documented income), M03 (emigration) or M04 (inheritance). Overseas property purchase with converted RMB is an excluded purpose in the other direction [S10].
Published cost
published fee 0.1% of amount, min CNY 100, max CNY 500, plus CNY 120 cable · S06
PRC tax
20% of the gain on the transfer of property (IIT Law Art. 3(3), category 财产转让所得). This is the dominant tax and it arises on the sale, not on the exchange.
U.S. tax
A US person is taxable on worldwide capital gain; foreign tax paid may be creditable, and the mechanics were not re-verified here. The conversion itself remains a 988(e) personal transaction.
Penalty if wrong
Under-declaring the sale price in China is a tax offence and would also invalidate the tax certificate relied on for the FX channel.

Pros

  • Property sale proceeds are one of the few large, cleanly documentable sources of RMB.
  • The tax paid on the transfer is itself the tax certificate the FX channel needs.

Cons

  • The 20% PRC tax on the gain is a real cost and cannot be avoided by choosing a different FX route.
  • No dedicated FX channel exists: you must fit the proceeds into M01, M03 or M04.
Documents needed
  • Title deed and sale contract
  • PRC tax payment record for the property transfer
  • Plus the documents of the channel used (M01, M03 or M04)
How it works, step by step
  1. Complete the sale in China and settle PRC tax on the transfer; obtain the tax payment record. Property transfer income is taxed at 20% of the gain under IIT Law Art. 3(3).
  2. Choose the channel: documented income (M01), emigration transfer (M03) if you have cancelled Chinese household registration and settled abroad, or inheritance transfer (M04) if the property came by inheritance.
  3. Assemble the file for that channel and apply, or present documents at the counter.
  4. Complete the FX purchase and remit to your own account.
  5. Report the account, and the sale, on the US side as applicable [S15, S18].

lawful M08 · A direct relative acts for you within your own annual total

Your parent, child or spouse handles the paperwork in China as your agent. This is agency, not a transfer of someone else's quota.

Who can use it
Anyone with a parent, child or spouse in China able to attend the counter on your behalf.
Capacity
Your own annual total only. Where the amount exceeds the annual total, the relative may still act, but must produce the full supporting documents [S02 Arts. 4, 38]. Using a relative's own quota instead is a different and prohibited act (U04).
Published cost
published fee 0.1% of amount, min CNY 100, max CNY 500, plus CNY 120 cable · S02
PRC tax
Depends on the underlying channel; the agency itself is not a taxable event.
U.S. tax
Agency does not change the US analysis: the disposition is still yours.
Penalty if wrong
Doing the same thing with a non-relative, or using their quota, is penalised: watch-list entry for that year plus two more [S09].

Pros

  • Expressly permitted, and it is not quota-borrowing.
  • Removes the need for you to travel to China for a counter transaction.

Cons

  • Only parents, children and spouse qualify [S02 Art. 38].
  • Banks increasingly require notarised authorisations.
  • It never increases your own annual total.
Documents needed
  • Both parties' valid ID documents
  • Your signed authorisation letter (授权书)
  • Proof of the direct relationship: household register, marriage certificate, or a certificate from a government office, police or notary [S02 Art. 38]
  • The documents for the underlying channel (M01)
How it works, step by step
  1. Confirm the relationship is one the rule recognises: parents, children or spouse only [S02 Art. 38].
  2. Sign an authorisation letter and have it legalised or notarised as the bank requires.
  3. Assemble proof of the relationship: household register, marriage certificate, or a certificate from a sub-district office, police or notary [S02 Art. 38].
  4. The relative attends the counter with both IDs and the authorisation, within your own annual total [S02 Arts. 4, 38].
  5. Above the annual total, the same agency is allowed but the full supporting documents for the category must be produced [S02 Art. 4].
  6. Keep copies of everything: an agency file with a gap is refused.

Sources: S02 · S09 · S11 · S22

lawful M09 · Do nothing: keep the RMB in China and spend it there

Leave the balance where it is. No conversion, no spread, no US tax on a conversion - but the account is still reportable.

Who can use it
Anyone not needing the money in USD immediately.
Capacity
Not applicable.
Published cost
no published fee (statutory route) · S15
PRC tax
Interest earned may be subject to PRC tax depending on its character; not analysed here.
U.S. tax
No 988 disposition because nothing is converted. Interest income remains reportable.
Penalty if wrong
Non-filing exposure only: FBAR non-willful maximum USD 16,536 per violation, willful maximum USD 165,353 (S16); Form 8938 USD 10,000 plus up to USD 50,000 (S18).

Pros

  • Zero conversion cost and zero exchange-gain exposure.
  • No PRC documentary review.

Cons

  • Currency risk stays with you: an RMB depreciation is a loss in USD terms.
  • FBAR and Form 8938 obligations continue regardless [S15, S18].
  • The documentation problem does not go away; it compounds.
Documents needed
  • Nothing to file in China
  • FBAR (FinCEN 114) and Form 8938 as applicable in the US [S15, S18]
How it works, step by step
  1. Nothing to do in China.
  2. Continue filing FBAR by 15 April each year if the aggregate of all foreign accounts exceeded USD 10,000 at any time in the year [S15].
  3. Continue filing Form 8938 with your return if the applicable threshold is met [S18, S19].
  4. Revisit the decision if the RMB needs to become USD, or if the bank's documentation requirements change.

lawful M10 · Pay US expenses directly with a China-issued card (consume, do not convert)

Rather than converting the balance, spend it. The card scheme converts at settlement, and SAFE states that card spending abroad repaid in FX after return does not count against the annual total [S10].

Who can use it
Anyone who can pay US merchants, tuition or bills with a UnionPay/Visa/Mastercard card issued in China.
Capacity
No published annual cap for genuine card spending. Cash withdrawals are separately capped (see M11).
Published cost
fee not published — blank · S10
PRC tax
None on the spend.
U.S. tax
No 988 disposition until the card is settled and the currency is bought; the final FX purchase is a separate event.
Penalty if wrong
Cash advances above the ATM limits engage S08 point 1.

Pros

  • No FX purchase at the counter and no conversion paperwork.
  • Card spending abroad repaid in FX after return does not count against the annual total [S10].

Cons

lawful M12 · Carry USD banknotes out of China

Buy USD banknotes in China and carry them out: up to USD 5,000 with no permit, up to USD 10,000 with a bank-issued carrying certificate, and in principle no more than USD 10,000 at all [S07].

Who can use it
Anyone willing to travel with cash within the published limits.
Capacity
No published annual limit, but each departure is capped at USD 5,000 without a permit and USD 10,000 with one; above USD 10,000 is in principle prohibited except for listed special cases with a SAFE permit [S07]. Moving USD 400,000 this way would need 40-80 separate departures.
Published cost
published fee USD 0.00 · S07
PRC tax
None on the purchase of banknotes.
U.S. tax
The purchase is a 988 disposition; as a personal transaction it is excluded up to USD 200 of gain. The cash is not income.
Penalty if wrong
Carrying FX in breach of the rules is punishable by a warning and a fine of up to 20% of the amount (S05 Art. 42); failing to declare on entry to the US risks forfeiture and seizure (S20).

Pros

  • Physical settlement, so no wire fee and no intermediary deductions.
  • The bank's counter note withdrawal is published Free [S11 item 2.2.2.1].

Cons

  • About 0.42% board spread on the notes [S22] plus travel cost.

  • Failure to declare at the US border risks seizure and forfeiture [S20].
Documents needed
  • Passport

How it works, step by step
  1. Buy USD banknotes at the bank; note withdrawal is free at the counter under the HSBC tariff, and the notes buy/sell board rate applies [S11 items 2.2.2.1].
  2. For USD 5,000 or less, no carrying certificate is needed and customs will release the cash [S07].
  3. For USD 5,000-10,000, obtain a carrying certificate from a designated bank and present it to customs [S07].
  4. Do not attempt more than USD 10,000 without a SAFE-issued permit; that is in principle prohibited [S07].
  5. On entering the US with more than USD 10,000 (the threshold applies to the family or group total, not per person), file FinCEN Form 105 with CBP [S20].
