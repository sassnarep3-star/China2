Tax and penalties: what is actually charged

The conversion is not the taxable event. Here are the four things that are, with the statutory figures and the arithmetic shown.

The conversion penalty, broken apart

People describe a "penalty" for converting. In the official texts reviewed it resolves into five different things, and only two of them are actually a cost of exchanging currency:

  1. PRC income tax on the money's origin. Not a conversion tax. The PRC individual income tax law taxes nine listed categories S05 — wages, remuneration, author's remuneration, royalties, business income, interest/dividends, rental, property transfer and incidental income. Exchanging your own renminbi is not among them. If the balance was never taxed, the tax is on the income, at up to 45% for comprehensive income IIT Law Art. 3(1).
  2. U.S. tax on the exchange gain. Real but capped at USD 200 for a personal transaction 26 USC 988(e)(2). Worked below.
  3. The bank's spread. A real cost, roughly 0.42% of the amount on one bank's published board rate S22.
  4. Fixed bank and correspondent fees. Tens of dollars per transfer S11.
  5. Penalties for doing it wrong — either PRC FX penalties, up to 30% of the amount S05 Arts. 39-45, or U.S. reporting penalties, which are the largest numbers in this project S16.

1. PRC tax: what the law actually taxes

QuestionAnswer from the official textSource
Is there a PRC tax on exchanging currency? No tax on the exchange appears in the FX instruments or in the IIT Law. The FX regulations impose documentation and authenticity requirements S05 Art. 12, and the IIT Law taxes categories of income, not conversions IIT Law Art. 2. S01 S05 S07
So why does the bank ask for a tax certificate? Because the FX rule requires it as a document proving the money's source: identity plus source-of-funds materials "including tax vouchers" SAFE 2026 guidance S02 Art. 13(1). A certificate of tax already paid is not a new tax. S03 S02
What if PRC tax was never paid? Then it is owed on the income, separately from the conversion. Rates: 3%-45% progressive on comprehensive income; 5%-35% on business income; a flat 20% on interest, dividends, property rental, property transfer and incidental income IIT Law Art. 3. S07
Does the emigration route avoid the tax? No. It requires a tax certificate or tax payment voucher as a condition of the application S06 Art. 8(6).S06

2. U.S. tax on the conversion itself: 26 U.S.C. 988

Disposing of nonfunctional currency is a section 988 transaction 26 USC 988(c)(1)(C)(i). Then 988(e) takes it away again for individuals: the section "shall not apply to any section 988 transaction entered into by an individual which is a personal transaction" 26 USC 988(e)(1), and "no gain shall be recognized ... by reason of changes in exchange rates ... The preceding sentence shall not apply if the gain ... exceeds $200" 26 USC 988(e)(2). The IRS states the same in plain words in Publication 525.

A "personal transaction" excludes transactions whose expenses meet section 162 or 212 tests 26 USC 988(e)(3). Business or investment use of the money is therefore outside the exclusion, and this is where an account-specific answer would need facts this project does not have.

Worked example: what the 2021-2025 rate move does to a conversion

Using the IRS's own yearly average rates IRS yearly averages — the renminbi went from 6.452 (2021) to 7.129 (2025) per dollar. That is a weaker renminbi, so a dollar buys more renminbi in 2025 than in 2021. Converting renminbi that was acquired earlier therefore produced a loss in dollar terms for 2021-2023 acquisitions, and a small gain for 2024.

The comparison that matters

Converting USD 400,000 through the documented route costs about $1,763.32. A single willful FBAR violation has a published civil maximum of USD 165,353 S16. One unreported year can therefore cost about 94 times the entire cost of converting lawfully. If the money has been sitting in a Chinese account, the largest financial exposure in this project is almost certainly not the conversion — it is whether the account was ever declared.

Maximums are not assessments. Penalty assertion depends on facts and circumstances, and the IRS publishes streamlined and delinquent-filing procedures for people who come forward before being contacted IRS FBAR page, filing delinquent FBARs. Whether any of those procedures apply to you is a question for a qualified adviser, not for this site.
