US Obligations

What the US side costs

A US citizen is taxed on worldwide income, and the treaty's saving clause does not change that. Relief comes from the foreign earned income exclusion and the foreign tax credit — and there is a hidden cost under § 988(e).

The saving clause

Protocol 1, paragraph 2 of the US–China treaty states: "Notwithstanding any provision of the Agreement, the United States may tax its citizens." The treaty does not switch off US tax, and the exchange of notes confirm it contains no tax-sparing credit. Relief comes only from the foreign tax credit on Form 1116 or the foreign earned income exclusion on Form 2555. Official IRS treaty text.

The exclusion does not cover the money in the account

Foreign earned income means wages, salaries, professional fees and other amounts paid for personal services rendered. It does not include money already sitting in a bank account, dividends, interest, or pension income. Once the CNY is no longer earned income, the FEIE cannot shelter it.

The hidden cost — 26 U.S.C. § 988(e) currency gain

The most under-appreciated cost in the whole exercise. Converting CNY into USD can create US taxable income even though China taxes nothing.

RuleEffectSource
§ 988(e)(1)The general rule that FX gains are ordinary income does not apply to an individual's personal transactions§ 988
§ 988(e)(2)Where an individual disposes of nonfunctional currency in a personal transaction, no gain is recognised by reason of exchange-rate movements — but the exclusion does not apply if the gain exceeds USD 200§ 988
§ 988(e)(3)"Personal transaction" excludes transactions to the extent expenses are deductible under § 162 or § 212§ 988
Two points that matter

1. The USD 200 threshold is a cliff, not a de minimis allowance. Cross it by one dollar and the whole gain is in income — not just the excess.
2. There is no Chinese tax credit for it. China does not tax a currency gain, so the US tax on it is pure incremental cost. For someone who acquired CNY years ago at a strong rate and converts today, this can be a double-digit percentage of the balance with no offsetting credit.
