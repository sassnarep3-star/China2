# 3. US Tax and Reporting Obligations of a US Citizen Moving CNY Out of China

**Research date:** 2026-11-07

---

## 3.1 The saving clause — the treaty does not switch off US tax

| Rule | Source |
|---|---|
| "Notwithstanding any provision of the Agreement, the United States may tax its citizens." — Protocol 1, paragraph 2 | [IRS US-1](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-trty/china.pdf) |
| The US–China treaty contains **no tax-sparing credit** (confirmed in the exchange of notes) | [IRS US-1](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-trty/china.pdf) |

**Consequence:** a US citizen cannot rely on the treaty to avoid US tax on China-source income. Relief comes only through the **foreign tax credit** (Form 1116) or the **foreign earned income exclusion** (Form 2555).

## 3.2 Foreign earned income exclusion

| Item | Value | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Maximum exclusion, 2025 | USD 130,000 per qualifying person | [IRS US-3](https://www.irs.gov/individuals/international-taxpayers/figuring-the-foreign-earned-income-exclusion) |
| Maximum exclusion, 2026 | USD 132,900 per person | [IRS US-3](https://www.irs.gov/individuals/international-taxpayers/figuring-the-foreign-earned-income-exclusion) |
| Maximum exclusion, 2024 | USD 126,500 | [IRS US-3](https://www.irs.gov/individuals/international-taxpayers/figuring-the-foreign-earned-income-exclusion) |
| Maximum exclusion, 2023 | USD 120,000 | [IRS US-3](https://www.irs.gov/individuals/international-taxpayers/figuring-the-foreign-earned-income-exclusion) |
| Housing amount limitation, 2025 | USD 39,000 (30% of the exclusion) | [IRS US-3](https://www.irs.gov/individuals/international-taxpayers/figuring-the-foreign-earned-income-exclusion) |
| Housing amount limitation, 2026 | USD 39,870 | [IRS US-3](https://www.irs.gov/individuals/international-taxpayers/figuring-the-foreign-earned-income-exclusion) |
| Two qualifying spouses, 2025 | Up to USD 260,000 combined | [IRS US-3](https://www.irs.gov/individuals/international-taxpayers/figuring-the-foreign-earned-income-exclusion) |
| Tests | Bona fide residence (full tax year) **or** physical presence (330 full days in any 12 consecutive months) | [IRS US-2](https://www.irs.gov/individuals/international-taxpayers/foreign-earned-income-exclusion) |
| Form | Form 2555 | [IRS US-2](https://www.irs.gov/individuals/international-taxpayers/foreign-earned-income-exclusion) |
| Interaction with the foreign tax credit | A credit cannot be claimed for tax on income that has been excluded | [IRS US-8](https://www.irs.gov/individuals/international-taxpayers/foreign-tax-credit) |

**Critical limitation for this problem:** the exclusion applies only to *foreign earned income* — wages, salaries, professional fees for personal services. It **does not** cover money already sitting in a bank account, dividends, interest, or pension income. Once the CNY in the account is no longer "earned income", the FEIE cannot shelter it.

## 3.3 Foreign tax credit

| Item | Source |
|---|---|
| Credit is available for income taxes paid to China, reducing US tax on the same income dollar-for-dollar up to the limitation | [IRS US-8](https://www.irs.gov/individuals/international-taxpayers/foreign-tax-credit) |
| Where a treaty reduces the foreign rate, only the tax actually paid qualifies for credit; a refund claim for the excess must be filed with China | [IRS US-8](https://www.irs.gov/individuals/international-taxpayers/foreign-tax-credit) |
| Individuals claim on Form 1116 | [IRS US-8](https://www.irs.gov/individuals/international-taxpayers/foreign-tax-credit) |
| Treaty art. 22 confirms the US will credit China income tax paid by or on behalf of a US citizen or resident | [IRS US-1](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-trty/china.pdf) |

**Consequence:** the 25% EIT + 10% WHT paid on a dividend is largely recoverable in the US through the foreign tax credit — *provided the income is taxed in the US at a rate at least as high as the Chinese leakage* and the credit is not limited by the Form 1116 baskets.

## 3.4 FBAR (FinCEN Form 114)

| Item | Value | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Who must file | US persons with a financial interest in, or signature authority over, at least one foreign financial account | [IRS US-4](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/report-of-foreign-bank-and-financial-accounts-fbar) |
| Threshold | Aggregate value of foreign financial accounts exceeded USD 10,000 **at any time** during the calendar year | [IRS US-4](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/report-of-foreign-bank-and-financial-accounts-fbar) |
| Due date | 15 April following the calendar year, automatic extension to 15 October | [IRS US-4](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/report-of-foreign-bank-and-financial-accounts-fbar) |
| Where filed | FinCEN BSA E-Filing System — not with the tax return | [IRS US-4](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/report-of-foreign-bank-and-financial-accounts-fbar) |
| Taxability of the account is irrelevant | Whether the account produced taxable income has no effect on FBAR obligations | [IRS US-4](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/report-of-foreign-bank-and-financial-accounts-fbar) |
| Record retention | 5 years from the due date | [IRS US-4](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/report-of-foreign-bank-and-financial-accounts-fbar) |
| Civil penalty, non-willful (per report), effective 17 Jan 2025 | USD 16,536 | [eCFR US-7](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-31/subtitle-B/chapter-X/part-1010/subpart-H/section-1010.821) |
| Civil penalty, willful (per account per year), effective 17 Jan 2025 | The greater of USD 165,353 or 50% of the balance | [eCFR US-7](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-31/subtitle-B/chapter-X/part-1010/subpart-H/section-1010.821) |

> **A CNY bank account in mainland China is a foreign financial account.** The FBAR obligation exists whether or not the money ever moves, and continues for every year the balance exceeds USD 10,000 at any point.

## 3.6 Currency gain on conversion — 26 U.S.C. § 988(e)

This is the most under-appreciated cost in the whole exercise.

| Rule | Text | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| § 988(e)(1) | The general rule that FX gains are ordinary income **does not apply** to a section 988 transaction entered into by an individual that is a **personal transaction** | [26 U.S.C. § 988](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/988) |
| § 988(e)(2) | Where an individual disposes of nonfunctional currency in a personal transaction, **no gain is recognised** by reason of exchange-rate movements between acquisition and disposition — **but this exclusion does not apply if the gain that would otherwise be recognised exceeds USD 200** | [26 U.S.C. § 988](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/988) |
| § 988(e)(3) | "Personal transaction" excludes transactions to the extent expenses are deductible under § 162 or § 212 | [26 U.S.C. § 988](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/988) |

**Worked example.** CNY 1,000,000 acquired at 6.50 (USD 153,846 basis). Converted at 7.20 → USD 138,889. The USD value fell, so there is a loss, not a gain. But if the account was funded when the rate was 7.30 and converted at 7.20, the gain is USD 1,370 — well above USD 200, so the exclusion is lost and the gain is **taxable ordinary income** in the US, with no Chinese tax credit available against it (China does not tax the gain).

The larger and more common exposure: money earned over many years and converted in one lump sum. Every dollar of exchange-rate appreciation between acquisition and conversion above the USD 200 de minimis is ordinary US income.

*Source note: § 988 was read at the Cornell Legal Information Institute, which is not a government site. The official text at uscode.house.gov could not be loaded from this environment. Flagged for manual verification.*
