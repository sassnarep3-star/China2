# 5. Conversion Methods: Full Catalogue with Pros and Cons

**Research date:** 2026-11-07

Each method is scored on the two things that matter to a US citizen:
- **China tax leakage** — Chinese income tax / VAT / surcharges / penalties.
- **US tax leakage** — US income tax on top, including the § 988(e) currency gain, plus reporting obligations.

Legal-risk ratings refer to Chinese FX control law. **No value in the Cost column is an estimate** — where an official rate was not retrieved the cell is blank and the row appears on the Flagged for Review page.

## Group A — Lawful current-account channels

### A1. Direct bank remittance of personal, already-taxed CNY
Convert CNY at a mainland bank and wire USD to a US account under the **current account**, presenting documents proving the money was lawfully obtained and already taxed.

| | |
|---|---|
| **How it works** | The bank sells you USD (within the USD 50,000 annual quota on ID alone) and wires it out. Beyond the quota, you present ID plus documents proving the transaction is genuine. |
| **China tax leakage** | **0%** on the principal if the money was already taxed in China when earned. No Chinese tax on the conversion itself. |
| **US tax leakage** | Only the § 988(e) currency gain, if the CNY has appreciated against your acquisition cost by more than USD 200. |
| **Costs** | Bank FX spread + wire charges. *(No official source for the spread — left blank.)* |
| **Legal risk** | Low. SAFE explicitly permits beyond-quota conversion with genuine supporting documents. |
| **Pros** | Cheapest lawful route; no additional Chinese tax; no approval process; money arrives in days; preserves the FBAR/8938 history cleanly. |
| **Cons** | Requires the bank to accept your proof of lawful source; the USD 50,000 quota is a documentation threshold, so a single large transfer needs a strong paper trail; the bank may ask for tax records going back years. |
| **Sources** | [SAFE quota](https://www.safe.gov.cn/en/2007/0105/819.html) · [SAFE beyond-quota with documents](https://www.safe.gov.cn/en/2015/1111/1175.html) · [SAFE violation consequences](https://www.safe.gov.cn/en/2017/0119/1244.html) · [§ 988(e)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/988) |

### A2. Remittance of after-tax salary while still employed in China
If you are a foreign national employed in China, your after-tax salary can be converted and remitted.

| | |
|---|---|
| **China tax leakage** | 0% additional — IIT was already withheld by the employer at source. |
| **Annual cap** | Professional commentary states there is **no annual quota** for foreign individuals converting legally-earned after-tax salary, and that the tax-clearance certificate requirement was waived. *(Secondary source — flagged.)* |
| **US tax leakage** | Covered by the foreign earned income exclusion if you still qualify (bona fide residence or 330-day physical presence). |
| **Pros** | Lawful, routine, no approval, no additional Chinese tax, and the FEIE can zero out the US side. |
| **Cons** | Only works while you are still employed and qualifying; the employer's cooperation is needed; documents required are employment contract + IIT payment proof. |
| **Sources** | [STA IIT Law — employer withholding](https://guangdong.chinatax.gov.cn/gdsw/yhyshjzt2024_zcwj_tgfgzlE/2024-07/19/content_01346c4ecaee40b2af8aa3f94a4ee107.shtml) · [IRS FEIE](https://www.irs.gov/individuals/international-taxpayers/figuring-the-foreign-earned-income-exclusion) · [Ecovis/DLA Piper — no annual quota](https://www.ecovis.com/focus-china/chinas-new-foreign-exchange-rules-waive-tax-clearance-for-payroll-remittances/) (secondary) |

### A3. Outward remittance of WFOE dividends
A wholly foreign-owned enterprise in China remits its after-tax profit to the US parent/shareholder.

| | |
|---|---|
| **How it works** | Company pays EIT, appropriates the statutory surplus reserve, then remits the dividend. Recorded under the **current account**, handled at the bank with evidencing documents, **no FX quota**. |
| **China tax leakage** | 25% EIT + 10% WHT (treaty-capped) = **32.5% effective**. |
| **US tax leakage** | Dividend is US-taxable, but the 35% of Chinese tax paid is creditable on Form 1116. Net US cost is typically zero if the US rate is not higher. |
| **Legal risk** | Low — SAFE confirms outward profit remittance needs only genuine documentation, no quota. |
| **Pros** | Fully lawful and routine; no FX approval; treaty halves the dividend WHT (20% → 10%); FTC largely eliminates US double tax. |
| **Cons** | 32.5% combined leakage is the "considerable tax" the question refers to; requires a real, profitable, audited WFOE; the 10% statutory surplus reserve must be appropriated first; tax recordal required for payments over USD 50,000. |
| **Sources** | [SAFE — profit remittance is current account, no quota](https://www.safe.gov.cn/en/2017/0126/1248.html) · [EIT Law Art. 4 — 25%](https://zhejiang.chinatax.gov.cn/art/2024-10-30/art_26318_626886.html) · [treaty Art. 9(2) — 10% WHT](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-trty/china.pdf) · [IRS FTC](https://www.irs.gov/individuals/international-taxpayers/foreign-tax-credit) |

### A4. Intra-group service fees (management, technical, royalty-like services)
The WFOE pays a US affiliate for services actually rendered.

| | |
|---|---|
| **China tax leakage** | VAT + surcharges on the service fee. EIT applies only if the US affiliate has a permanent establishment in China — and the treaty limits that. |
| **US tax leakage** | The fee is US-source business income of the US affiliate, taxable in the US, but creditable for Chinese VAT? **No** — VAT is not an income tax and is not creditable under the FTC. |
| **Legal risk** | Low if the services are genuine and arm's-length. Transfer-pricing exposure if they are not. |
| **Pros** | Moves money without the 32.5% dividend leakage; deductible to the WFOE. |
| **Cons** | VAT is not creditable against US tax, so it is a real cost; tax recordal required above USD 50,000; transfer-pricing scrutiny; the service must be real and documented. |
| **Sources** | [Announcement 40 existence](https://www.safe.gov.cn/en/file/file/20170913/e2053fa3ba074f0aa04e0381cbf0f826.pdf) · [treaty Art. 7 — profits taxable only in residence state absent PE](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-trty/china.pdf) · [IRS FTC — only income taxes qualify](https://www.irs.gov/individuals/international-taxpayers/foreign-tax-credit) · *VAT rate not verified — flagged* |

### A5. Royalties and interest
| | |
|---|---|
| **China tax leakage** | 10% WHT under the treaty (20% statutory domestically) + 6% VAT on royalties. |
| **US tax leakage** | US-taxable; the 10% WHT is creditable on Form 1116. |
| **Pros** | Treaty caps the WHT at 10% — half the domestic rate. |
| **Cons** | Requires genuine IP or a genuine loan; VAT on royalties is not creditable in the US; thin-capitalisation and transfer-pricing rules on interest; tax recordal above USD 50,000. |
| **Sources** | [treaty Arts. 10(2), 11(2)](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-trty/china.pdf) · [Ann. 61/2018 — 20% statutory](https://fgk.chinatax.gov.cn/eng/c102962/c102967/c102997/c103004/c5245843/content.html) · [Guo Shui Han 47 — 10%](https://fgk.chinatax.gov.cn/eng/c102962/c102967/c102997/c103003/c5248318/content.html) · *VAT rate not verified — flagged* |

### A6. Equipment rental royalties
| | |
|---|---|
| **China tax leakage** | Effective **7%** — the treaty Protocol taxes equipment-rental royalties on only 70% of the gross amount, at 10%. |
| **Pros** | The lowest treaty rate available on any passive income stream. |
| **Cons** | Requires a genuine lease of equipment to a Chinese entity; VAT applies and is not US-creditable. |
| **Sources** | [treaty Protocol 1 para. 6 — 70% base](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-trty/china.pdf) · [treaty Art. 11(2)](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-trty/china.pdf) |

### A7. Capital reduction / liquidation of the Chinese entity
Reduce registered capital or wind up the WFOE and remit the residual to the shareholder.

| | |
|---|---|
| **China tax leakage** | EIT on any gain over the original investment; the distributed reserve itself is already after-tax. |
| **Legal risk** | Low on the FX side, but a formal process with creditor notice, audit and SAFE registration. |
| **Pros** | Recovers capital without the dividend WHT on the capital portion. |
| **Cons** | Slow (months); requires shareholder resolution and creditor notice; only makes sense if the entity is being closed; EIT applies to the gain element. |
| **Sources** | [SAFE — current account, evidencing documents at the bank](https://www.safe.gov.cn/en/2017/0126/1248.html) · *procedural detail not verified — flagged* |

### A8. Direct payment of overseas expenses from the CNY account
Pay foreign suppliers, tuition, insurance, mortgages and credit cards directly out of the Chinese account instead of moving the money.

| | |
|---|---|
| **China tax leakage** | 0% — no income is created by paying an expense. |
| **US tax leakage** | 0% — no income, no currency gain, no FBAR movement. |
| **Legal risk** | Low if the expense is genuine and documented. |
| **Pros** | **Zero tax on both sides.** Converts CNY into the *economic benefit* of the payment without ever creating taxable income. Bank document review generally applies above USD 50,000 per payment. |
| **Cons** | Only works to the extent you have genuine overseas obligations; requires contracts and invoices; can't be used for pure investment. |
| **Sources** | [SAFE CN-4 — evidencing documents at the bank](https://www.safe.gov.cn/en/2017/0126/1248.html) · *Huifa [2020] No. 14 art. 49 threshold — secondary, flagged* |

### A9. Overseas ATM withdrawals
| | |
|---|---|
| **China tax leakage** | 0%. |
| **Legal ceiling** | **RMB 100,000 per person per year** across all cards, RMB 10,000 per card per day. Exceeding the annual limit bars the card for the current and following year. Borrowing or lending cards is prohibited. |
| **Pros** | Immediate cash in the destination country; no paperwork. |
| **Cons** | RMB 100,000/year is a hard, small ceiling; ATM fees and poor FX rates; the money arrives as cash, which cannot be used for large transfers; not a route for a large balance. |
| **Sources** | [SAFE Huifa [2017] No. 29](https://www.safe.gov.cn/en/2017/1230/1397.html) |

### A10. Carrying cash out of China
| | |
|---|---|
| **Legal ceiling** | *(Not verified from an official text. Secondary sources report RMB 20,000 cash per trip, FX cash up to USD 5,000 freely, USD 5,000–10,000 with a bank-issued permit, and over USD 10,000 requiring a SAFE warrant. All flagged as unverified.)* |
| **China tax leakage** | 0% on the principal. |
| **Legal risk** | High — illegal carrying of FX carries a warning plus a fine of up to 20% under FX Administration Regulations art. 42. |
| **Pros** | No bank process. |
| **Cons** | Small ceiling; physically risky; a fine of up to 20% if the declaration rules are breached; you must still declare on arrival in the US. |
| **Sources** | [PBOC FX Regs art. 42](https://www.pbc.gov.cn/english/130721/2025080815063231112/index.html) · *cash limits — secondary, flagged* |

### A11. Personal property transfer abroad (the "leaving China" channel)
The formal channel for an individual who has emigrated or is settling abroad to move lawfully-acquired personal property out of China.

| | |
|---|---|
| **How it works** | PBOC Announcement [2004] No. 16 and its implementing rules (Huifa [2004] No. 118, Huifa [2005] No. 9) govern the sales and payment of FX for external transfers of personal property — split into **immigrant property transfer** and **settlement property transfer**. |
| **China tax leakage** | *(Not verified — left blank.)* |
| **Pros** | This is the *designed* lawful channel for exactly this fact pattern; a single transfer can move the whole balance. |
| **Cons** | Slow, document-heavy, and conditional on immigration status and proof of lawful acquisition; the substantive conditions and caps are unverified here. |
| **Sources** | [SAFE catalogue confirming Announcement [2004] No. 16, Huifa [2004] No. 118, Huifa [2005] No. 9](https://www.safe.gov.cn/en/2014/1126/1131.html) · [SAFE English FX law catalogue PDF](https://www.safe.gov.cn/en/file/file/20170913/e2053fa3ba074f0aa04e0381cbf0f826.pdf) |

### A12. Offshore CNH via Hong Kong
Convert CNY to HKD/USD in the offshore CNH market in Hong Kong and move it from there.

| | |
|---|---|
| **China tax leakage** | 0%. |
| **US tax leakage** | § 988(e) currency gain as elsewhere. |
| **Legal risk** | **High for mainland-resident funds.** Moving the CNY to Hong Kong in the first place is itself a cross-border transfer subject to the same rules; doing it outside the banking system is the definition of illegal FX trading under FX Administration Regulations art. 45. |
| **Pros** | Offshore rates can be better; HK banking infrastructure is deep. |
| **Cons** | Does not solve the *export* problem — it relocates it; the CNY still has to lawfully leave the mainland; adding an offshore layer adds cost and compliance surface. |
| **Sources** | [PBOC FX Regs art. 45](https://www.pbc.gov.cn/english/130721/2025080815063231112/index.html) |

## Group B — Prohibited or criminal channels (listed so they can be ruled out)

| Method | Legal status | Penalty exposure | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Underground banking / 对敲 (duiqiao)** | **Criminal.** Disguised cross-border FX trading is the crime of illegal business operation. | Serious case from RMB 5m transactions or RMB 100k gains; fine 1–5× illegal gains; up to 5 years' imprisonment; 5+ years where especially serious. | [SPC Fa Shi [2019] No. 1](https://www.court.gov.cn/zixun/xiangqing/141562.html) |
| **Cryptocurrency conversion** | **Prohibited.** All virtual-currency business activities are illegal financial activities; overseas exchanges serving mainland residents are illegal. | Criminal liability pursued. | [PBOC notice, Sept 2021](http://www.pbc.gov.cn/goutongjiaoliu/113456/113469/4348521/index.html) |
| **"Ant moving" / splitting a large sum into many small conversions using friends' quotas** | Prohibited — SAFE treats this as splitting. | Watchlist, quota restriction, credit-record entry, AML/criminal referral. | [SAFE](https://www.safe.gov.cn/en/2017/0119/1244.html) |
| **Borrowing or lending bank cards to lift the ATM limit** | Prohibited. | Card barred; watchlist. | [SAFE Huifa [2017] No. 29](https://www.safe.gov.cn/en/2017/1230/1397.html) |
| **Friend swaps (CNY in, USD out, matched informally)** | Falls within disguised FX trading. | Same criminal exposure as underground banking. | [PBOC FX Regs art. 45](https://www.pbc.gov.cn/english/130721/2025080815063231112/index.html) |
| **Unlicensed FX business** | Prohibited. | Confiscation + fine 1–5× gains where gains exceed RMB 500,000, else RMB 500,000–2,000,000. | [PBOC FX Regs art. 46](https://www.pbc.gov.cn/english/130721/2025080815063231112/index.html) |
| **False documents to exceed the quota** | FX evasion. | Repatriation + fine up to 30% (30–100% if serious) + criminal liability. | [PBOC FX Regs art. 39](https://www.pbc.gov.cn/english/130721/2025080815063231112/index.html) |

## Method comparison at a glance

| # | Method | China tax | US tax | Legal risk | Scale | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A1 | Direct bank remittance of taxed personal CNY | 0% | § 988(e) gain only | Low | Large | **Personal savings already taxed** |
| A2 | After-tax salary remittance | 0% | FEIE may zero it | Low | Medium | Currently employed in China |
| A3 | WFOE dividend | 32.5% | Largely offset by FTC | Low | Large | Company profit |
| A4 | Service fees | VAT only | US-taxable, VAT not creditable | Low | Large | Ongoing service relationship |
| A5 | Royalties / interest | 10% WHT + VAT | Largely offset by FTC | Low | Medium | Genuine IP or loan |
| A6 | Equipment rental | 7% effective | Largely offset by FTC | Low | Medium | Genuine equipment lease |
| A7 | Capital reduction / liquidation | EIT on gain only | FTC on the EIT | Low | Large | Closing the entity |
| A8 | Direct payment of overseas expenses | **0%** | **0%** | Low | Medium | **Any genuine overseas obligation** |
| A9 | Overseas ATM withdrawals | 0% | § 988(e) | Low | RMB 100k/yr | Small, urgent cash |
| A10 | Carrying cash | 0% | § 988(e) | High (≤20% fine) | Small | Not recommended |
| A11 | Personal property transfer abroad | *blank* | § 988(e) | Low | Large | Emigrated / leaving China |
| A12 | Offshore CNH via HK | 0% | § 988(e) | High | Large | Not recommended alone |
| B | Underground banking / crypto / splitting | n/a | n/a | **Criminal** | Large | **Never** |
