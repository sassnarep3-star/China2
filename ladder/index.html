The answer in one paragraph

There is no PRC tax on converting RMB into USD. What exists is a documentary gate: the bank may convert a non-domestic individual's lawful current-account income only against identity plus proof of the transaction including the tax voucher. If the underlying income was already taxed and withheld, the extra PRC tax on conversion is zero and the true cost is the bank's commission (0.1%, CNY 100–500, plus CNY 120 cable on the verified tariff), plus the FX spread — roughly 0.3%–1.8% all-in. If the income was never taxed, the 3%–45% or flat 20% that people call a “conversion penalty” is simply the unpaid PRC tax on the source, and no route avoids it lawfully. The cheapest legal sequence in that case is to pay it, collect the certificate, convert, and claim the PRC tax against US tax on Form 1116.

Seven questions, answered from the primary text

Is there a PRC tax on converting RMB to USD?No such tax appears in any instrument reviewed here. PRC tax attaches to the income; conversion is a documentary and pricing event. S01 · pbc.gov.cn S02 · safe.gov.cn S07 · chinatax.gov.cn
Then why does the bank refuse or quote a “tax”?Because the bank must see either a tax certificate for lawful current-account income, or an original exchange memo to reverse a conversion. Without one of those, the counter has no lawful basis to convert a large balance. S02 · safe.gov.cn S20 · safe.gov.cn
What does USD 50,000 a year actually cover?The annual aggregate applies to individuals' FX sales and to domestic individuals' purchases. It is not a universal ceiling on moving your own documented money out. S02 · safe.gov.cn
What can I do with a passport only?Convert back at most the equivalent of USD 500 in a day (USD 1,000 at a port-side counter before departure). That is a documentary exemption for re-conversion, not a quota for earned income. S17 · safe.gov.cn S02 · safe.gov.cn
Can I carry cash instead?Up to USD 5,000 out of China without a permit, USD 5,000–10,000 with a bank certificate; above USD 10,000 only in listed special cases. Entering the US with more than USD 10,000 requires a FinCEN Form 105 report. S15 · safe.gov.cn S13 · cbp.gov
What are the real penalties?For unlawful FX conduct: up to 30% of the amount, or 30%–100% if serious; 20% for illegal cash carriage. For US reporting failures the exposure is separate and can exceed the tax. S04 · safe.gov.cn S10 · irs.gov
Can I use crypto or a friend's quota?No. Exchanging RMB for virtual currency is an illegal financial activity, and borrowing a quota puts both parties on a watch list. S16 · pbc.gov.cn S01 · pbc.gov.cn S18 · safe.gov.cn

Where the cost actually goes

Five things are routinely added together and called “the tax”. Only one of them is a tax on conversion, and it is zero.

  1. PRC tax on the source income. 3%–45% comprehensive, 5%–35% business, 20% flat on dividends/interest/rent/property gain, or 10% treaty rate on dividends and royalties. S07 · chinatax.gov.cn S09 · irs.gov
  2. The bank's FX spread. Bank revenue, not a tax. Not published in advance; the only honest number is the executable quote on the day. model assumption A2
  3. Commission and cable fees. Published: 0.1% with a CNY 100 floor and CNY 500 cap, plus CNY 120 cable per transfer, plus 1% to turn cash into remittable FX. S06 · hsbc.com.cn
  4. PRC penalties for doing it the wrong way. Up to 30% of the amount, 30%–100% if serious, 20% for illegal cash carriage. S04 · safe.gov.cn
  5. US tax and reporting. Worldwide income is taxable, but PRC tax paid is creditable (S12 · irs.gov); the FBAR and Form 8938 duties attach to holding the account, not to converting. S10 · irs.gov S11 · irs.gov
