Methods: 13 ways to move CNY to USD, with pros, cons and steps

Every route below was checked against the official text linked in its row. Routes that are prohibited in China are listed too, because the brief asked for as many ways as possible — they are labelled, not recommended. None is recommended for you personally, because your eligibility facts are unknown (G-01).

How to read the status labels. Lawful if documents qualify the rule permits it if your documents and status qualify. Unverified it may exist, but the governing terms were not verified in this session, so it carries no numbers. Prohibited the published rule forbids it.
R01

Bank purchase of FX against documented lawful China income, then telegraphic transfer (SWIFT) abroad

Lawful if documents qualify Bank channel

The bank sells USD against the RMB balance under Art. 13(1) of the Detailed Rules and Art. 59 of the 2020 Current Account Guidelines, then remits by telegraphic transfer. The annual USD 50,000 'facilitation quota' is an ID-only convenience for settlement/purchase by domestic individuals: a foreign individual only gets the ID-only quota if they hold a Foreigner's Permanent Residence ID card, so most foreign individuals proceed on documents instead of quota.

Who it is for: Foreign individual (e.g. U.S. citizen) with lawful current-account RMB income earned in China - salary, labour remuneration, services - and transaction/tax evidence. This is the mainstream route for money already sitting in a mainland bank account.

Pros

  • Explicitly contemplated by SAFE: ID plus transaction-amount evidence including tax vouchers.
  • Not capped by the USD 50,000 facilitation quota when documented - the constraint is documentary, not a hard annual number.
  • Runs through the monitored banking system, so the funds have a clean, provable U.S. paper trail for FBAR/Form 8938 and for the source of the deposit.
  • Fee is small and published: at HSBC Premier, 0.1% capped at CNY 500 plus a CNY 120 cable charge.

Cons

  • Requires the source income to be documented and, where applicable, already taxed in China. If the balance cannot be traced to lawful, taxed income, the bank can refuse (S01 Art. 12).
  • The bank, not the customer, decides whether the documents satisfy it; outcomes vary by branch and by account history.
  • The CNY/USD spread is set commercially and is not published in the tariff, so total cost cannot be verified in advance (S24 note 7).
  • Correspondent and receiving-bank charges are excluded from the tariff and are unverified.
  • If the underlying income was never reported in the U.S., moving it does not cure that; it makes it visible.

Exactly how it would work

  1. 1. Ask the bank in writing for its document list for 'purchase of FX against current-account income' (购汇) and for the remittance, and ask which category it will book the transaction under.
  2. 2. Assemble identity documents, the employment or service contract, and the tax payment certificate / withholding record for the income (S02 Art. 13(1); S03).
  3. 3. Obtain a written, itemised quote: applied CNY/USD rate, the rate source and timestamp, the sender fee, cable charge, any 'Pay Exact' charge, correspondent fees and the receiving bank's fee (S24 items 2.2.4.1-2.2.4.2 and remark 1.1).
  4. 4. Complete the bank's FX purchase and remittance instructions in your own name; the beneficiary must be your own account to keep the trail clean.
  5. 5. Keep the exchange memo, the tax certificate, the SWIFT confirmation and the statements - you will need them for the U.S. side (S05, S08, S10).
  6. 6. Report on the U.S. side: income already translated when earned (S05/S07), FBAR if aggregate foreign accounts exceeded USD 10,000 at any time (S08), Form 8938 if the applicable threshold is crossed (S10).

Ceiling: No published USD ceiling for a documented current-account purchase; documentary sufficiency is the binding constraint (S02, S03). (S02)

Cost, published only: Published tariff only: 0.1% of the remittance, floor CNY 100, cap CNY 500, plus CNY 120 cable per transaction (Premier and Advance; Private Banking individual TX issuance is Free). Optional 'Pay Exact' CNY 200 equivalent. (S01 S02 S03 S04 S05 S08 S10 S24)

R02

HSBC Global Transfer to your own same-name HSBC account abroad, then convert or hold

Lawful if documents qualify Bank channel

An intra-group transfer from the HSBC China account to a self-named HSBC account overseas. The tariff prices this separately from a SWIFT wire.

Who it is for: Customers of HSBC China who also hold an HSBC account in the destination country (e.g. HSBC US).

Pros

  • Published fee is Free for Private Banking and Premier customers.
  • For Advance customers the published charge is a flat USD 20 per transaction (indicative CNY 145), far below a percentage fee on large amounts.
  • One leg only, inside one banking group, which usually means fewer correspondent deductions than a multi-bank SWIFT chain.
  • Keeps the funds in the same-name structure, which is the cleanest for U.S. reporting.

Cons

  • Requires an existing eligible HSBC account abroad; opening one as a U.S. person has its own eligibility, KYC and tax-documentation requirements that are not verified here.
  • This is a transfer price only; the RMB-to-USD conversion itself is a separate step with its own documentary requirement (S02 Art. 13) and its own spread.
  • The tariff excludes other institutions' charges and any taxes or levies (S24 note 7).
  • Advance segment eligibility was restricted from 18 March 2026 to specified customer groups (S24 important note 4).
  • Availability of Global Transfer to Self depends on the destination country supporting the service.

Exactly how it would work

  1. 1. Confirm in writing that your HSBC China segment qualifies (Premier requires a prior-month Total Relationship Balance of at least CNY 500,000 or equivalent) and that Global Transfer to the U.S. is available to you (S24 important notes 3 and item 2.2.6.1).
  2. 2. Complete the FX purchase leg with the same evidence as R01 - Global Transfer moves foreign currency, it does not by itself authorise the conversion (S02, S03).
  3. 3. Link the overseas same-name account and send a small test amount first; reconcile the amount received against the amount sent.
  4. 4. Obtain the written rate and fee confirmation before each transfer; keep every confirmation.
  5. 5. Report on the U.S. side per S08 (FBAR) and S10 (Form 8938).

Ceiling: No published amount ceiling in the tariff; eligibility and the FX conversion evidence are the constraints. (S24)

Cost, published only: Published: Private Banking Free; Premier Free; Advance CNY 145 indicative per transaction, actual charge USD 20 or equivalent. (S02 S03 S05 S08 S10 S24)

R03

Emigrant property transfer (personal assets moved abroad after emigrating)

Lawful if documents qualify Capital-account approval

Application to the SAFE branch where the emigrant's original household registration was, with the documents in Art. 8 of the Provisional Measures, including proof of cancelled Chinese residence, proof of foreign residence, proof of property rights, and a tax certificate or tax-payment voucher. Approval is by the local branch below RMB 500,000 equivalent and by SAFE head office at or above it (Art. 10). The former requirement to file with head office above RMB 500,000 and the forced multi-year instalment schedule were changed by Huifa [2014] No. 2.

Who it is for: A natural person who has emigrated from mainland China to a foreign country (or to Hong Kong/Macao), moving lawful pre-emigration domestic assets that have been realised into cash.

Pros

  • Statutory channel built for exactly this problem: getting a large legitimate balance out.
  • Amounts are not limited by the USD 50,000 individual facilitation quota - the ceiling is the approved amount.
  • The 2014 reform removed the head-office filing above RMB 500,000 and the mandatory instalment schedule, so a lump sum is possible within the approved amount.
  • Produces an official approval document, which is strong evidence of lawful origin.

Cons

  • Requires that Chinese household registration has been cancelled and foreign residence is documented - a U.S. citizen who never had Chinese hukou cannot use it.
  • Requires a tax certificate from the competent tax authority; the amount of tax, if any, depends on the nature of the assets and is not verified here.
  • Art. 6 of the Measures still requires one application for the whole amount, and the PBOC's own summary describes staged remittance for emigrant transfers above RMB 200,000 - the relationship between Art. 6 and the 2014 reform is a real ambiguity (flag F06).
  • Approval is discretionary in practice; timelines are not published.
  • Pre-2014 and post-2014 texts are both still published, so branch practice may vary.

Exactly how it would work

  1. 1. Confirm you are within the definition (emigrant who held the assets before obtaining immigration status); if not, this route is closed.
  2. 2. Ask the local SAFE branch for its current checklist under the Provisional Measures as amended, in writing.
  3. 3. Obtain the tax certificate or tax-payment voucher from the competent tax authority (S17 Art. 8; S18 Art. 8(6)).
  4. 4. Assemble the Art. 8 file: written application with source and realisation explanation, personal information form, FX business application form, ID, cancelled-residence proof, foreign residence proof, property-rights documents; notarise what is not already notarised.
  5. 5. File with the correct SAFE branch; expect head-office review if the amount is at or above RMB 500,000 equivalent.
  6. 6. Take the approval and 核准件 to the designated bank to buy FX and remit directly to your own overseas account - the Measures prohibit paying out foreign cash domestically (Art. 11).
  7. 7. Keep the approval, the bank vouchers and the tax certificate; report on the U.S. side per S08/S10 and translate at the applicable rate (S05, S07).

Ceiling: Approved amount; local SAFE branch below RMB 500,000 equivalent, SAFE head office at or above (S17 Art. 10). (S17)

Cost, published only: No government fee is published in the Measures. The bank's FX/remittance fee depends on the bank and segment; the HSBC tariff figures in R01/R02 are the only published numbers used here. FX spread unverified. (S01 S02 S16 S17 S18 S05 S07)

R04

Inheritance transfer (foreign citizen inheriting a mainland estate)

Lawful if documents qualify Capital-account approval

Application to the SAFE branch where the deceased's household registration was, with the inheritance documents and the deceased's property documents plus the tax certificate. One application for all assets from the same decedent; remittance may be in a lump sum or instalments.

Who it is for: A foreign citizen (or Hong Kong/Macao resident) who has lawfully inherited assets located in mainland China.

Pros

  • A dedicated statutory channel; not subject to the USD 50,000 facilitation quota.
  • One application covers all assets inherited from the same decedent.
  • Produces official approval documentation.

Cons

  • Requires a completed inheritance with documented title; probate in China is slow and often contested.
  • Requires the deceased's tax certificate and property documents.
  • Assets inherited from different decedents require separate applications.
  • Notarial requirements are extensive (Art. 9).

Exactly how it would work

  1. 1. Complete the inheritance and obtain the notarised inheritance documents.
  2. 2. Obtain the tax certificate or tax-payment voucher for the estate from the competent tax authority.
  3. 3. File at the SAFE branch of the deceased's original household registration with the Art. 9 document set.
  4. 4. Obtain approval and take it to the designated bank to purchase FX and remit to your own overseas account.
  5. 5. Keep all approvals; report the account and any income on the U.S. side (S08, S10, S05).

Ceiling: Approved amount; local branch below RMB 500,000 equivalent, head office at or above (S17 Art. 10). (S17)

Cost, published only: No published government fee in the Measures; bank fee per the bank's tariff (see R01); FX spread unverified. (S17 S18 S05 S08 S10)

Sources: S17 S18 S05 S08 S10

R05

Reconversion of unused RMB with the original exchange memo

Lawful if documents qualify Bank channel

Art. 13(2) of the Detailed Rules: reconversion of unused RMB against the original exchange memo, valid for 24 months from the date of the original exchange. The ID-only exception is narrower than often reported: same-day cumulative exchanges of up to USD 500 equivalent, or up to USD 1,000 at premises outside customs before departure.

Who it is for: A foreign individual who previously exchanged foreign currency into RMB in China and still holds the unused RMB.

Pros

  • Fast, counter-level, and needs only the original memo for amounts within the memo.
  • No tax certificate requirement is stated for this specific reconversion.
  • Cheap: it is an FX transaction at the bank, with the usual spread only.

Cons

  • Only works for RMB that came from a prior FX exchange, which is not the usual 'money stuck in China' case.
  • The 24-month window is a hard expiry.
  • The USD 500 / USD 1,000 ID-only exceptions apply only to the narrow same-day / border cases; they are not a general allowance.
  • Requires the physical original exchange memo.

Exactly how it would work

  1. 1. Locate the original exchange memo(s) and check the date - 24 months from the exchange date is the deadline (S02 Art. 13(2)).
  2. 2. Take ID and the memo to the bank and request reconversion of the unused amount.
  3. 3. Request the rate and any fee in writing before signing.
  4. 4. Remit to your own overseas account or carry within the cash limits (see R09) and report on the U.S. side (S08, S10).

Ceiling: The amount on the original memo; 24-month validity (S02 Art. 13(2)). (S02)

Cost, published only: No published bank fee identified for the reconversion itself in the sources read; the spread is commercial and unpublished, so the total is left unverified. (S02 S05 S08 S10)

Sources: S02 S05 S08 S10

R06

Spend the balance abroad on a UnionPay / bank card instead of converting it

Lawful if documents qualify Payment channel

Card transactions settle at the card scheme's rate. Cash withdrawals are separately capped: RMB 100,000 equivalent per person per calendar year, and RMB 10,000 equivalent per card per day (Huifa [2017] No. 29).

Who it is for: Anyone holding a mainland-issued RMB or foreign-currency bank card who will spend money abroad rather than move a lump sum.

Pros

  • No formal FX purchase application and no documentary file for ordinary purchases.
  • SAFE states explicitly that card use does not offset the USD 50,000 annual FX purchase quota (S22).
  • Useful for living costs, tuition and travel already being incurred.

Cons

  • It is spending, not repatriation: it does not move a USD balance to your U.S. account.
  • Cash withdrawals are hard-capped at RMB 100,000 per person per calendar year; exceeding it suspends overseas cash withdrawals for the current and the following year.
  • Merchant and scheme spreads are commercial and unpublished - unverified.
  • Very large card spending can trigger bank monitoring; misuse of the channel is punishable under S01.

Exactly how it would work

  1. 1. Confirm with the card issuer the scheme, the settlement currency and the rate applied.
  2. 2. Track the RMB 100,000 annual cash-withdrawal cap across all your cards, including supplementary cards (S21 point I).
  3. 3. Prefer card payments to cash withdrawals; the daily cash cap is RMB 10,000 equivalent per card (S21 point IV).
  4. 4. Keep receipts; card spending is not a taxable event but the account still needs FBAR/8938 reporting (S08, S10).

Ceiling: Cash withdrawal: RMB 100,000 equivalent per person per calendar year; RMB 10,000 equivalent per card per day (S21). (S21)

Cost, published only: No official published fee or spread was located for card settlement; left blank and flagged. (S21 S22 S01 S08 S10)

R07

Carry foreign cash out of China within the cash-carrying limits

Lawful if documents qualify Cash

Up to USD 5,000 equivalent: no Carrying Certificate, customs releases it. USD 5,000-10,000: a Carrying Certificate from a designated bank. Above USD 10,000: in principle not permitted except listed special cases, with a SAFE-issued certificate. Entering the U.S. with more than USD 10,000 requires a FinCEN Form 105 filing.

Who it is for: Anyone who needs a modest amount of foreign currency outside the banking channel.

Pros

  • No bank remittance fee; HSBC prices over-the-counter foreign-currency note withdrawal as Free (S24 item 2.2.2.1).
  • Simple and immediate for small amounts.

Cons

  • Physically capped: USD 10,000 equivalent per person per exit means 40 trips for USD 400,000. It is not a realistic route at the brief's upper end.
  • Still requires a lawful FX purchase first, with the same documentary requirement (S02 Art. 13).
  • Above USD 10,000 entering the U.S. requires a FinCEN Form 105 report; failure risks seizure and penalties (S13).
  • Theft/loss risk, and no paper trail comparable to a wire.
  • Attempting to evade the cap is an FX violation punishable under S01.

Exactly how it would work

  1. 1. Buy the foreign currency lawfully first (see R01) and keep the exchange memo.
  2. 2. For USD 5,000-10,000, obtain a Carrying Certificate from a designated bank; keep it with the cash (S14 point 2).
  3. 3. Do not exceed USD 10,000 equivalent per person per exit.
  4. 4. File FinCEN Form 105 if you bring more than USD 10,000 into the U.S. (S13).
  5. 5. Declare the account and the cash movements as required on the U.S. side (S08, S10).

Ceiling: USD 5,000 without a certificate; USD 10,000 with a bank certificate; above USD 10,000 only in listed special cases with a SAFE certificate (S14). (S14)

Cost, published only: HSBC prices over-the-counter foreign-currency note withdrawal as Free for all segments (S24 item 2.2.2.1). The FX purchase spread is unpublished. (S14 S15 S13 S24 S02)

Sources: S14 S15 S13 S24 S02

R08

Use the USD 50,000 annual facilitation quota by splitting across family members

Prohibited Rejected workaround

SAFE treats splitting (分拆) to evade quota or authenticity management as a violation. Individuals are placed on a watch list for the year of listing plus two consecutive years, and must then transact at the counter with full evidence under strict review.

Who it is for: People advised to 'just use relatives' quotas'.

Pros

  • None that survives review: it is expressly prohibited (S20).

Cons

  • Expressly prohibited by Huifa [2015] No. 49 (S20).
  • Watch-list consequences for three years, with counter-only, evidence-heavy processing.
  • May also be treated as evasion under S01 with fines up to 30% of the amount.
  • A non-PR foreign individual is not entitled to the ID-only quota anyway (S04).

Exactly how it would work

  1. Do not do this. If you were advised to, stop and use R01/R03 instead.

Ceiling: Prohibited. (S20)

Cost, published only: Not a costed route. Statutory exposure under S01: fine up to 30% of the amount, or 30% to the equivalent amount if serious. (S20 S04 S01)

Sources: S20 S04 S01

R09

Buy crypto or stablecoin in China and sell it abroad

Prohibited Rejected workaround

Yin Fa [2026] No. 42 states virtual currency has no legal-tender status in China and that virtual-currency business activity - including exchanging legal tender for virtual currency and vice versa, acting as central counterparty, and providing information intermediation or pricing - is illegal financial activity, strictly prohibited and to be banned. Financial institutions may not provide account opening, transfer or settlement services for it, and may not accept virtual currency as collateral.

Who it is for: People advised to 'just buy USDT and sell it in the U.S.'.

Pros

  • None that is lawful in China. Any apparent 'low fee' is the price of an illegal channel (S23).

Cons

  • Illegal financial activity under S23; participants bear their own losses.
  • Bank accounts used for it can be frozen or closed; the counterparty risk is total.
  • Creates a U.S. tax and reporting mess on top (property transactions, FBAR/8938 treatment of offshore exchange accounts).
  • Also likely a violation of S01 where it is used to move funds out without approval.

Exactly how it would work

  1. Do not do this. The PRC position as published in February 2026 is prohibition, not regulation (S23).

Ceiling: Prohibited. (S23)

Cost, published only: Not a costed route. Downside is total loss of the funds plus regulatory exposure. (S23 S01)

Sources: S23 S01

R10

Underground banks / 'money changers' / third-party swap (对敲)

Prohibited Rejected workaround

Off-book conversion matches your RMB to someone else's USD. It is illegal private trading of foreign exchange under S01 Art. 45, and it is the classic vehicle for the tax-evasion and money-laundering conduct S21 and S23 target.

Who it is for: People offered an off-book conversion at a 'better rate'.

Pros

  • Quoted spreads look attractive precisely because the transaction is unlawful.

Cons

  • Illegal private FX trading: warning, confiscation of illegal gains, fine up to 30% of the amount, or 30% to the equivalent if serious (S01 Art. 45); criminal liability where it constitutes a crime.
  • Counterparty risk is unsecured and uninsurable; there is no legal recourse if the funds vanish.
  • It leaves no verifiable paper trail, so you cannot later prove the source of the USD - which is exactly what the IRS and FinCEN ask about.
  • Bank accounts involved are frequently frozen during investigations.

Exactly how it would work

  1. Do not do this.

Ceiling: Prohibited. (S01)

Cost, published only: Not a costed route. Statutory exposure under S01 Art. 45: fine up to 30% of the amount, 30% to the equivalent amount if serious. (S01 S21 S23)

Sources: S01 S21 S23

R11

Third-party remittance apps (Wise, Western Union and similar) funded from China

Unverified Provider channel

These providers are licensed in their home jurisdictions, but the operating question is whether the CNY leg can be funded lawfully from a mainland account. Their published CNY support and fees were not verified against a primary provider document in this session.

Who it is for: Customers able to fund an offshore balance or use a supported local payout.

Pros

  • Usually transparent pricing when the corridor is supported.
  • Familiar consumer interface and tracking.

Cons

  • Corridor availability for mainland China is restricted and changes; not verified here.
  • The PRC-side funding leg must still satisfy S01 Art. 12 and S02 - an app does not create an exemption.
  • Using an app to move funds abroad without approval can be treated as evasion under S01.
  • No primary fee document was read this session, so all costs are left blank and flagged (F04).

Exactly how it would work

  1. 1. Obtain the provider's own written confirmation that the CNY-from-mainland corridor is supported for a U.S. beneficiary and that the funding source is compliant.
  2. 2. Ask the sending bank whether funding the provider is treated as a permitted current-account payment.
  3. 3. Get an executable quote and keep it.
  4. 4. Do not proceed on the basis of this page alone - the corridor is unverified.

Ceiling: Unverified.

Cost, published only: No primary provider fee document was read this session. All cost cells are blank and flagged, not estimated. (S01 S02)

R12

QDII / cross-border investment schemes, Hong Kong insurance and similar products

Unverified Investment channel

Regulated cross-border investment channels such as QDII, Stock Connect or the Greater Bay Area Wealth Management Connect allow specified investments. They buy an investment product; they do not convert an existing deposit into a personal USD balance.

Who it is for: Investors eligible for a specific scheme, rather than someone simply trying to move an existing deposit.

Pros

  • Regulated and documented when you are eligible.
  • May be appropriate if the goal is investment rather than repatriation.

Cons

  • Eligibility, quota and product approvals are scheme-specific and were not verified here.
  • The money ends up in an investment, not in your U.S. bank account.
  • Surrender, tax and reporting consequences differ by product and are outside the sources read.
  • Selling an insurance or investment product later is itself a cross-border event with its own rules.

Exactly how it would work

  1. 1. If you are considering this, obtain the scheme's current eligibility and quota rules from the regulator that publishes them.
  2. 2. Get the product's tax and surrender terms in writing.
  3. 3. Treat any 'buy a policy in Hong Kong' advice as unverified until the insurer's own documents and the PRC FX position are confirmed.

Ceiling: Scheme-specific; unverified.

Cost, published only: No cost can be published without the scheme rules and product terms. Blank and flagged. (S01 S02)

Sources: S01 S02

R13

Gift or family support received from a China-resident relative

Unverified Family channel

The relative's own outbound remittance is the regulated step, under the relative's eligibility and documentation. On the U.S. side a foreign gift is generally not income, but the recipient may have a separate reporting obligation above a threshold that was not verified in this session.

Who it is for: A U.S. person receiving support from a relative who remains a Chinese resident.

Pros

  • Common and lawful where the sender's own compliance is clean.
  • No U.S. income inclusion for a genuine gift, in general.

Cons

  • It is the relative who must satisfy SAFE documentation, and their quota and evidence may not stretch to the amount.
  • Repeated structured transfers between related parties can look like splitting (S20).
  • U.S. gift reporting thresholds and forms were not verified this session - blank and flagged (F08).
  • It does not solve the underlying question of whose money it is.

Exactly how it would work

  1. 1. The China-resident relative confirms with their bank which current-account category and evidence apply.
  2. 2. The relative sends from their own account to yours, in their own name, with a stated purpose.
  3. 3. Keep the sender's documents; you may need them to explain the deposit.
  4. 4. Check the U.S. gift-reporting rule with the primary IRS page before relying on any threshold - not verified here.

Ceiling: Sender-side; unverified.

Cost, published only: Sender-side bank fee applies and was not verified for the sender's bank. Blank and flagged. (S02 S20 S01)
