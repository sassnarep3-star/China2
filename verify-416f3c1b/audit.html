A01 Personal currency gain above $200 is described as a capital gain — contradicted by the statute

Pages: briefing/index.html; briefing/sources.html; site/executive-summary.html; site/cost-breakdown.html; site/conversion-routes.html · Verdict: contradicted

What the pages say: “Personal FX gain excluded from income unless > $200; above that, capital gain” (briefing/index.html). “above that it is reported as a capital gain” (briefing/sources.html, site/executive-summary.html). “$0 where the personal gain is $200 or less per transaction; above that, tax on the gain as a capital gain” (site/cost-breakdown.html).

What the official source says: 26 U.S.C. §988(a)(1)(A) requires foreign currency gain or loss on a §988 transaction to be 'computed separately and treated as ordinary income or loss'. Section 988(a)(1)(B) allows a capital-election only for certain forward, futures and option contracts identified before the close of the trading day — not for a simple disposition of currency. A personal currency conversion above the $200 line therefore produces ORDINARY gain, not capital gain. On a true long-term gain the two treatments differ by up to 20 percentage points of federal rate, so this is not cosmetic.

Cross-scope request: Owner of briefing/** and site/**: replace 'capital gain' with 'ordinary income' in the five files listed, or delete the sentence. Cross-scope: not edited by this session.

A02 The USD 50,000 facilitation quota is presented as a general conversion ceiling

Pages: root index.html (hub card); briefing/index.html; briefing/methods.html; briefing/costs.html · Verdict: verified-limited

What the pages say: Card text and method M02 describe a 'Bank wire within the USD 50,000/year facilitation quota' with '8 years for $400k', i.e. the quota is treated as the operative ceiling for an individual conversion.

What the official source says: The USD 50,000 annual facilitation quota is real (Detailed Rules Art. 2) and is genuinely ID-only within the quota. But Art. 2 applies to settlement/purchase by domestic individuals, and SAFE's own 2024 Q&A states that a foreign individual gets the quota only if they hold a Foreigner's Permanent Residence ID card; otherwise the documented current-account route under Art. 59 of Huifa [2020] No. 14 applies and has no published USD ceiling. The pages do state elsewhere that the quota is not the ceiling for documented income, so the claim is true only in the narrow PRC-ID case — which is where it is most likely to mislead a U.S. citizen reading 'my money is stuck'.

Cross-scope request: Owner of briefing/** and the root hub: qualify the M02 row and the hub card with 'only for PRC-ID or permanent-residence-ID holders; documented current-account income is not capped by it'.

A03 Route table still prices the USD 7 Global Transfer and the 0.25%–0.4% cash fee from the superseded 2021 tariff

Pages: ladder/ladder.html (route table); ladder/sources.html (S06 quote) · Verdict: contradicted

What the pages say: Ranked route table carries Global Transfer and cash-withdrawal costs derived from the '1 January 2021' HSBC tariff. ladder/sources.html quotes it: 'same-name HSBC Global Transfer: free for Premier; reference CNY 50, actual USD 7 per transaction' and 'foreign cash withdrawal: 0.25%-0.4%, minimum CNY 40'.

What the official source says: The current HSBC China tariff, effective 31 October 2026, prices same-name Global Transfer to Self at Free for Private Banking and Premier and CNY 145 indicative (actual USD 20) for Advance — item 2.2.6.1. Item 2.2.2.1 prices foreign-currency notes withdrawal over the counter at Free for all three segments. The old 0.25%–0.4% cash charge is not in the current schedule. ladder/sources.html does label S06 as superseded by S21 for these items, but the route table still displays the derived prices.

Cross-scope request: Owner of ladder/: reprice the Global Transfer and cash rows from S21 items 2.2.6.1 and 2.2.2.1, or mark those rows 'superseded — not priced'. Cross-scope: not edited by this session.
