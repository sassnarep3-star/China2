## 1. The finding that changes the question

You asked how to get money out of China with the least tax penalty. The premise worth testing first is that there is
a tax on converting currency. There is not, in either country.

- **China.** The Individual Income Tax Law taxes nine enumerated categories of income — wages and salaries; labour
  services; author's remuneration; royalties; business income; interest, dividends and bonuses; property leasing;
  property transfer; incidental income. Exchanging your own renminbi is not one of them
  ([S19](https://www.chinatax.gov.cn/n810219/n810744/n3752930/n3752974/c3970366/content.html), Arts. 1–3).
- **United States.** Converting currency is a §988 transaction, but for an individual's *personal* transaction no gain
  is recognised by reason of exchange-rate changes unless the gain would exceed **$200**
  ([S06](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2024-title26/html/USCODE-2024-title26-subtitleA-chap1-subchapN-partIII-subpartJ-sec988.htm),
  §988(e)(1)–(3)).

So the "considerable tax" quoted to you is almost certainly one of four different things wearing the same name: an
unpaid **PRC income tax on the money when it was earned**; a **source-of-funds / documentation refusal** by the bank;
a **U.S. reporting exposure** that exists whether or not you transfer; or a **bank fee or spread** described loosely
as tax. Each has a different fix, and only the first is a tax.

## 2. The constraint is documentation, not the USD 50,000 quota

The most common belief is that China caps you at USD 50,000 a year. The quota is real, but it is not your ceiling.

| | What the rule actually says | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Annual facilitation quota | USD 50,000 equivalent per person per year, ID-only at the bank, for **domestic individuals'** settlement/purchase | [S02](https://www.safe.gov.cn/tianjin/2024/0430/2464.html) Art. 2 |
| Foreign individuals | The quota applies to a foreign individual **only if they hold a Foreigner's Permanent Residence ID card** | [S04](https://www.safe.gov.cn/tianjin/2024/0621/2529.html) |
| The route that does apply | ID **plus source-of-funds evidence including tax vouchers**, under Art. 59 of the 2020 Current Account Guidelines | [S03](https://www.safe.gov.cn/tianjin/2026/0713/3088.html) |
| Published USD ceiling on that route | **None found** — it is limited by evidence, not by a number | [S02](https://www.safe.gov.cn/tianjin/2024/0430/2464.html), [S03](https://www.safe.gov.cn/tianjin/2026/0713/3088.html) |

The practical answer: a documented current-account purchase against lawful China income is not capped at USD 50,000.
A balance that cannot be traced to lawful, taxed income cannot be converted at all — the bank is required to review
the real, lawful transaction basis before it sells foreign exchange
([S01](https://www.safe.gov.cn/safe/2008/0806/5321.html) Art. 12).

remittance, minimum CNY 100, maximum CNY 500, plus a CNY 120 cable charge** for Premier and Advance; Private Banking

## 5. Is there a currency gain on your balance?

At the IRS's own yearly averages, renminbi acquired in 2021–2023 and converted at the 2025 average shows an exchange
**loss**, so no gain is recognised. Only 2024 shows a gain, and on the 2024 average it crosses the $200 line at roughly
**$23,763** converted. The arithmetic is on the site's **Tax** page. It uses yearly averages, not your actual
acquisition dates, so it is an illustration, not a computation for you.

## 7. Order of operations

1. Establish, in writing, what the balance is and where it came from, with dates and tax evidence.
2. Check your U.S. position for prior years (FBAR, Form 8938) before the transfer makes the account visible.
3. Ask the bank in writing which current-account category applies and for the full document list. If they say "tax",
   make them identify the tax type, taxpayer, period, base, amount and legal basis in writing.
4. Get an itemised, executable quote — rate, source, timestamp, every fee.
5. Send a small test transfer, reconcile what lands against what left.
6. Send the rest; keep every document.
7. File the U.S. side: FBAR, Form 8938, foreign tax credit or FEIE (not both on the same income), FinCEN 105 for cash.

## 10. One paragraph answer

Do not try to defeat a conversion tax, because neither country imposes one. Prove the money instead: assemble the
employment, service or inheritance documents and the PRC tax vouchers, then ask the bank to buy foreign exchange
against documented current-account income under Art. 13(1) of the Detailed Rules and Art. 59 of the 2020 Current
Account Guidelines — a route with **no published USD ceiling**, whose published wire fee is **$30.86 at $10,000 and
$86.97 at $400,000**. Clean up your U.S. reporting before you move anything, because the penalty exposure
(**$16,536–$165,353** per violation) is three orders of magnitude larger than the fee. Above $200 of personal currency
gain the U.S. result is ordinary income, not capital gain. Avoid crypto, underground banks and quota splitting: all
three are prohibited, and the "saving" is the price of an unlawful transaction.
