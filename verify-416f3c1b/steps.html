Step by step, from “the money is stuck” to a filed U.S. return

This is the order that minimises both cost and risk. It is deliberately front-loaded on documentation and on your U.S. filing position, because those — not the wire fee — determine the outcome.

Nothing here is personalised advice. The sequence assumes facts you have to confirm: that the balance is lawful, documented China income or another eligible category, and that you can produce tax evidence. If that assumption fails, stop at Phase 1 and get professional help (G-01, G-03).

Phase 0 — before you talk to anyone

  1. Write down, for yourself, where the balance came from: salary, services, a sale, a gift, savings you brought in. Attach dates and amounts. Everything downstream depends on this and nothing on this site can substitute for it.
  2. Find the tax evidence you already have: withholding records, annual IIT filings, the employer's certificate of income. If none exist, that is the first problem to solve, not the exchange rate.
  3. Check your U.S. position for past years: FBAR for each year the aggregate exceeded $10,000, and Form 8938 where the threshold applies. Fixing this before moving a large sum is far cheaper than after.

Phase 1 — get the bank's written position (free, and it settles the argument)

  1. Ask, in writing: “Which current-account category will you book a purchase of foreign exchange against my documented China income under, and what is your full document list?” Cite Art. 13(1) of the Detailed Rules and Art. 59 of the 2020 Current Account Guidelines.
  2. If they say “tax”, ask them to identify in writing the tax type, the taxpayer, the period, the tax base, the amount and the legal basis — and to distinguish a tax payment from a tax certificate from a bank fee.
  3. Ask for an itemised quote showing the applied CNY/USD rate and its source, the sender fee, cable, correspondent fees, the receiving bank's fee and any 'Pay Exact' charge.
  4. Ask whether they will remit to your own same-name overseas account, and whether the amount is limited in any way.

Phase 2 — choose the route that matches your facts

  1. Lawful China employment or service income, documented: R01 (bank purchase + SWIFT wire) or, if you hold an HSBC account abroad, R02 (same-name Global Transfer).
  2. You emigrated from China and cancelled your household registration: R03 (emigrant property transfer, SAFE approval).
  3. You inherited a mainland estate: R04.
  4. You previously changed foreign currency into renminbi and still hold the memo: R05.
  5. Small amounts you will actually spend: R06 (card) or R07 (cash within USD 10,000).
  6. Never: R08 quota splitting, R09 crypto, R10 underground banks.

Phase 3 — execute, in this order

  1. Get the executable quote in writing and check the effective rate against a reference rate yourself. The difference between the two is the spread you are really paying — the number nobody publishes.
  2. Send a small test transfer first (for example USD 1,000-5,000). Reconcile what lands against what left. This reveals correspondent deductions that no tariff shows.
  3. Send the remainder in the size that matches your documentation. There is no published advantage to splitting a documented transfer; splitting to dodge a limit is what SAFE calls evasion (R08).
  4. Keep the exchange memo, tax certificate, SWIFT confirmation, bank statements and the quote, together, in one folder.

Phase 4 — the U.S. side, which is where the money is actually at risk

  1. Translate the amounts into dollars at the rate you consistently use {src_link('S05')}.
  2. File the FBAR if the aggregate of your foreign accounts exceeded $10,000 at any time in the year {src_link('S08')}.
  3. File Form 8938 if you cross the applicable threshold {src_link('S10')}.
  4. Claim the foreign tax credit on the same income if PRC tax was paid, or the foreign earned income exclusion if you qualify — not both on the same income {src_link('S11')} {src_link('S12')}.
  5. File FinCEN Form 105 if you physically bring in more than $10,000 {src_link('S13')}.
  6. If you have unreported prior years, consider the IRS streamlined compliance procedures before the transfer makes the account visible.
