What each document was used for

S01 · Regulations of the PRC on Foreign Exchange Administration (State Council Order No. 532)

Art. 5: the State does not restrict current international payments and transfers. Art. 12: current-account FX receipts/payments must have a real, lawful transaction basis and banks must reasonably review supporting documents. Arts. 39-40, 45: evasion/illegal arbitrage of FX is punishable by a fine of up to 30% of the amount, or 30% to the equivalent amount if serious. Art. 48(3): failure to submit valid documents or submitting false documents - up to CNY 50,000 for an individual.

https://www.safe.gov.cn/safe/2008/0806/5321.html

S02 · Detailed Rules for the Administration of Individual Foreign Exchange (Huifa [2007] No. 1, amended republication)

Art. 2: annual facilitation quota of USD 50,000 equivalent per person per year for domestic individuals' FX settlement/purchase; within the quota, ID only at the bank; above the quota, current-account items proceed under Arts. 10-12. Art. 13(1): a foreign individual's lawful current-account RMB income in China is purchased with ID plus transaction-amount evidence including tax vouchers. Art. 13(2): reconversion of unused RMB requires the original exchange memo, valid 24 months from the exchange date; the ID-only exception applies only to same-day exchanges up to USD 500 and up to USD 1,000 at border/outside-customs premises before departure. Art. 20: outbound transfer of personal property by individuals emigrating, and by foreign citizens inheriting Chinese estate, follows the Provisional Measures. Arts. 30-31: cash withdrawal above USD 10,000 equivalent same-day requires prior SAFE filing; cash deposit above USD 5,000 equivalent same-day requires a customs declaration or the original bank withdrawal slip. Repeal note: former Art. 39 (CNY 30,000/CNY 1,000 fines) was repealed by Huifa [2023] No. 8.

https://www.safe.gov.cn/tianjin/2024/0430/2464.html

S03 · 2026 FX administration internet information - individual FX (08): how does a foreign individual purchase and remit labour remuneration earned while working in China?

Answer: under Art. 59 of the Current Account FX Business Guidelines (2020 edition, Huifa [2020] No. 14), a foreign individual's lawful current-account RMB income obtained in China is purchased at the bank with the person's valid ID and source-of-funds evidence for the transaction amount, including tax vouchers.

https://www.safe.gov.cn/tianjin/2026/0713/3088.html

S04 · 2024 current-account FX policy Q&A case (9): does a foreign individual enjoy the annual facilitation quota for FX purchase?

Answer: an individual in China who holds a Foreigner's Permanent Residence ID card may use the annual facilitation quota for FX purchase; otherwise Art. 59 of Huifa [2020] No. 14 applies (ID plus source-of-funds/tax evidence). This means a non-PR foreign individual is not automatically entitled to the USD 50,000 ID-only quota for purchasing FX.

https://www.safe.gov.cn/tianjin/2024/0621/2529.html

S05 · Yearly average currency exchange rates

China Yuan yearly averages: 2025 = 7.129; 2024 = 7.189; 2023 = 7.075; 2022 = 6.730; 2021 = 6.452. The page also states the IRS has no official exchange rate and generally accepts any posted rate used consistently, and that income/expenses must be expressed in USD at the spot rate when received, paid or accrued.

https://www.irs.gov/individuals/international-taxpayers/yearly-average-currency-exchange-rates

S06 · 26 U.S.C. sec. 988 - Treatment of certain foreign currency transactions

Sec. 988(c)(1)(C): disposition of non-functional currency is a section 988 transaction and the gain or loss is foreign currency gain or loss. Sec. 988(e)(1): the section does not apply to a section 988 transaction entered into by an individual that is a personal transaction. Sec. 988(e)(2): if non-functional currency is disposed of by an individual in a personal transaction, no gain is recognised by reason of exchange-rate changes - but this does not apply if the gain that would otherwise be recognised exceeds USD 200. Sec. 988(e)(3): 'personal transaction' excludes any transaction to the extent allocable expenses meet section 162 or 212 (other than the part of 212 dealing with taxes).

https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2024-title26/html/USCODE-2024-title26-subtitleA-chap1-subchapN-partIII-subpartJ-sec988.htm

S07 · Foreign currency and currency exchange rates

U.S. taxpayers must report foreign-currency income in USD; foreign currency gain or loss may need to be recognised under section 988; the IRS has no official exchange rate.

https://www.irs.gov/individuals/international-taxpayers/foreign-currency-and-currency-exchange-rates

S08 · Report of Foreign Bank and Financial Accounts (FBAR)

A U.S. person with a financial interest in, or signature authority over, a foreign financial account must file FinCEN Form 114 if the aggregate value of foreign accounts exceeded USD 10,000 at any time in the calendar year. Whether the account produced taxable income has no effect on the filing requirement. Due 15 April with an automatic extension to 15 October. Civil penalty maximums are adjusted annually for inflation.

https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/report-of-foreign-bank-and-financial-accounts-fbar

S09 · 31 CFR 1010.821 - Penalty adjustment and table

Non-willful FBAR violation of a transaction (31 U.S.C. 5321(a)(5)(B)(i)): statutory USD 10,000, adjusted maximum USD 16,536. Willful FBAR violation (31 U.S.C. 5321(a)(5)(C)(i)(I)): statutory USD 100,000, adjusted maximum USD 165,353. General civil penalty for willful BSA violations (31 U.S.C. 5321(a)(1)): range USD 71,545 to USD 286,184. The table sets maximums and does not limit totals for continuing violations.

https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-31/subtitle-B/chapter-X/part-1010/subpart-H/section-1010.821

S10 · Do I need to file Form 8938, Statement of Specified Foreign Financial Assets?

Thresholds: unmarried taxpayer living in the U.S. - more than USD 50,000 on the last day of the year or more than USD 75,000 at any time; married filing jointly living in the U.S. - more than USD 100,000 / USD 150,000; taxpayer living abroad filing other than jointly - more than USD 200,000 / USD 300,000; married filing jointly living abroad - more than USD 400,000 / USD 600,000. No Form 8938 is required if no income tax return is required.

https://www.irs.gov/businesses/corporations/do-i-need-to-file-form-8938-statement-of-specified-foreign-financial-assets

S11 · Foreign Tax Credit

If you paid or accrued foreign taxes and are subject to U.S. tax on the same income you may claim a credit (Form 1116) or a deduction. Generally only income, war profits and excess profits taxes qualify. If you elect to exclude foreign earned income you cannot credit taxes on the excluded income. The amount of foreign tax that qualifies is not necessarily the amount withheld; under a treaty only the reduced treaty rate qualifies.

https://www.irs.gov/individuals/international-taxpayers/foreign-tax-credit

S12 · Foreign earned income exclusion

Requires foreign earned income, a tax home in a foreign country and either bona fide residence for an uninterrupted period including an entire tax year or physical presence in a foreign country for at least 330 full days in any 12 consecutive months. Exclusion amounts stated on the page: USD 107,600 (2020), 108,700 (2021), 112,000 (2022), 120,000 (2023). The page does not state an amount for 2026, so no 2026 figure is used in this dashboard.

https://www.irs.gov/individuals/international-taxpayers/foreign-earned-income-exclusion

S13 · Money and Other Monetary Instruments (FinCEN Form 105)

Anyone who brings, sends or receives currency or monetary instruments totalling more than USD 10,000 at one time into or out of the United States must report it on FinCEN Form 105. The threshold applies to the total carried or sent collectively by a family or group, not per person. Failure to file risks forfeiture and civil or criminal penalties.

https://www.cbp.gov/travel/international-visitors/money-monetary-instruments

S14 · Notice on Issuing the Provisional Measures for Administration of Carrying Foreign Currency Cash In and Out of China (Huifa [2003] No. 102)

Carrying foreign cash not exceeding USD 5,000 equivalent out of China requires no Carrying Certificate and customs releases it; USD 5,000 to USD 10,000 requires a Carrying Certificate from a designated bank, checked by customs; in principle no one may carry more than USD 10,000 equivalent out, and only listed special cases may apply to SAFE for a certificate. Reduplicated (full text identical) at S15.

https://www.safe.gov.cn/safe/2003/0828/5444.html

S15 · Same notice as S14, SAFE Tianjin republication (Huifa [2003] No. 102)

Identical text to S14 on the USD 5,000 / USD 10,000 cash-carrying thresholds and the Carrying Certificate.

https://www.safe.gov.cn/tianjin/2024/0430/2466.html

S16 · Circular on Further Improving and Adjusting Foreign Exchange Administration Policies under the Capital Account (Huifa [2014] No. 2)

Section VI: administration of FX sales and payments for personal property transfers is simplified. The requirement to report to SAFE head office any property transfer exceeding RMB 500,000 equivalent is cancelled; approval is by the local SAFE branch where the emigrant's original permanent residence was registered (or the decedent's, for inheritance). The requirement that an immigrant's property be transferred in several instalments is cancelled, and the bank may remit in a lump sum or in batches within the limit in the approval document. The English page carries SAFE's own note that the Chinese text prevails.

https://www.safe.gov.cn/en/2014/0801/741.html

S17 · PBOC releases and publishes the Provisional Measures for Administration of Sale and Payment of Foreign Exchange for Outbound Transfer of Personal Property

Two categories: emigrant asset transfer and inheritor asset transfer; other forms of personal asset transfer are outside the Measures. Art. 6: one application for the whole amount, remitted in instalments (first remittance not more than half; then up to half of the remainder one year after the first remittance; the rest two years after the first remittance); amounts of RMB 200,000 equivalent or less may be remitted in a lump sum after approval. Art. 8: documents include ID, proof of cancelled Chinese residence and foreign residence, proof of the applicant's property rights, and a tax certificate or tax-payment voucher issued by the competent tax authority at the place of the property or the income source. Art. 10: applications of less than RMB 500,000 equivalent are approved by the local SAFE branch; at or above that, by SAFE head office after local review. The PBOC page is an unofficial translation and its own 2004 news item (S18) says the Measures took effect on 1 December 2004, whereas the translation's last line says 1 February 2004 - see flag F07.

https://www.pbc.gov.cn/english/130721/2025080815062856255/index.html

S18 · PBOC news release: publication and implementation of the Provisional Measures for personal asset outbound transfer

Chinese text of the Measures: Art. 8(6) requires the tax certificate or tax-payment voucher issued by the competent tax authority at the place of the property or of the income source; Art. 10 sets the RMB 500,000 approval split. The release is dated 2004-11-16 and states the Measures took effect on 1 December 2004.

https://www.safe.gov.cn/safe/2004/1116/4133.html

S19 · China Individual Income Tax Law (2018 revision)

Art. 1: resident individuals (domiciled in China, or resident 183 days or more in a tax year) pay IIT on income from within and outside China; non-resident individuals pay IIT on income from within China. Art. 2 lists the nine taxable categories of income (wages and salaries; remuneration for labour services; author's remuneration; royalties; business income; interest, dividends and bonuses; property leasing; property transfer; incidental income). Exchanging one's own currency is not one of the nine categories. Art. 3: comprehensive income 3%-45%, business income 5%-35%, interest/dividends/property leasing/property transfer/incidental income a flat 20%. Art. 16: where income is in a currency other than RMB it is converted at the RMB central parity rate.

https://www.chinatax.gov.cn/n810219/n810744/n3752930/n3752974/c3970366/content.html

S20 · Circular on Further Improving Individual Foreign Exchange Administration (Huifa [2015] No. 49)

Individuals must not use splitting or other means to evade quota or authenticity management. SAFE keeps a 'watch list' (关注名单); an individual placed on it remains there for the year of listing and the following two consecutive years, and during that period any individual FX settlement/purchase must be done at a bank counter with valid ID and transaction-amount evidence, subject to strict authenticity review.

https://www.safe.gov.cn/safe/2015/1231/5442.html

S21 · Circular on Regulating Large-sum Overseas Cash Withdrawals with Bank Cards (Huifa [2017] No. 29)

Overseas cash withdrawals on a person's domestic bank cards (including supplementary cards) may not exceed RMB 100,000 equivalent per calendar year; exceeding it suspends overseas card cash withdrawals for the current and the following year. From 1 January 2018 the per-card daily cap for foreign-currency cards is RMB 10,000 equivalent (previously USD 1,000), and the RMB card cap stays at RMB 10,000 equivalent per card per day.

https://www.safe.gov.cn/safe/2017/1230/8116.html

S22 · Bank card overseas large-sum cash withdrawal Q&A with a SAFE official

SAFE states the card-cash rules do not contradict the USD 50,000 annual quota for individual FX purchase and do not offset that quota; individuals who genuinely need large sums of cash overseas should use the individual FX rules, for example buying FX lawfully and then carrying foreign banknotes out. Withdrawals above the annual quota suspend overseas card cash withdrawals in the current and following year and may be punished under the FX Regulations.

https://www.safe.gov.cn/en/2017/1230/1391.html

S23 · Notice on Further Preventing and Handling Risks Relating to Virtual Currency and Related Activities (Yin Fa [2026] No. 42)

Virtual currency has no legal-tender status in China. Business activities relating to virtual currency - including exchanging legal tender for virtual currency and vice versa, acting as central counterparty, and providing information intermediation or pricing services - are illegal financial activities and are strictly prohibited and to be banned. Financial institutions (including non-bank payment institutions) may not provide account opening, fund transfer or clearing/settlement services for virtual-currency business, may not issue or sell virtual-currency-related financial products, and may not accept virtual currency as collateral. Any entity or individual investing in virtual currency bears its own losses. Effective on publication (2026-02-06) and repealing Yin Fa [2021] No. 237.

https://www.pbc.gov.cn/tiaofasi/144941/3581332/2026020619591971323/index.html
