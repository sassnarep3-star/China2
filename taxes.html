Tax and reporting

US and PRC rules that actually apply

Two jurisdictions, one balance. The PRC taxes the income; the US taxes worldwide income but credits the PRC tax. Reporting duties are separate from tax and are where the penalties live.

How the credit works in practice

US tax is computed on the same income, then reduced by the PRC tax paid on it, limited to the US tax attributable to that income. When the PRC rate is higher, the excess becomes a carryforward rather than a refund. Two traps: the credit is unavailable against income you excluded under the foreign earned income exclusion, and where a treaty reduces the PRC rate, only the reduced amount is creditable (S13).

Worked example at $400,000

Case C (20% flat): PRC tax $80,000. US federal tax on the same amount, single filer 2026, standard deduction: $103,134. Credit allowed: $80,000. US tax remaining: $23,134.

Case B (progressive labour): PRC tax $148,896. Credit allowed: $103,134. US tax remaining: $0. The PRC rate is higher across this range, so the credit absorbs the US liability.

Model arithmetic on statutory inputs; not a return, not advice, and it excludes state tax and any other income (F11, F12).

What is not modelled

  • State income tax and local taxes.
  • Treaty relief on dividends, interest and royalties — described but not re-verified here (F04).
  • Corporate-level tax before a dividend, or VAT on services.
  • Any change in law after 8 November 2026.
