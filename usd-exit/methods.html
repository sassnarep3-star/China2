M01 Annual facilitation quota — US$50,000 per person per year available

(个人年度便利化额度（等值5万美元）)

The baseline lawful channel. Buy up to US$50,000-equivalent per calendar year at any bank with just your ID, then wire it to your own US account.

EligibilityA 境内个人 (domestic individual) as defined in Decree 532 Art. 52(2) — a Chinese citizen, or a foreign national who has resided in China for one continuous year. A US citizen holding a Chinese residence permit for a continuous year falls in this class. A US citizen who is instead a 境外个人 (non-domestic individual) — e.g. holding a Foreign Permanent Resident ID — follows M03.
Ceiling$50,000 / year · $50,000 / transfer
The US$50,000 is an annual FACILITATION quota, not a hard legal ceiling on all conversion: genuine current-account needs above it are allowed with authenticity documents (see M02). It may not be split across people to exceed it. P02 P03
Documents
  • Valid ID (passport for a foreign national; Chinese ID for a citizen)
  • 个人购汇申请书 (individual FX purchase application)
  • Bank card / account credentials
  • Receiving US account details (ABA + account number, account in your own name)

Step by step

  1. Confirm your classification: are you a 境内个人 (Chinese citizen, or foreign national resident 1 continuous year) or a 境外个人? The quota structure differs. Ask the bank to state the classification in writing.
  2. Open/enable online banking on the mainland CNY account and confirm the account is a personal settlement account (I类户) — a II/III-class account has payment caps that block large wires.
  3. File the 个人购汇申请书 (Individual FX Purchase Application) — in-app or at the counter. Tick ONE purpose. Declared purposes must be genuine: an unopened capital item (overseas property, securities, life insurance) may not be declared (P15).
  4. Confirm the bank will apply the transaction to your 年度便利化额度 and that the amount is ≤ US$50,000 equivalent for the calendar year. Track the used quota in the bank app — it is cumulative per person per year, per SAFE's system, not per bank.
  5. Convert CNY→USD at the bank's prevailing rate (its own spread applies; unpublished — F-SPREAD).
  6. Wire USD to your own US bank account from the FX/settlement account. For a remittance from the FX account ≤ US$50,000/day, ID alone suffices; above that, authenticity documents are required (P02 Art. 14).
  7. Keep the bank's FX purchase receipt (购汇凭证) and the wire confirmation. Retain the purpose evidence that matches what you declared.
  8. US side: the transfer itself is not US taxable income. Report the account on FinCEN 114 if the aggregate of all foreign accounts exceeded US$10,000 at any point in the year (U01), and on Form 8938 if the applicable threshold is met (U02).

Chinese tax

Buying foreign currency with your own already-taxed CNY is not itself a taxable event.

The PRC Individual Income Tax Law taxes nine enumerated income categories (wages; labour remuneration; author's remuneration; royalties; business income; interest/dividends/bonus; property leasing; property transfer; incidental). A pure currency conversion is not among them (P11 Art. 2). Tax attaches to the income that produced the money, already withheld at source. If the balance includes untaxed income, or income from a property transfer (20% flat on the net gain, P11 Art. 3/6), that is where the real Chinese tax sits — not in the conversion. P11

US tax

A transfer of your own money is not income, but §988 can create ordinary gain/loss on the currency itself.

A US citizen is taxed on worldwide income (U08). Moving your own funds is not income. However, under 26 USC §988 a disposition of nonfunctional currency is a §988 transaction and exchange gain/loss is ORDINARY (not capital) — with a personal-transaction exception: no gain is recognised on a personal transaction unless the gain exceeds US$200 (U05 §988(e)(2)). Retain rate records for each conversion. U05 U08

Pros

  • Cheapest lawful route: the bank's own tariff, capped (CNY 500 + CNY 120).
  • No lawyer, no approval, no SAFE filing.
  • Repeats every calendar year — a US$400,000 balance takes 8 calendar years at one person's quota (derived).

Cons

  • US$50,000 per person per year is the binding constraint: US$400,000 needs 8 years (derived).
  • Only genuine current-account purposes may be declared; capital items are excluded (P15).
  • The retail spread is invisible in advance and unpublished (F-SPREAD).
  • Requires an in-person or app-based interaction with a mainland bank — this session cannot perform it for you.

M02 Above-quota current-account conversion with authenticity documents conditional

(超过年度便利化额度的经常项目购汇（凭真实性凭证）)

Genuine current-account needs above US$50,000/yr are NOT capped — but each conversion must be backed by documents proving the transaction, including the Chinese tax voucher.

EligibilityA 境内个人 with a genuine current-account need (study abroad, medical treatment, tourism-related settlement, family support) exceeding the annual facilitation quota, or electing not to use it.
Ceiling—
No published cap for genuine current-account items — the cap is replaced by document review. The amount is bounded by the proven transaction, not by a number in the regulation. Deliberately null: no official figure exists to copy. P02 P03
Documents
  • Valid ID
  • Proof of the current-account purpose (admission letter / tuition invoice / medical estimate / contract)
  • Chinese tax voucher (税务凭证)
  • Source-of-funds evidence for the CNY
  • 个人购汇申请书

Step by step

  1. Identify the current-account purpose and collect the primary evidence BEFORE going to the bank: admission letter + tuition invoice (study), hospital estimate/invoice (medical), or the underlying contract/invoice.
  2. Obtain the Chinese tax voucher (税务凭证) for the income that funded the conversion — SAFE's own Q&A requires source-of-funds materials 'including tax voucher' (P08, P13).
  3. Present ID + the authenticity documents at the counter (this cannot be done in an app; above-quota conversions are counter transactions).
  4. The bank reviews and, if satisfied, processes the conversion outside the facilitation quota. It may escalate internally; expect a slower turnaround than M01.
  5. Wire the USD to the payee (school, hospital) or to your own account where the purpose allows. Retain every document with the wire confirmation.
  6. US side: report the foreign account (FinCEN 114 / Form 8938 as applicable). If the money is a gift you receive, see M12 and the Form 3520 US$100,000 threshold (U08).

M03 Non-domestic-individual route (境外个人) — settlement quota, no purchase quota unless you hold a Foreign PR ID conditional

(境外个人结汇/购汇)

If you are classified 境外个人, you get a US$50,000/yr SETTLEMENT (CNY-in) quota but generally NOT a purchase (USD-out) quota — unless you hold a Foreign Permanent Resident ID.

EligibilityA 境外个人: a foreign national who has NOT resided in China for one continuous year, plus (per SAFE's own guidance) holders of a Foreign Permanent Resident ID, Hong Kong/Macau/Taiwan residence permits, or Chinese passport holders with foreign permanent residence.
Ceiling$50,000 / year · $50,000 / transfer
US$50,000/yr settlement facilitation quota. The purchase (FX-buying) facilitation quota is NOT available to 境外个人 generally, EXCEPT holders of a Foreign Permanent Resident ID. Genuine purchase needs are handled case-by-case with documents. P03 P08
Documents
  • Passport
  • Entry/exit records or residence permit proving length of residence
  • Foreign Permanent Resident ID if held
  • Tax voucher
  • Source-of-funds documents

Step by step

  1. Ask the bank in writing which classification it has recorded for you — 境内个人 or 境外个人. The answer changes which quota you can use and is the single most consequential administrative fact in this whole exercise.
  2. If 境外个人 WITHOUT a Foreign PR ID: you have no purchase facilitation quota. Prepare the full documentary package for a case-by-case purchase (M02 standard) and expect the bank to require source-of-funds plus tax voucher.
  3. If you hold a Foreign Permanent Resident ID: you are treated as having the annual purchase quota — proceed as M01, citing P08.
  4. For lawful RMB income earned in China (salary), purchase and remit with ID + transaction-bearing source materials including the tax voucher (P13).
  5. Wire to your own US account; retain receipts.
  6. US side reporting unchanged (U01, U02).

Chinese tax

Employment income earned in China is already subject to IIT withholding; conversion adds no tax.

Salaries are taxed at progressive 3–45% as comprehensive income; the foreign-currency amount is converted at the PBOC central parity for tax purposes (P11 Arts. 3, 16). Remitting after-tax salary is not a second taxable event. P11 P13

US tax

Chinese salary is US-taxable; the FTC is the relief valve.

A US citizen reports worldwide income (U08). Chinese IIT paid on that salary may be credited on Form 1116 (U06) — or, if you qualify, the salary may be excluded under the FEIE, in which case no credit is allowed for tax on the excluded portion (U06). The treaty does not limit US taxation of US citizens (U10 saving clause). U06 U08 U10

Pros

  • Correct classification can unlock the annual quota via a Foreign PR ID (P08).
  • Salary remittance is an explicitly supported, documented route (P13).

Cons

  • Misclassification silently removes your purchase quota — a common and costly administrative trap.
  • Bank discretion; a counter visit is unavoidable.

M04 Emigration / household-registration cancellation transfer — the only bulk lawful channel conditional

(移民转移（注销户籍）)

If you are leaving China for good and cancelling your 户籍, PBOC [2004] No.16 allows a one-off transfer of your entire personal assets, staged over up to ~2 years — with tax clearance required first.

EligibilityA person who has emigrated from mainland China (or settled in Hong Kong/Macau) and who transfers lawful property they owned BEFORE acquiring emigrant status. Art. 8(4) requires a PUBLIC SECURITY household-registration cancellation certificate (户口注销证明) AND a certificate of overseas settlement issued or authenticated by a Chinese embassy/consulate. IIT Law Art. 13 separately requires a PRC tax settlement before the household registration is cancelled (P11). Art. 12 bars property that cannot prove a lawful source.
Ceiling—
The regulation stages by TIME, not by an annual dollar ceiling: first remittance <= half of the total, then <= half of the remainder after one year, then the balance after two years. Totals of RMB 200,000 or less may be remitted in one go after approval. Local SAFE decides below RMB 500,000; at or above it, SAFE head office approves. NO annual USD figure is published - the per-year cell is blank by design, not omission. P06
Documents
  • 申请报告 (written application stating the reason, the source of the property and how it was realised)
  • 《移民财产对外转移申请人情况表》 signed by the applicant
  • 《个人财产对外转移外汇业务申请表》
  • Passport + 中国户籍注销证明 (public security household-registration cancellation certificate) + 国外定居证明 issued/authenticated by a Chinese embassy or consulate
  • Property title documents (e.g. 房屋产权证, 房地产买卖契约, 拆迁补偿安置协议书)
  • 税务证明/完税凭证 issued by the competent tax authority (Art. 8(6))
  • Notarised power of attorney if an agent acts

Step by step

  1. Complete the PRC tax settlement (税款清算) at the tax authority BEFORE cancelling household registration — this is a statutory precondition under IIT Law Art. 13, not a bank formality (P11).
  2. Obtain the tax clearance certificate (完税证明/税收证明) — PBOC [2004] No.16 requires a tax certificate as part of the application package (P06).
  3. Cancel 户籍 and obtain the cancellation certificate (户口注销证明) plus proof of the right to settle abroad (foreign visa / permanent residence).
  4. Apply through the bank to SAFE for the emigration transfer, submitting identity, the household-registration cancellation, the tax certificate, and proof of the assets and their source.
  5. For totals of RMB 500,000 or more, your local SAFE branch reviews first and then submits to SAFE head office for approval; below that, the local branch decides (P06 Art. 10).
  6. Transfer in the statutory pattern (PBOC [2004] No.16 Art. 6): the FIRST remittance may not exceed half of the total approved property; after one full year from the first remittance, up to half of what remains; after two full years, the whole balance. Where the total approved property is RMB 200,000 or less it may be remitted in one go after approval (P06).
  7. Wire each tranche to your own overseas account. Keep every approval document.
  8. US side: the arrival of your own capital is not income and is not a gift — Form 3520 gift reporting does NOT apply to your own funds. The account still needs FinCEN 114 / Form 8938 while it is foreign (U01, U02).

Chinese tax

This route triggers the most Chinese tax scrutiny of any option — by design.

IIT Law Art. 13 requires settlement before household-registration cancellation, and Art. 10 makes 'emigrating and cancelling Chinese household registration' a mandatory filing trigger. Expect the tax authority to look at the whole balance: salary (3–45%), business income (5–35%), and passive items at a flat 20% (P11 Art. 3). This is the one route where an undeclared balance is most likely to surface. P11 P06

US tax

Moving your own capital is not income; the pre-departure tax settlement may be creditable.

The transfer is not US income. Foreign taxes paid in the settlement on income also taxed by the US may support a Form 1116 credit (U06), subject to the treaty-reduced-rate rule (U07). The treaty's saving clause preserves full US taxing rights over citizens (U10). U05 U06 U07 U10 U08

Pros

  • The only published route that can move an entire balance in about two years, and it is a written regulation, not a discretionary favour (P06).
  • The ONLY published route that moves a large balance (hundreds of thousands) in ~1–2 years instead of 8.
  • Bank fees are the same capped tariff as any wire (C01).

Cons

  • Art. 12 grounds for refusal: property that cannot prove a lawful source, or that is caught by unresolved criminal/civil proceedings, is refused outright (P06).
  • Requires actually giving up Chinese household registration — irreversible and consequential.
  • Tax clearance first: the balance must survive scrutiny (P11 Art. 13).
  • SAFE head-office approval at ≥ RMB 500,000; long and discretionary (P06).
  • Staged, so not instant.

M05 Employment-income remittance (documented salary route) available

(境内个人工资收入购汇汇出)

If the balance is salary, this is the cleanest documented path: ID + payroll/source materials + tax voucher, converted and wired.

EligibilityA person with lawful RMB employment income in China, whether classified 境内个人 or 境外个人 (P13 addresses the 境外个人 case explicitly; P08 the general source-of-funds requirement).
Ceiling$50,000 / year · $50,000 / transfer
Ordinarily processed inside the US$50,000 facilitation quota with ID alone; above that, documentary review (P02 Art. 14, P03). The figure is the quota, not a cap on genuine proven salary. P02 P03 P13
Documents
  • Passport/ID
  • Employment contract
  • Payslips
  • Bank statement showing salary credits
  • 个人所得税纳税记录 (IIT payment record / tax voucher)
  • 个人购汇申请书

Step by step

  1. Assemble the payroll paper trail: employment contract, monthly payslips, and the bank statement showing salary credits into the CNY account.
  2. Obtain the annual IIT withholding record (个人所得税纳税记录) from the tax authority — the tax voucher SAFE requires (P08, P13).
  3. Take ID + the package to the bank. Under US$50,000/yr, the conversion can usually be done in-app; above it, expect counter review.
  4. Convert and wire to your own US account. The bank records the transaction so your cumulative quota is tracked in SAFE's system.
  5. Repeat each calendar year. Retain everything for the US return.
  6. US side: report the salary on your 1040; claim the FTC on Form 1116 for Chinese IIT on the same income (U06); file FinCEN 114 / Form 8938 as applicable (U01, U02).

Chinese tax

Salary is taxed at source at 3–45%; the later conversion is not taxed again.

Comprehensive income (wages, labour remuneration, author's remuneration, royalties) uses the 3–45% progressive schedule; the foreign-currency amount converts at the PBOC central parity (P11 Arts. 3, 16). Remitting already-taxed salary adds nothing. P11 P13

US tax

This is the route where double taxation is most avoidable — via the FTC or the FEIE.

Chinese IIT on salary is creditable on Form 1116 (U06). If you instead exclude the salary under the FEIE, no credit is allowed for tax on the excluded portion (U06) — the two are mutually exclusive on the same dollars. The treaty caps Chinese tax on the relevant items at 10% gross for dividends/interest/royalties, and only the treaty-reduced amount is creditable (U07, U06). U06 U07 U08

Pros

  • Strongest documentary footing of any route.
  • Routinely processed; least discretionary.
  • FTC/FEIE planning can reduce the combined US+China burden to roughly the higher of the two systems, not the sum.

Cons

  • Only works to the extent the balance is provably salary.
  • Still bound by US$50,000/yr for the frictionless version.

M20 Inheritance transfer of inherited Chinese property conditional

(继承财产转移)

The other half of PBOC [2004] No.16: a foreign citizen or HK/Macau resident who lawfully inherits mainland property can realise it and remit the proceeds - one application, paid in one go or in instalments.

EligibilityA foreign national, or a Hong Kong/Macau resident, who has lawfully inherited property situated in mainland China. Taiwan residents are covered by Art. 14 by analogy. This is NOT available for transferring your own property - that is the emigration limb (M04).
Ceiling—
Art. 9 requires ONE application for all property inherited from the same decedent, which may then be remitted in one lump or in instalments (Art. 6 second paragraph) - so there is no annual ceiling and no USD figure to copy. Local SAFE approves below RMB 500,000; at or above it, SAFE head office (Art. 10). Blank by design. P06
Documents
  • Passport + 国外定居证明 (embassy-issued or authenticated)
  • Inheritance evidence + proof of relationship to the decedent (notarised if required)
  • Decedent's property title documents
  • Tax certificate / 完税凭证 from the competent tax authority at the property's location
  • 《个人财产对外转移外汇业务申请表》
  • SAFE approval letter + 核准件 for the bank

Step by step

  1. Confirm you are a foreign citizen or HK/Macau resident and that the property is a genuine lawful inheritance - Art. 12 refuses property that cannot prove a lawful source or is subject to unresolved proceedings (P06).
  2. Obtain the inheritance documents: the will/intestacy succession evidence and proof of your relationship to the decedent (Art. 9(4)). Notarisation is required where the documents are not already notarised (Art. 9 closing paragraph).
  3. Obtain the certificate of overseas settlement: for a foreign citizen, issued or authenticated by a Chinese embassy/consulate (Art. 9(3)).
  4. Obtain the decedent's property title documents AND the tax certificate or tax-payment voucher issued by the competent tax authority where the property is situated (Art. 9(5)).
  5. Realise the property (sell it) and pay the Chinese transaction taxes on the sale; keep the tax vouchers.
  6. Apply to the SAFE branch where the DECEDENT's household registration was located (Art. 5) - not where you live. One application covers everything inherited from that decedent (Art. 9).
  7. SAFE issues an approval letter and a capital-account approval document (核准件); take it to the designated bank to buy FX and remit (Art. 10).
  8. The bank must remit directly to your own account in your country of residence - withdrawing the foreign cash inside China is expressly forbidden (Art. 11).
  9. US side: an inheritance is generally not US taxable income, but a distribution from a foreign estate above US$100,000 is within Form 3520's scope - check the instructions before filing. Report the account while it remains foreign (U01, U02, U08).

Foreign taxes paid on the realisation may be creditable on Form 1116 to the extent the same gain is taxed by the US (U06), and the treaty's saving clause preserves full US taxing rights over citizens (U10). M06 Wise (from-CNY transfer via licensed PRC payment provider) conditional

(Wise 人民币汇出)

A fintech path for the same lawful money: you must be a non-mainland-Chinese national with a Chinese tax record from the last 3 years, and the money must go to an account you own.

EligibilityPer Wise's own page: 'You're not mainland Chinese' (HK/Macau/Taiwan residents eligible), AND you worked in China, AND you hold a valid China tax record showing taxes paid in the last 3 years. Personal accounts only.
Ceiling— · $44,652 / transfer
Single-transfer maximum CNY 300,000 from a China bank app (= US$44,652 at the 2026-11-09 parity, derived); CNY 50,000 from Alipay (= US$7,442, derived). The annual figure is a computed 'overall limit' based on your declared income minus Chinese tax paid — Wise publishes the METHOD but not a fixed number, so the annual cell is deliberately null. C02
Documents
  • Passport
  • China tax record (last 3 years)
  • China bank account or Alipay access
  • Own overseas account details

Step by step

  1. Confirm eligibility against Wise's three conditions before anything else — the mainland-Chinese exclusion is a hard gate for a Chinese citizen who has not naturalised abroad.
  2. Create/verify a Wise personal account; first-time verification takes 1–2 working days and requires documents.
  3. Upload the China tax record (个人所得税纳税记录) covering the last 3 years. Wise states the limit is computed from salary earned minus tax paid over the years evidenced.
  4. Add your own overseas account (or Wise balance) as recipient. Wise states that, due to regulation, CNY can only be sent from China to another account you own.
  5. Initiate the transfer from your China bank app (max CNY 300,000) or Alipay (max CNY 50,000). Pay into Wise; Wise converts and pays out.
  6. If the transfer exceeds your computed limit, Wise prompts for additional tax records to raise it (C02).
  7. US side: this is still your own money crossing a border — FinCEN 114 / Form 8938 thresholds still apply while any account is foreign (U01, U02). Wise's USD balance is not a foreign account once held at Wise's US entity, but do not assume — verify with the provider.

Step by step

  1. Do not do this. 31 USC §5321(a)(4) provides a civil penalty for violating the structuring prohibition in §5324, capped at the amount of currency involved in the transaction (U09).
  2. China prohibits the mirror-image conduct: splitting purchases to circumvent the quota (P01 Art. 7, P15).
  3. Lawful alternative: make ONE honest transfer at whatever size is required, and file the report — FinCEN Form 105 above US$10,000 (U03), FBAR and 8938 as applicable (U01, U02).
  4. A filed report is not an accusation. An unfiled one is a violation.
