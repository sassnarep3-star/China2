Executive summary

The brief was: money is stuck in a CNY account and cannot be converted "without paying a considerable amount of taxes." The research does not support that framing. China does not tax the conversion. What it restricts is the movement, and what the US can tax is income, not the transfer. Those are three different things and conflating them is what makes the problem look unsolvable.

1 · What is actually being asked

QuestionAnswerSource
Is there a Chinese tax on converting CNY to USD? No. A currency conversion is not one of the nine enumerated IIT income categories. Tax attaches to the income that produced the money — salary at 3–45%, passive items at a flat 20%. P11
Is there a limit on how much can be converted? Yes — a quota, not a tax. US$50,000 per person per calendar year with ID only. Above that, genuine current-account needs are permitted with authenticity documents; capital items are excluded. P02 P03 P15
How do I move a large balance then? Two honest options. Eight calendar years at one person's quota with no approvals and no irreversible steps; or the emigration transfer under PBOC [2004] No.16 — roughly two years, but you must cancel your 户口 and complete a PRC tax settlement first. P02 P06
Where does the US tax bite? Not on the transfer. A US citizen is taxed on worldwide income and §988 makes FX gain ordinary — but a personal transaction is excluded unless the gain exceeds US$200. The real exposure is reporting: FBAR, Form 8938, Form 3520. U05 U08 U01 U02

2 · The decision tree

  1. Work out what the money is. Salary that was taxed at source? A property sale? An inheritance or gift? Untaxed informal earnings? This single fact determines the Chinese tax, and it is the only question this document cannot answer for you.
  2. Ask the bank, in writing, which class you are in: 境内个人 or 境外个人. A 境内个人 gets the US$50,000 purchase quota. A 境外个人 generally does not — unless they hold a Foreign Permanent Resident ID, in which case they do. P08 P03
  3. Is the answer to (1) "taxed salary" and the amount ≤ US$50,000? Use the quota. One wire, ~$68 of published bank fees, done this year. P02 C01
  4. Is the amount larger, and is the money provably salary? Either run the quota over several years, or use the documented above-quota route with the tax voucher — legal, but discretionary. P02 P08
  5. Are you leaving China permanently? The emigration route is the only published bulk channel. Weigh ~2 years of approval against 8 years of patience, and be clear that cancelling 户口 is irreversible. P06 P11
  6. Whatever you choose, file the US side. FinCEN 114 above US$10,000 aggregate; Form 8938 above the threshold; Form 3520 above US$100,000 of gifts from a nonresident individual. Getting this wrong costs more than any of the Chinese taxes discussed here. U01 U02 U08 U04

3 · What the conversion penalty actually consists of

Five separate things get called "the tax". Only one of them is a tax.

ComponentIs it a tax?SizeSource
Bank wire commission + cable chargeNo — a fee0.1% of the CNY amount, min CNY 100 / max CNY 500, plus CNY 120 per wireC01
Retail FX spread (bid/ask)No — a marginNot published. Left blank throughout this site rather than guessed. Usually the largest single component.— F-SPREAD
China IIT on the underlying incomeYes3–45% on comprehensive income; 20% flat on interest, dividends, property rent, property transfers, incidental incomeP11
China FX-control penalty (if you break a rule)No — a penaltyup to 30% of the amount involved; individual administrative fines up to RMB 50,000P04
US tax on FX gainYes, but rarelyOrdinary, not capital — with a personal-transaction exclusion unless the gain exceeds US$200U05

The "considerable tax" in the brief is almost certainly items 2 and 3 confused with each other: a bank margin is invisible and feels like a tax, and a real IIT bill on untaxed income is a genuine tax that no route avoids. Item 3 cannot be engineered away; items 1 and 2 can be minimised by choosing the cheapest route, and item 4 is entirely optional — you only pay it by breaking a rule.
