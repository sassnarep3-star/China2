Getting money out of China

What actually stands between a CNY bank balance and a USD balance in the US — and which part of it is a tax, which part is a quota, and which part is a myth.

The direct answer

There is no Chinese tax on converting CNY into USD. The thing that stops you is a US$50,000-per-person-per-year quota, not a tax. The tax risk is a separate question — what the money is made of, and whether the US has been told about the account.

If you read only one thing

1 · The constraint is a quota, not a tax

US$50,000 per person per calendar year, ID only. A genuine current-account need above that is allowed with authenticity documents — but not for buying overseas property or securities, which are excluded. P02 P03 P15

2 · The one bulk route is emigration

If you are leaving for good and will cancel your 户口, PBOC [2004] No.16 allows staged transfer of your whole balance over about two years — but a PRC tax settlement comes first, by statute. P06 P11

3 · The expensive mistake is a reporting failure

Not paying Chinese tax — forgetting the FBAR or Form 8938. Civil maxima run to US$16,536 non-willful and US$165,353 willful per violation. U01 U02 U04

4 · Do not split, borrow quotas, or use virtual currency

Splitting is prohibited by Decree 3 Art.7 independently of any repealed penalty clause; virtual-currency business was banned outright on 2026-02-06. P01 P12
