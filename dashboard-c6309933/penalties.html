The conversion penalty, broken down

What the charge is actually made of — and how much of it can be verified at all.

The headline finding Of the ten layers that make up what feels like a "conversion penalty", only three could be quantified from official documents (L4, L7, L9), and one of those (L7) is usually zero. Six layers are BLANK because no official source published them. The two layers most likely to be large — L1 (PRC tax on the underlying income) and L3 (the bank's FX spread) — are both blank. Answering those two is worth far more than optimising the wire.

What this means in practice

Stop optimising the wire

The whole verified wire cost is between $0 and $115.02 on any amount from $10,000 to $400,000 (S09 ↗). Even eliminating it entirely saves under $115.02. Time spent shopping wire fees is time not spent on L1 and L3.

Get the dealing rate in writing

One phone call resolves L3. Ask the bank for an indicative CNY/USD dealing rate for the actual amount, and compare it against the same-day PBOC central parity rate. The difference is the real conversion penalty.

Settle PRC tax before you move the money

S10 makes "whether the outbound item has paid tax as required" an express subject of the 15-working-day review (S10 ↗). Paying it after the attempt is more expensive than paying it before.

Reporting exposure does not shrink by instalments

FBAR exposure under S05 is per year the account went unreported. Splitting the transfer across years does not reduce it, and it multiplies the RMB 100 minimum and RMB 120 cable on every wire.
