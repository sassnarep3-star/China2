China Money — CNY→USD repatriation research

Lawful routes, verified costs and honest blanks for moving a mainland China CNY balance into USD as a U.S. citizen. Research, not tax or legal advice.

The short answer Nothing fetched in this session imposes a tax on the act of converting CNY into USD. 26 U.S.C. §988(e)(2) says a gain on a personal currency transaction is not recognised at all unless the gain exceeds $200 (S06 ↗), and the IRS states it has no official exchange rate and accepts any posted rate used consistently (S03 ↗). What is being experienced as a "conversion tax" is almost certainly PRC tax on the income that created the balance, which the bank and the tax authority gatekeep before the money is allowed to leave — not a charge on the conversion. That PRC rate could not be verified from an official source in this session and is therefore left BLANK, not guessed (flag F01).

Read this before using any number here The verified bank charge is a floor, not a total. The HSBC tariff states that its free lines do not extend to receiving-bank or correspondent-bank charges in the payment path (S09 ↗), and it publishes no FX dealing spread. The spread is very likely the largest single cost — at 0.50% it would be $2,000 on $400,000, more than seventeen times the highest verified bank fee in this dataset — but 0.50% is an assumption, so it is not published here (flag F02). Discover it with one phone call: ask for an indicative dealing rate in writing.

What is actually known

  • Requester is a U.S. citizen. — Worldwide income is reportable to the IRS regardless of residence (S04 confirms the U.S. dollar functional-currency rule and that U.S. tax must be remitted in USD).
  • A CNY balance exists in a bank account in mainland China. — The balance is a foreign financial account for U.S. reporting purposes; the reporting thresholds are S05-adjacent and are set out on the taxes page.
  • The requester believes conversion into USD carries a considerable tax cost. — Unverified as a tax. Nothing fetched in this session imposes a tax on the act of converting currency. See the conversion-penalty breakdown.

Answers established so far

A01. No tax on the act of converting CNY into USD was located in any document fetched this session.

Support: S03 and S04 govern how the IRS wants foreign currency translated and state the IRS has no official exchange rate. Neither imposes a charge. S06 makes a personal foreign-currency gain non-recognised unless the gain exceeds USD 200.

Confidence: Limited to the documents fetched. This is not a conclusion that no PRC charge exists; the PRC statutory rate table was not verified (flag F01).

A02. The largest verified cost component in this dataset is the bank's wire and cable charge, and it is small and capped.

Support: S09: 0.1% with a RMB 100 minimum and RMB 500 maximum, plus a RMB 120 cable. For the SHA charge-sharing options (M03, M04) the verified HSBC charge ranges from RMB 220 to RMB 620 across USD 10,000 to USD 400,000, i.e. about USD 30.86 to USD 86.97 at the S03 rate of 7.129; adding the RMB 200 OUR pre-charge (M05) raises that to RMB 420-820, about USD 58.91 to USD 115.02. It is RMB 0 for Private Banking (M01) and Premier Elite (M02), and RMB 0 for a counter cash withdrawal (M07). The 0.1% percentage component first hits its RMB 500 cap at the USD 80,000 tier.

Confidence: High for HSBC China at the tariff effective 2026-10-31. No other bank verified.

A03. The USD 50,000 figure that usually blocks a large transfer is a recordal threshold and a purchase quota, not a tax.

Support: S10 requires a tax recordal for a single outbound payment above USD 50,000 equivalent, with personal study/travel/visiting-relatives FX exempt under Article 3(13); S11 confirms one recordal per contract, an online channel, and an unchanged threshold. S02 separately sets the individual annual FX purchase quota at USD 50,000.

Confidence: High. Both are official documents.

A04. A lump-sum exit does exist and does not require year-by-year staging.

Support: S01 Section VI permits an emigrant's property to be remitted in a lump sum or in batches within the SAFE-approved limit, and cancels the old RMB 500,000 record-filing requirement.

Confidence: High for the rule. Eligibility depends on Q02, which is unanswered.

A05. On the U.S. side the material exposure is reporting, not the conversion.

Support: S05 sets the FBAR civil ceilings at USD 16,536 non-willful and USD 165,353 willful per violation. S07 and S08 set the FEIE at USD 132,900 for 2026 and USD 137,300 for 2027, but the FEIE reaches earned income only.

Confidence: High for the cited amounts. Application depends on Q07 and Q08.
