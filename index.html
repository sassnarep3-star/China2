Getting CNY out of China: what it actually costs

A US citizen's RMB balance is not blocked by a tax on converting currency. It is blocked by documentation, and the largest real cost is the bank's FX spread. Below is the costed answer, then the research behind it.

$10,000 out

$37 – $157

0.37% – 1.57% of the amount

$100,000 out

$307 – $1,507

0.31% – 1.51% of the amount

$400,000 out

$1,207 – $6,007

0.30% – 1.50% of the amount

Cheapest route that actually delivers USD to an overseas account: documented income, converted at the counter, moved by same-bank Global Transfer or SWIFT. Excludes any PRC tax on the source money (that is a separate question, answered inside) and excludes US preparer fees.

Short version. If the RMB is already-taxed income, the extra PRC tax to convert it is zero and the all-in cost is roughly 0.3%–1.8%. If it was never taxed, the 3%–45% or flat 20% being called a “conversion penalty” is simply unpaid PRC tax, and the lawful fix is to pay it, get the certificate, convert, and claim it in the US on Form 1116. If you emigrated from China, the dedicated emigration transfer route has no annual quota but takes up to two years. Crypto, private swaps, borrowed quotas and underground banks are criminal or prohibited, with fines of 20%–100%.
