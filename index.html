Getting money out of China as a US citizen

A working dashboard for one question: what does it actually cost to turn RMB in a mainland bank account into USD in a US account, from $10,000 to $400,000? Every figure below is either computed from an official statute or left blank and flagged. Nothing is guessed.

Headline finding
0%

There is no Chinese tax on converting your own money

The PRC Individual Income Tax Law taxes nine categories of income. "Converting RMB to USD" is not one of them. What people experience as a conversion penalty is almost always one of three different things: income tax already owed on the money's source, a bank refusing to process without a tax certificate, or the commercial spread and wire fees. Each of those is a separate problem with a separate fix.

The US side is usually zero too

Wiring your own funds to your own account is not a realisation event. For currency, §988(e)(1) excludes an individual's personal transactions from §988, and §988(e)(2) recognises no gain on a personal transaction unless the gain would exceed $200.

If the money is untaxed income, the US tax is usually neutralised by the foreign tax credit: the PRC rate is normally higher, so the credit absorbs the US liability. At $400,000 the credit leaves $23,134 of US tax in the 20% scenario, in the model used here.

Executive summary

The short version. Establish ownership and the lawful source of the balance first, then choose the document set. If the income was already taxed in China, the authorised-bank conversion route (M01) costs 0% in PRC tax and 0% in US tax on the act of moving it; the only unknown is the bank's commercial price. The USD 50,000 annual quota is not the controlling rule for a US citizen converting documented lawful income — Article 13 of the implementing rules sets documents, not a dollar cap — but the eligibility boundary is genuinely unclear and is flagged (F02).

The expensive mistake to avoid. Every prohibited channel (smurfing, underground swaps, fake invoices, crypto bridges, card cash-out) is priced as a percentage of the amount: up to 30%, or 30%–100% if serious, plus confiscation. At $400,000 that is up to $120,000 on the low scale and $400,000 on the high scale. No legitimate fee comes close.

What cannot be certified. No provider can be named cheapest, no personal tax amount can be stated, and no commercial fee can be filled in, because none of those facts is available from an official source or from the information supplied. Those items are blank and listed on Flagged for review.
