Tax: what is actually charged, and on what

Converting and wiring are not taxed in themselves. The income behind the balance is, in China when earned and in the US as worldwide income, and the US also looks at exchange gain.

Exchange gain: an illustration

You hold yuan; your tax currency is the dollar. If the yuan is worth more dollars on the day you convert than on the day you got it, the difference is an exchange gain. Gain = dollars received × (1 − 6.6851 ÷ the rate when acquired), using Bank of China's selling rate of 6.6851 for today and the IRS yearly average as a stand-in for the acquisition rate. A negative figure is a loss. This is not a tax bill: it shows the size of the amount the US rules apply to.

Two rules decide the treatment (26 USC 988 · IRS Pub. 525). In a personal transaction a gain of $200 or less is ignored and a larger one is reported as a capital gain. Otherwise disposing of a foreign-currency bank deposit is a section 988 transaction and the gain is ordinary income. Which applies depends on why the money was held; take the actual dates and rates to a US tax adviser.
