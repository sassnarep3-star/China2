Documented bank purchase and wire

M01 Bank of China — buy USD and wire it Lawful

Rate from BOC's board; commission 1‰ (min ¥50, max ¥260) plus ¥150 cable for destinations outside Hong Kong, Macau and Taiwan.

Who. A foreign passport holder with lawful, tax-paid renminbi income earned in China and an account at this bank.

Limit. No published cap for documented current-account income; the cap is what your papers support.

Steps

  1. Confirm the balance is lawful renminbi income you received in China (pay, fees, rent, interest, sale proceeds) and that Chinese tax on it has been paid or withheld. The rule is: identity document plus source-of-funds papers that show the amounts, including tax vouchers.
  2. Collect the papers: passport; the tax payment record for the income (the withholding agent must give you the figures); and whatever shows the amount and origin of the money, such as the employment contract or bank statements. Which papers a branch accepts is its own decision — ask first.
  3. Go to (or log in to) the bank that holds the renminbi. Ask to buy US dollars (购汇) against those papers. The amount you may buy is the documented, after-tax amount.
  4. The bank converts at its selling rate at that moment (the board rate is only indicative) and debits the renminbi.
  5. Ask for an outward telegraphic transfer (境外电汇) to your own US account: beneficiary name, account number, bank name, SWIFT/ABA and address. Pay the commission and the cable fee.
  6. Keep the purchase slip and the wire confirmation. If one payment is above USD 50,000 the bank may ask how the tax-filing rule applies; for foreign individuals the announcement points back to the personal foreign-exchange rules.
  7. In the US: note the dollar amount received and the date. Work out any exchange gain against your dollar cost of the renminbi, keep the Chinese account on your FBAR and Form 8938 for the year, and claim credit for Chinese income tax where the rules allow.

For

  • The route the rules are written for: current-account income is convertible with ID and source papers
  • Cheapest route at size where both rate and fee are published
  • One transaction, bank-to-bank, with a paper trail that answers later questions from either tax authority

Against

  • Needs tax vouchers for the underlying income; money without papers cannot be bought out this way
  • Branch practice varies and is not published
  • Foreign intermediary and receiving-bank charges are extra and not published by the sending bank

Sources. BOC rates · BOC fees · SAFE FAQ 08 · PBOC Decree 3 · FX Regulations · Tax-filing rule · Income Tax Law · 26 USC 988 · IRS FBAR

M09 HSBC Global Transfer to your own HSBC account abroad Lawful

Tariff item 2.2.6.1: free for Private Banking and Premier, USD 20 (shown as about ¥145) for Advance. The exchange rate applied is not stated in the tariff, so the all-in cost is left blank.

Who. HSBC China customer who also holds an HSBC account in a country that offers Global Transfer to Self.

Limit. Service limits are not stated in the tariff.

Steps

  1. Hold accounts in your own name at HSBC China and at HSBC in the destination country, linked in online banking.
  2. Convert renminbi to US dollars at HSBC China under the same documentary rule as any purchase of foreign exchange (ID plus source-of-funds papers including tax vouchers).
  3. Send the dollars with Global Transfer to the linked own-name account.
  4. Move the money on from the overseas HSBC account to your usual US bank if needed, and handle the US reporting as for a wire.

For

  • No transfer fee for Private Banking and Premier
  • Own-name to own-name

Against

  • Needs an HSBC account on both sides
  • Exchange rate for this service is not published in the tariff
  • The documentary rule for buying foreign exchange is unchanged

Sources. HSBC tariff · SAFE FAQ 08 · HSBC rates

Special legal entitlements

M10 Annual facilitation quota (USD 50,000 a year) — only with a Chinese Foreign Permanent Resident ID Conditional

Within the quota a purchase needs ID only. Cost per wire is the same as a bank wire; the table uses Bank of China's rate and fee with one wire per calendar year.

Who. Foreign individuals holding a PRC Foreign Permanent Resident ID card. An ordinary work or residence permit does not qualify.

Limit. USD 50,000 per person per calendar year.

Steps

  1. Check that you hold a Foreign Permanent Resident ID card; SAFE states that these holders 'apply the annual facilitation quota'.
  2. Buy up to the equivalent of USD 50,000 per calendar year at a bank with that ID.
  3. Wire the dollars to your own US account; repeat in later years for larger amounts.
  4. Never split one sum across other people's quotas: the rules forbid evading the limit by splitting.
  5. US side as for a wire.

For

  • ID only, within the quota
  • Same low wire cost

Against

  • Not available to most foreign passport holders
  • USD 400,000 would take eight calendar years
  • Amounts above the quota fall back to the documented route anyway

Sources. SAFE Q&A 9 · SAFE detailed rules · PBOC Decree 3 · BOC rates · BOC fees

M11 Convert unused renminbi back (money you originally brought in) Lawful

Reconversion needs ID and the original exchange memo. Without a memo only USD 500 a day can be exchanged on ID alone. Cost shown is Bank of China's rate and wire fee.

Who. A foreign individual whose renminbi came from exchanging foreign currency in China and who still has the original exchange memo, dated within 24 months.

Limit. Amount on the original memo; ID-only exchange USD 500 per day (USD 1,000 airside before departure).

Steps

  1. Find the original exchange memo (兑换水单) for the earlier conversion into renminbi; it is valid for 24 months from the exchange date.
  2. Take passport and memo to the bank and convert the unused renminbi back, up to the amount on the memo.
  3. Wire the dollars out or withdraw notes within the cash-carry limits.
  4. US side: this is your own capital returning; still compute exchange gain or loss on the renminbi.

For

  • No tax voucher needed: the money was never Chinese income
  • Simple if the memo is recent

Against

  • Useless for renminbi earned in China
  • Memo older than 24 months no longer works
  • USD 500-a-day ID-only exchange is too small to matter at these amounts

Sources. SAFE FAQ 10 · PBOC Decree 3 · BOC rates · BOC fees

Limit. Sale price less taxes on the sale.

Steps

  1. Complete the sale and the title transfer at the housing authority.
  2. Pay the taxes on the sale and obtain the tax certificate; above USD 50,000 the filing form for outbound payments is also listed among the papers.
  3. Take passport, the registered sale contract, proof of title transfer and the tax papers to the bank.
  4. The bank checks that the amount requested does not exceed the figure on the tax certificate, sells you the dollars and wires them abroad.
  5. US side: report the sale itself (gain on the property) and any exchange gain; credit Chinese income tax where allowed.

For

  • Expressly permitted for foreign individuals
  • Large single amounts possible

Against

  • Chinese tax on the gain is 20% under the income tax law; other transfer taxes were not verified
  • Capped at price less taxes
  • Wording in force today was read only from a draft and a 2017 branch guide

Sources. PBOC Decree 3 · SAFE housing draft · Tax-filing rule · Income Tax Law · BOC rates · BOC fees

M13 Inherited assets in China (inheritance transfer, SAFE approval) Lawful

A separate approval route: apply once for everything inherited from the same person to the SAFE office where the deceased was registered, then buy and remit in one or several payments. Cost shown is Bank of China's rate and wire fee.

Who. A foreign citizen who lawfully inherited assets located in mainland China.

Limit. The approved amount.

Steps

  1. Establish the inheritance (notarised proof of the right to inherit) and turn the assets into renminbi.
  2. Obtain the tax certificate from the tax office where the estate is located.
  3. Apply once to the SAFE office of the deceased's place of household registration for all assets inherited from that person.
  4. With the approval, buy dollars at the bank; the bank must wire them straight to your own account in your country of residence — no cash withdrawal in China.
  5. US side: a bequest from a foreign person above USD 100,000 in a year is reported on Form 3520.

For

  • A defined legal route for sums of any size
  • One application covers the whole estate

Against

  • Approval takes time and the proof of inheritance must be notarised
  • Late or missing Form 3520 carries penalties

M14 Emigrant asset transfer (former Chinese national, assets held before emigrating) Lawful

Apply once for the whole amount to the SAFE office of your former place of household registration. There has been no instalment schedule since 10 February 2014: after approval the bank may send the approved amount in one payment or in several. Cost shown is one Bank of China wire. Corrected 11 November 2026: this entry previously priced three wires over two years under the 2004 schedule.

Who. A person who moved abroad from mainland China and took foreign nationality, for lawful assets owned in China before obtaining that status.

Limit. The approved amount, in one payment or several. No instalment schedule since 2014.

Steps

  1. Gather proof of status (foreign passport, consular proof of settlement abroad, cancellation of household registration), proof of ownership and origin of the assets, and tax certificates.
  2. Apply once to the SAFE office of your former place of household registration for the full amount you intend to move.
  3. After approval take the approval document to the bank, buy dollars and wire them to your own account abroad — in one payment or several, within the approved amount.
  4. US side: your own assets, not income on arrival; compute exchange gain or loss and keep the accounts on FBAR and Form 8938 meanwhile.

For

  • Works for old savings and property that have no salary tax trail in your foreign name
  • Any amount, with approval

Against

  • SAFE approval and tax certificates required before the bank will act
  • Money stays exposed to the exchange rate while the approval is pending

M15 Wise — send from renminbi to your own account abroad Lawful

Wise runs this through a PBOC-licensed payment partner. Own account only; at most ¥300,000 per transfer from a bank app; total limit equals after-tax salary shown on the tax records. The fee is quoted in the app and was not read.

M16 Withdraw cash abroad from a mainland bank card (UnionPay ATM) Lawful

Hard caps: ¥100,000 per person per calendar year across all cards, ¥10,000 per card per day. Exceeding the yearly cap blocks overseas withdrawals for that year and the next. Only the card issuer's fee could be read (China Merchants Bank: 0.5%, minimum ¥10).

Who. Anyone holding a mainland-issued bank card.

Limit. ¥100,000 per person per calendar year; ¥10,000 per card per day.

Steps

  1. Use your own mainland card at an ATM abroad that accepts UnionPay.
  2. Withdraw at most ¥10,000 equivalent per card per day and ¥100,000 equivalent per calendar year in total.
  3. Deposit the dollars in your US account.
  4. Do not borrow or lend cards to get around the cap: the circular forbids it.

For

  • No papers
  • Useful for small top-ups

Against

  • About USD 15,000 a year at today's rate
  • USD 400,000 would take 27 years
  • Network exchange rate and ATM-owner surcharge were not read

M17 Carry US-dollar notes out of China Lawful

Up to USD 5,000 per exit needs no permit; USD 5,000-10,000 needs a bank-issued carrying permit; more than USD 10,000 needs a SAFE permit and is refused in principle. A second exit within 15 days is limited to USD 1,000. The US requires a FinCEN 105 report above USD 10,000.

Who. A traveller leaving China with dollars lawfully bought or withdrawn.

Limit. USD 5,000 per exit without a permit; USD 10,000 with a bank permit.

Steps

  1. Buy dollars at the bank under the documentary rule (or withdraw dollars you already hold).
  2. For more than USD 5,000 ask the bank for a carrying permit (携带证), valid 30 days, one use, at most USD 10,000.
  3. Declare as required when leaving; space trips more than 15 days apart.
  4. On arrival in the US declare if the total is above USD 10,000, then deposit.

For

  • No wire fee
  • Simple for a few thousand dollars

Against

  • 40 trips with permits for USD 400,000, more than 15 days apart
  • Theft and loss risk
  • Cash deposits draw bank questions in the US
  • Fine up to 20% for breaching the carrying rules

Sources. Cash-carry measures · FX Regulations · CBP · BOC rates · HSBC tariff

M22 Leave the money in China and spend it there Lawful

No conversion, so no conversion cost today. The balance stays exposed to the exchange rate and stays reportable in the US every year.

Who. Anyone.

Limit. No limit.

Steps

  1. Keep the account; bank deposit interest is currently exempt from Chinese individual income tax.
  2. Report the account on FBAR above USD 10,000 aggregate and on Form 8938 above its thresholds.
  3. Convert later, or spend in China.

For

  • Nothing to pay now
  • Keeps options open while papers are gathered

Against

  • Exchange-rate risk in both directions
  • US reporting continues; penalties for missing FBARs are large
  • Does not solve the problem

Unlawful — avoid

M23 Unlawful channels — listed so they can be recognised and refused Unlawful

Underground banks and matched 'swap' deals, borrowing other people's quotas or splitting one sum among them, buying dollars through crypto dealers, sham trade or invoices, undeclared cash, lent or borrowed bank cards. No instructions are given. The figures in the table are the fine bands in the Regulations, not a price.

Who. Nobody.

Steps

  1. Do not use. Fines are up to 30% of the amount, and from 30% up to the whole amount in serious cases, on top of being ordered to reverse the deal; criminal liability is possible.
  2. The counterparty risk is total: the money can simply disappear, and there is no legal claim to it.

For

  • Nothing that survives the penalties

Against

  • Fine up to 30% of the amount; 30%-100% in serious cases
  • Possible criminal liability
  • Up to ¥50,000 personal fine for untrue documents
  • Money with no lawful paper trail also causes US bank and tax problems
