Pros, cons and steps

Bank conversion and wire of documented income Lawful

Who it fits: A foreign passport holder whose RMB is lawful income earned in China, such as salary, and who holds the tax records for it. Limit: No yearly cap in the rule read; the amount must match the documents.

For

  • The whole documented amount can go in one transaction.
  • Lowest published cost of any route: about 0.2% to 0.5%.
  • Leaves a clean paper trail for U.S. reporting.
  • Can be handled by an agent holding your documents if you are abroad (S07, Art. 4).

Against

  • Needs source papers that show the amount, including tax vouchers.
  • The bank checks that the papers are genuine and match the payment; branches differ in what they accept.
  • A single payment above USD 50,000 in the listed categories needs a tax recordal form first (S10).

Step by step

  1. Gather your passport, the contract or payslips that show the income, and the PRC tax payment records for the same period.
  2. Ask the branch, in writing if you can, which source-of-funds papers it accepts and whether it wants a tax recordal form for a single payment above USD 50,000.
  3. If the bank asks for the recordal form, file it with the local tax office and take the stamped copy back to the bank.
  4. The bank sells you dollars at its posted selling rate (RMB 6.6911 per dollar at Bank of China when read) and wires them to your own U.S. account. Bank of China charges 0.1% (RMB 50 to 260) plus RMB 150 cable.
  5. Keep the exchange memo, the wire receipt and the tax records.
  6. On the U.S. side, keep filing FBAR and Form 8938 for any year the account was over the thresholds, and work out the currency gain if it is above USD 200.

Annual USD 50,000 facilitation quota Conditional

Who it fits: A Chinese citizen, or a foreigner who holds a Foreign Permanent Resident ID card. Other foreigners do not get this quota. Limit: USD 50,000 per person per calendar year.

For

  • ID only, no source papers inside the quota.
  • Can be done in the bank's app.

Against

  • USD 400,000 would take eight calendar years.
  • Not open to most foreign passport holders (S02).
  • Using other people's quotas for your money is splitting and is prohibited (S08).

Step by step

  1. Confirm with the bank that your ID type has the quota.
  2. Buy up to USD 50,000 in the app or at the counter and state the true purpose.
  3. Wire it to your own U.S. account; pay the wire fee each time.
  4. Repeat in each later calendar year until done.

Same bank route at HSBC China (published tariff) Lawful

Who it fits: An HSBC China customer who qualifies under M01 or M02. Limit: Same rules as M01 or M02.

For

  • Published fee schedule in English.
  • A same-name HSBC account abroad can make receiving simpler.

Against

  • HSBC's own exchange rate was not read today, so the total is blank.
  • Which customer tier pays which fee was not re-confirmed today (flag F05).

Step by step

  1. Check your account tier against the current tariff PDF.
  2. Follow M01 (documents) or M02 (quota).
  3. Ask for the rate and every fee in writing before confirming.

Wise, sending from CNY Conditional

Who it fits: A non-mainland person who works or worked in China and has a PRC tax record from the last three years; the money goes only to an account in your own name. Limit: CNY 300,000 per transfer from a bank app; overall limit roughly your after-tax China salary.

For

  • Done online, no branch visit.
  • Built for exactly the salary-with-tax-record case.

Against

  • Salary only; savings from other sources do not count toward the limit.
  • The fee and rate appear only when you request a quote, so the cost is blank here.
  • USD 400,000 would need about nine transfers.

Step by step

  1. Open and verify a Wise account in your own name.
  2. Upload your PRC tax record; Wise sets your overall sending limit from it.
  3. Create a CNY to USD transfer to your own U.S. bank account or Wise account.
  4. Pay from your Chinese bank account or Alipay, up to CNY 300,000 at a time.
  5. Save each quote and receipt.

Overseas ATM withdrawals on a mainland card Conditional

Who it fits: Anyone with a mainland bank card who needs a small amount abroad. Limit: RMB 100,000 per person per calendar year across all cards; RMB 10,000 per card per day.

For

  • No documents.
  • Works for small amounts.

Against

  • USD 400,000 would take 27 calendar years at today's rate.
  • Going over the yearly cap bars withdrawals for that year and the next.
  • Card and ATM fees and the network rate were not read today, so the cost is blank.

Step by step

  1. Check the card works abroad and ask the issuer for its fee.
  2. Withdraw up to RMB 10,000 per card per day, and no more than RMB 100,000 in the calendar year.
  3. Deposit the cash into your U.S. account and keep the slips.

Carry dollar cash out of China Conditional

Who it fits: Someone who can lawfully buy the dollars first (M01 or M02) and travels anyway. Limit: USD 5,000 per exit without a permit; up to USD 10,000 with a bank-issued permit.

For

  • No wire fee.
  • Simple for a few thousand dollars.

Against

  • USD 400,000 would take 40 permitted trips or 80 without permits.
  • A second exit within 15 days is limited to USD 1,000.
  • Loss and theft risk; carrying against the rules can be fined up to 20% (S06, Art. 42).
  • More than USD 10,000 entering the U.S. must be declared (S26).

Step by step

  1. Buy dollar cash at the bank under M01 or M02.
  2. For more than USD 5,000, get the bank's carrying permit; it is valid 30 days for one exit.
  3. Show the permit to Chinese customs when leaving.
  4. If you carry more than USD 10,000 into the U.S., file FinCEN Form 105.
  5. Deposit the cash and keep the exchange memo.

Emigrant property transfer through SAFE Conditional

Who it fits: A person who moved from mainland China and took foreign status, transferring assets owned before emigrating. Limit: Whole amount in one application; paid out over two years in three stages unless it is RMB 200,000 or less.

For

  • A formal route for large capital sums.
  • No fixed dollar ceiling in the rule read.

Against

  • Takes at least two years above RMB 200,000.
  • Needs SAFE approval and proof the assets are lawful and yours.
  • Does not apply to people who were never mainland residents.

Step by step

  1. Apply to the SAFE branch for your former household registration area with ID, proof of emigrant status and proof of the assets.
  2. After approval, the bank converts and wires up to half.
  3. One year after the first payment, send up to half of what remains.
  4. Two years after the first payment, send the rest.

Inheritance transfer through SAFE Conditional

Who it fits: A foreign citizen who inherited a mainland estate. Limit: One application per decedent; paid in one or several transfers.

For

  • Can move the whole inheritance.
  • A bequest is not income to the U.S. recipient (S25).

Against

  • Needs SAFE approval and inheritance papers.
  • A bequest above USD 100,000 from a foreign estate must be reported on Form 3520.

Step by step

  1. Apply to the SAFE branch where the decedent was registered, with proof of relationship and of the estate.
  2. After approval, the bank converts and wires the money.
  3. File Form 3520 with your U.S. return if the bequest is above USD 100,000.

Proceeds from selling a mainland home Conditional

Who it fits: A foreign individual who sold a home in mainland China. Limit: The sale proceeds, on the capital-account documents the bank requires.

For

  • A recognised capital-account route.
  • An agent can act for you with a notarised power of attorney.

Against

  • The full document list was not read today.
  • PRC taxes on the sale were not researched today, so they are blank.

Step by step

  1. Ask the bank for its document list for a foreign individual's home-sale proceeds.
  2. Provide the sale contract, tax papers for the sale and ID; add a notarised power of attorney if an agent acts.
  3. The bank converts and wires the proceeds.

Company profits paid out as a dividend Conditional

Who it fits: Only if the money sits in a company you own rather than a personal account. Limit: Distributable after-tax profit.

For

  • The treaty caps PRC tax on the dividend at 10% for a U.S. resident beneficial owner.

Against

  • Costs 10% of the gross dividend in PRC tax.
  • Company-level tax and bank fees were not researched today.

Step by step

  1. Have the company declare a dividend from after-tax profit.
  2. The company withholds PRC tax at the treaty rate and files the tax recordal.
  3. The bank converts and wires the net dividend.
  4. Report the dividend on your U.S. return and claim the PRC tax as a foreign tax credit.

A relative sends their own money as a gift Conditional

Who it fits: The money truly belongs to a relative who is a Chinese citizen, and they choose to give it to you. Limit: The relative's own USD 50,000 yearly quota.

For

  • A gift is not income to the U.S. recipient (S25).
  • ID only inside the relative's quota.

Against

  • If the money is really yours, this is lending a quota and is prohibited (S08).
  • Five or more people remitting to one overseas person is a listed splitting feature (S09).
  • Gifts above USD 100,000 a year from one foreign person and related persons need Form 3520.

Step by step

  1. Confirm whose money it is; do not route your own money through relatives.
  2. The relative buys dollars within their own quota and wires them to you.
  3. File Form 3520 if the year's total passes USD 100,000.

Convert back RMB you originally brought in as foreign currency Conditional

Who it fits: Your RMB came from dollars you exchanged in China and you still hold the exchange memo. Limit: The unused amount on a memo up to 24 months old; USD 500 a day on ID alone.

For

  • No income papers needed, only the original memo.

Against

  • Useless if the memo is older than 24 months or lost.
  • USD 500 a day on ID alone is impractical for large sums.

Step by step

  1. Find the original exchange memo and check its date.
  2. Take it and your passport to the bank and convert back the unused amount.
  3. Wire the dollars or carry them within the cash limits.

Leave the money in China and spend it there No conversion

Who it fits: There is no pressing need for dollars. Limit: None.

For

  • No conversion cost and no bank paperwork.

Against

  • FBAR and Form 8938 filing continues each year.
  • The dollar value moves with the exchange rate.

Step by step

  1. Keep filing FBAR and Form 8938 while the balance is over the thresholds.
  2. Collect the source and tax papers now in case you convert later.
