The short answer

  1. Converting is not what gets taxed. Nothing in the PRC exchange rules read today taxes the exchange itself. Banks convert a foreigner's lawful income on ID plus papers that show the amount, including tax vouchers. S01 S06
  2. The tax, if any, is PRC income tax on how the money was earned: 3% to 45% on wages, 20% on dividends, rent and asset sales, and none at present on bank interest. If it was already withheld, converting adds no PRC tax. S14 S15
  3. The cheapest lawful route is one documented bank wire. At Bank of China's posted rate and fees it costs about 0.225% to 0.534% of the amount, before any fees taken by other banks. S17 S18
  4. The U.S. taxes the income when earned and credits PRC tax paid on it. Moving the money is not a new taxable event, but a currency gain above $200 is recognised, and the yuan is stronger now than in 2023 to 2025. S28 S21 S29
  5. The large U.S. penalties are for missed forms, not for the transfer: FBAR, Form 8938 and Form 3520. Bring those up to date first. S22 S23 S24 S25
