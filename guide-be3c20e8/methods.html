Lawful, conditional, unverified and prohibited channels are separated. Prohibited channels have no execution steps; their statutory exposure is on the penalty-layers page.

R01 · lawful · documentary · complete pricing

BOC documented-income online FX purchase + one wire

Convert lawful current-account RMB income at BOC and wire USD to your own U.S. account.

Best for: Foreign individual with ID, transaction-amount source materials and a tax certificate.

Pros

  • BOC publishes both a USD board and an e-banking wire tariff.
  • One outgoing wire is possible when the bank accepts the documents.
  • Own-account remittance is the statutory property-transfer model and Wise's own-account rule is consistent.

Cons / limits

  • The board is reference-only and changes intraday.
  • The bank may refuse documents or apply local/agreement fees.
  • Intermediary and receiving fees are unpublished; U.S. reporting and §988 still apply.

Step-by-step

  1. Ask the PRC bank in writing whether it accepts your passport, lawful-source materials and tax certificate for the documented-income route; keep the answer.
  2. Review the live BOC board, then obtain an executable quote in online banking or at the branch. The web board says the actual transaction rate governs.
  3. Buy USD with that actual rate and retain the exchange memo and tax/source pack.
  4. Submit one cross-border telegraphic transfer to your own U.S. account; compare the bank's quote with BOC's published 0.10% fee, CNY 50 minimum, CNY 260 maximum and CNY 150 cable charge.
  5. Before execution, ask for correspondent, intermediary and receiving-bank fees in writing; the tariff excludes charges by other institutions.
  6. Record acquisition and disposal rates; have FBAR, Form 8938, §988 and FTC/FEIE effects reviewed before filing.

R02 · conditional · own quota only · complete pricing

BOC own annual-convenience-quota purchase + wires

Use your own annual FX purchase convenience quota, then wire USD; larger amounts need later calendar years.

Best for: A qualifying domestic individual or foreign permanent-residence ID card holder who needs a slow, quota-compliant route.

Pros

  • The annual quota and wire thresholds are published by SAFE.
  • The BOC board and e-banking tariff allow a reproducible cost illustration.
  • Using only your own quota does not involve borrowed quotas or a watch-list evasion.

Cons / limits

  • A U.S. passport alone does not establish quota eligibility.
  • Amounts above the quota require multiple calendar years and unknown future rates.
  • Splitting or borrowing quotas is prohibited and can trigger the watch list.

Step-by-step

  1. Confirm with the bank, in writing, that you hold the foreign permanent-residence ID card or otherwise qualify for the annual convenience quota.
  2. Use no more than US$50,000 of your own quota per calendar year; do not borrow another person's quota.
  3. Obtain the actual bank transaction rate each time rather than relying on a historical board.
  4. Buy USD and wire from your own FX account to your own overseas account under the published wire rule.
  5. Repeat only in a later calendar year using your own quota; record each year's rate and documents.
  6. Keep the exchange memos and complete any FBAR/Form 8938 filings triggered by the account balance.

R03 · lawful · conversion only · partial pricing

BOC CNY→USD purchase and hold in a PRC FX savings account

Convert CNY to USD and deposit it in your own foreign-currency savings account in China; no money leaves China yet.

Best for: Someone who needs to stop CNY exposure while documents for a later remittance are assembled.

Pros

  • SAFE expressly allows purchased FX to be deposited in the person's own FX savings account.
  • No outgoing wire fee is incurred at this step.
  • The published board still permits a spread illustration.

Cons / limits

  • This is not repatriation; the funds remain a foreign financial account for U.S. reporting purposes.
  • Future remittance rate and bank charges are unknown.
  • The BOC board is reference-only.

Step-by-step

  1. Confirm the bank will open or credit your own FX savings account and accept the source/tax documents.
  2. Obtain an executable USD sell rate; the published board is only a reference.
  3. Buy USD and credit the USD to the same-name FX savings account; retain the memo.
  4. Do not treat the conversion as a transfer; continue to document the source and filing obligations.
  5. When ready to remit, return to a lawful wire/approval route and obtain fresh quotes.

R04 · conditional · permits · partial pricing

BOC USD cash purchase + physical carry

Buy foreign notes at BOC and carry them abroad within the published permit limits.

Best for: Small amounts where a lawful cash route is genuinely needed and all permits/reports can be obtained.

Pros

  • BOC publishes a cash sell price; foreign-currency cash withdrawal rules are published.
  • Up to US$5,000 per trip needs no carry permit; a bank permit covers the next band.

Cons / limits

  • Cash withdrawal above the daily threshold requires advance SAFE reporting.
  • Over US$10,000 is generally prohibited except special SAFE permits; CBP aggregates families/groups above US$10,000.
  • Security, deposit, cash-conversion and permit fees are unpublished; repeated trips create future-rate and reporting exposure.

Step-by-step

  1. Ask the bank whether it has the notes and what cash sell rate applies; reserve large withdrawals if required.
  2. Buy the USD notes with your own funds and keep the purchase memo.
  3. If the amount is within US$5,000, no carry permit is needed; otherwise obtain a bank-issued or, in special cases, SAFE-issued permit before departure.
  4. Do not exceed the permit amount or validity; keep the permit for inspection.
  5. On U.S. entry, report collectively carried currency/monetary instruments above the CBP threshold.
  6. Deposit or convert the cash only after obtaining the receiving institution's written rate and fees.

R05 · lawful · documentary · complete pricing

HSBC China outward telegraphic transfer (Premier/Advance)

Convert CNY at HSBC China and send a USD telegraphic transfer to your own U.S. account.

Best for: A customer with accepted documentation and a Premier/Advance account who wants a published tariff.

Pros

  • The HSBC board and current tariff are both published.
  • The outward TT formula has a clear cap and cable charge.
  • The bank's document-review duty is explicit.

Cons / limits

  • The board is dated and reference-only; the PDF has two effective-date headers.
  • Intermediary/receiving fees and taxes are excluded from the tariff.
  • Private Banking, Premier Elite and local waivers change the fee; personal eligibility is unknown.

Step-by-step

  1. Ask HSBC China for its written source-of-funds checklist and confirmation that your account segment is Premier or Advance.
  2. Obtain a same-day executable USD sell rate; do not rely on the web board alone.
  3. Buy USD and submit the outward TT to your own U.S. account.
  4. Compare the quoted sender fee with the published 0.10% formula, CNY 100 minimum, CNY 500 maximum and CNY 120 cable charge.
  5. If full beneficiary credit is needed, ask about Pay Exact and the published CNY 200 charge; remember any shortfall can still be collected.
  6. Obtain written correspondent/receiving charges and keep records for U.S. reporting and currency-gain analysis.

R06 · conditional · segment · complete pricing

HSBC China outward telegraphic transfer (Private Banking individual)

Same conversion and wire, using the tariff's free individual Private Banking TT pricing.

Best for: A customer whom HSBC confirms is an individual Private Banking customer.

Pros

  • The tariff says outward TT issuance and cable are free for individual Private Banking customers.
  • The published board still permits a spread illustration.

Cons / limits

  • Private Banking eligibility and any separate account terms were not verified.
  • The board is reference-only and the PDF has two date headers.
  • Third-party charges and personal tax/reporting exposure remain blank.

Step-by-step

  1. Ask HSBC to confirm in writing that your account is an individual Private Banking account and that the free TT/cable tariff applies.
  2. Obtain a same-day executable USD sell rate and full source-of-funds approval.
  3. Buy USD and instruct the TT to your own U.S. account.
  4. Confirm that no separate cable or issuance charge will be debited; keep the tariff and written confirmation.
  5. Request all correspondent/receiving charges in writing and complete U.S. reporting/currency-gain checks.

R07 · conditional · own HSBC account · complete pricing

HSBC same-name Global Transfer (Private Banking/Premier)

Convert CNY and use HSBC Global Transfer to your own HSBC account abroad where the service is available.

Best for: A customer with confirmed same-name HSBC accounts in both countries and a free-tariff segment.

Pros

  • The tariff publishes Global Transfer as free for Private Banking and Premier.
  • A same-name transfer fits the own-account theme in the official rules.
  • The board still lets the spread be illustrated.

Cons / limits

  • U.S.-side availability and terms were not independently read here.
  • The exchange spread remains; the board is reference-only.
  • Premier monthly/waiver status and third-party charges are unknown.

Step-by-step

  1. Ask both HSBC entities in writing whether same-name Global Transfer to your U.S. HSBC account is available and which segment applies.
  2. Obtain an executable USD conversion rate and written source-of-funds approval.
  3. Buy USD, then initiate Global Transfer to the same-name account.
  4. Confirm the free transfer charge from the tariff; do not confuse the education-payment $7 charge with Global Transfer.
  5. Keep the exchange memo and written fee confirmations; complete U.S. reporting and §988 checks.

R09 · conditional · provider · blank pricing

Wise from-CNY transfer to your own overseas account

Use a licensed partner's from-CNY product, subject to tax-record verification and an overall limit.

Best for: A non-mainland individual with recent official PRC tax records and an account in the same name.

Pros

  • Wise publishes eligibility, own-account restrictions and per-transfer caps.
  • The product uses a PBOC-regulated licensed partner and provides bank-app/Alipay payment routes.

Cons / limits

  • The exact fee, conversion rate and personal overall limit are not published on the help page.
  • Overall limit depends on income and tax paid; this is not a regulator's eligibility guarantee.
  • Mainland Chinese nationals are not eligible under the stated rule.

Step-by-step

  1. Confirm in the official product that you are not mainland Chinese, worked in China and have a tax record covering the required recent period.
  2. Complete first-time verification and upload the official tax record; do not fund before seeing the assigned limit.
  3. Choose bank-app or Alipay payment and observe the applicable published per-transfer cap.
  4. Set up the transfer to your own overseas account and capture the exact fee, conversion rate and delivery estimate on screen.
  5. Keep the quote, tax record and transfer receipt; compare with a bank wire before funding.
  6. Complete U.S. reporting and currency-gain checks with the documented dates and rates.

R10 · conditional · approval · blank pricing

SAFE emigrant property transfer approval

One-off approval to convert and remit lawful property owned before emigration, after hukou cancellation.

Best for: A person with foreign citizenship/permanent residence and pre-status lawful property in mainland China.

Pros

  • The later SAFE rule abolished compulsory staging and HQ filing; the bank may remit once or in instalments within the approved amount.
  • The service guide publishes no administrative fee and gives acceptance/decision timelines.
  • The route is designed for a full property balance, not an annual quota.

Cons / limits

  • Eligibility, hukou cancellation, source/title and tax documents are private and strict.
  • The approval is single-use and requires an in-person visit in the published guide.
  • Bank FX rate, wire fee and third-party charges are blank; future staged rates are unknown.

Step-by-step

  1. Confirm that you obtained the qualifying foreign status and that the property was owned before that status.
  2. Obtain hukou-cancellation, foreign-settlement, ID, income-source, property-title and tax-certificate documents.
  3. Apply to the original-hukou SAFE office; ask for its current checklist and whether the single-use approval covers the full amount.
  4. Wait for the published acceptance and decision periods; do not move funds before approval.
  5. Take the approval document to the designated bank; obtain a written executable rate and all bank/correspondent/receiving charges.
  6. Instruct remittance only to your own overseas account; the rule prohibits domestic FX cash withdrawal.
  7. Keep every document for PRC tax clearance and U.S. reporting/currency-gain analysis.

R11 · conditional · approval · blank pricing

SAFE inheritance property transfer approval

Separate approval for a foreign citizen or qualifying resident to remit liquidated PRC inheritance.

Best for: An heir with official inheritance, title and tax documents.

Pros

  • The rule defines inheritance transfer separately and abolished separate applications for different decedents and notarisation of listed documents.
  • The bank must remit to the heir's own overseas account.

Cons / limits

  • An inheritance-specific service guide, administrative fee, bank rate and wire charges were not read here.
  • Eligibility and document acceptance are private; unproven lawful source is refused.

Step-by-step

  1. Obtain death, relationship/inheritance, property-title and tax-certificate documents.
  2. Apply to the SAFE office for the deceased's original hukou; ask for its current checklist.
  3. Do not treat inherited capital as salary or use another person's quota.
  4. After approval, obtain a written executable rate and all bank/correspondent/receiving charges.
  5. Instruct the bank to remit to your own overseas account; do not take domestic FX cash.
  6. Keep documents for U.S. gift/bequest reporting review and currency-basis analysis.

R12 · conditional · cash ceiling · partial pricing

Overseas ATM withdrawals with a mainland card

Withdraw local currency abroad from a mainland card, within annual/daily SAFE ceilings, then deposit/convert it.

Best for: Small amounts over time where card and foreign ATM fees/rates are obtained in writing.

Pros

  • SAFE publishes an annual per-person ceiling and a per-card daily ceiling; one HSBC tariff publishes overseas ATM charges.
  • The arithmetic for withdrawals and years is reproducible from those caps.

Cons / limits

  • The foreign ATM owner's fee, UnionPay/network conversion rate, cash deposit and security costs are blank.
  • Exceeding the annual cap blocks overseas withdrawals in the current and second years.
  • This is a slow cash route and creates CBP reporting exposure for large collective cash.

Step-by-step

  1. Ask the card issuer for its written overseas ATM fee, network FX rate and daily limit.
  2. Confirm the SAFE annual and per-card daily ceilings apply to your cards, including supplementary cards.
  3. Withdraw only within the published caps; keep each receipt and the exact conversion shown.
  4. Deposit/convert the cash at a U.S. institution only after obtaining its written rate and fees.
  5. If carrying or sending more than the CBP threshold at one time, file the required report.
  6. Record every date/rate for U.S. currency-gain and FBAR work.

R13 · unverified · do not assume · blank pricing

Direct cross-border RMB settlement to an overseas account

A possible bank current-account RMB settlement followed by offshore conversion; not verified for an accumulated savings/capital balance.

Best for: Only if a bank and SAFE give a written lawful basis, permitted purpose, limit, rate and tariff.

Pros

  • PBOC supports current-account cross-border RMB settlement for individuals, including lawful salary, under bank due diligence and AML duties.

Cons / limits

  • No general personal savings/capital route, fee, exchange rate, limit or receiving-bank term was published on that page.
  • A generic policy statement is not approval for your balance.

Step-by-step

  1. Do not start with a transfer; first ask the bank for the exact rule and permitted transaction category in writing.
  2. Ask SAFE to confirm the classification of your balance if the bank cannot cite a rule.
  3. Request the full fee, rate, limit and receiving-bank terms in writing.
  4. If any of those are missing, leave every cost cell blank and use a verified route.

R14 · conditional · permits · partial pricing

HSBC USD cash purchase + physical carry

Buy foreign notes at HSBC China, where notes withdrawal is published as free, and carry them under the permit rules.

Best for: Small amounts where HSBC has notes and all PRC/U.S. permit and reporting requirements are met.

Pros

  • HSBC publishes a cash sell/buy board and says foreign-currency notes withdrawal over the counter is free.
  • The PRC and U.S. cash limits/reporting rules are published.

Cons / limits

  • The cash buy/sell spread is larger than the TT spread; the board is reference-only and dated.
  • Security, deposit, cash-conversion and permit fees are blank.
  • Cash withdrawal and carry permits have advance-notice and amount limits.

Step-by-step

  1. Ask the branch whether it has USD notes, the advance-notice requirement and the actual cash sell rate.
  2. Buy the notes and keep the purchase and withdrawal memos; confirm the free notes-withdrawal tariff applies.
  3. Observe the PRC carry-permit rules and the permit validity period.
  4. Do not exceed the bank/SAFE permit limits; retain the documents for inspection.
  5. Report collectively carried currency/monetary instruments above the CBP threshold on U.S. entry.
  6. Obtain written U.S. deposit/conversion terms before depositing.

U01 · unlawful · not-priced pricing

Quota splitting or borrowing another person's quota

Prohibited workaround using multiple people's quotas to evade quota/authenticity management.

Best for: Not a route; listed so the penalty is explicit.

Pros

  • None; it is prohibited.

Cons / limits

  • SAFE says individuals must not use splitting to evade quota/authenticity management; borrowing quotas can place a person on the watch list for the listing year and the following two years.
  • Documentary checks are stricter during the watch period.

Step-by-step

No execution steps are provided for a prohibited route. Use a lawful route only after a written bank/SAFE decision.
