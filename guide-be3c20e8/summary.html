Executive summary

Seven conclusions. A computed illustration is labelled as such; an unpublished value is blank.

S1

Least-tax answer first

No PRC provision reviewed here imposes a separate tax on the mere act of converting CNY into USD. That is not a conclusion that your balance is tax-free: PRC individual income tax applies by income type and residence, and a bank can refuse the transaction without lawful-source and tax documents. The least-penalty path is to settle or document any tax on the underlying funds, use one of the lawful bank/approval routes, avoid prohibited workarounds, and meet U.S. reporting duties on time.

S2

The gate is documentary, not a quota trick

For a foreign individual with lawful current-account RMB income, SAFE's rule is valid ID plus transaction-amount source materials including tax documents. A foreign permanent-residence ID card holder may use the annual convenience quota; other foreign individuals generally do not get that quota merely from holding a foreign passport. Emigrant and inheritance property transfers are separate approval routes.

S5

The penalty is a stack, not one number

Separate: tax on the underlying income or asset; the bank's document review; the FX spread; sender/cable/card/cash fees; third-party and receiving-bank charges; PRC fines for evasion, splitting or underground dealing; possible U.S. ordinary gain on personal currency disposition above $200; and reporting penalties for missed FBAR, 8938, 3520 or CBP forms. Filing compliance is not a conversion tax.
