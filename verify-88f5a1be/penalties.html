The conversion penalty, broken down line by line

There is no single “conversion penalty”. The phrase covers seven different layers with different legal bases, different amounts, and different ways to avoid them. This page prices each layer at $10,000 → $400,000 where the law gives a number, and leaves it blank where it does not.

Layer 1 — PRC conversion tax: $0

SAFE imposes a quota and authenticity review, not a tax (S01). FE Regs Art. 5 does not restrict current transfers (S05). Amount at every tier: $0. Source: S01 · SAFE Tianjin (safe.gov.cn) · S05 · SAFE Beijing (safe.gov.cn) · Finding V01.

Layer 2 — PRC income tax on the source: 3–45% / 5–35% / 20% (if unpaid)

Tax attaches to the income when it arises (S08). If already paid, incremental tax at remittance is $0. If unpaid, the bank stalls conversion until tax certifies — the practical “penalty” for large balances. Your personal amount: blank (F01) — it depends on income type, years, and tax already paid. Source: S08 · State Taxation Administration (chinatax.gov.cn) · Finding V09.

Layer 3 — Bank spread: blank (unpublished)

No bank publishes a CNY/USD customer spread. Ladder models 0.30–1.50% as an explicit assumption; this session leaves it blank (F02). At $400,000, that assumption would be $1,200–$6,000 — not a quote. Get the executable rate in writing at the counter. Source: S20 · HSBC Bank (China) Company Limited (hsbc.com.cn) · Finding V27.

Layer 4 — Wire fees: $30.86 → $86.97 (published)

HSBC Premier/Advance TT: 0.1% min CNY 100 max CNY 500 + CNY 120 cable (S20). Fee floors: $10k → $30.86; $50k → $66.83; $100k → $86.97 (capped); $400k → $86.97. Private Banking individuals and Global Transfer PB/Premier: $0. Source: S20 · HSBC Bank (China) Company Limited (hsbc.com.cn) · Finding V21. See costs for all 40 tiers.

Layer 5 — U.S. tax on the conversion: $0 unless personal gain > $200

Moving your own already-taxed money is not new income. Personal FX gain is excluded unless it exceeds $200 per transaction (S16). Above that, the gain is a capital gain. Business/investment currency is different (ordinary under §988(a)). Source: S16 · U.S. Government Publishing Office (govinfo.gov) · Finding V19.

Layer 6 — U.S. reporting penalties: avoidable by filing

FBAR (>$10,000 aggregate any time, S11): non-willful ≤ $16,536/report; willful ≤ greater of $165,353 or 50% of balance (S12/S13). Form 8938 (S15): $10,000 + $10,000/30 days, max $60,000/form (S14). FinCEN 105 for >$10,000 cash entry (S18). These dwarf wire fees — at $400,000, one willful FBAR penalty (50% = $200,000) exceeds 2,000× the wire fee. File on time; use Streamlined procedures for past years. Findings V15–V18, V24.

Layer 7 — PRC fines for unlawful routes: 30% / 30–100%

Evasion, fraudulent purchase, and illegal dealing: up to 30%; serious 30–100% + criminal liability (S05). At $400,000: standard max $120,000; serious $120,000–$400,000. Crypto bridges are additionally illegal financial activity (S10). These fines are why M14–M17 are rejected. Finding V11. Dollar bands in costs.

Bottom line

If PRC tax on the income is already settled, the lawful cost of conversion is Layer 4 + Layer 3 only (fees $30.86–$86.97 + unpublished spread). The cheapest lawful strategy is not a tax shelter: settle Layer 2 so the authenticity review passes; claim the FTC (Layer 7 in the index summary) so PRC tax is not paid twice; minimise Layer 4 with competing written quotes; and shorten Layer 3 by using the documented-income route (M01) instead of quota staging (M02). Every “cheap” alternative attacks the quota by breaking the law and converts a modest quantifiable cost into Layers 6–7.

Research, not advice. No private account facts were used, no bank was contacted, no funds moved, no returns filed. Every unverified figure is blank or flagged; modelled bands are marked and are not quotes.
