Tax and penalties · what is a tax, a fee, a fine

Breaking down the “conversion penalty”

Four different things are usually called a conversion penalty. Only one is a tax, and it attaches to where the money came from — not to the exchange. The rest are a bank margin, a bank fee, and U.S. reporting obligations with penalties of their own.

No tax amount is shown for your balance. The PRC rate depends on the type of income, and nobody has established what your balance is made of (F01, F02). Any dollar figure for “the tax” would be invented.

1 · The tax is on the income, not the exchange

PRC individual income tax applies by income type: 3%–45% progressive on comprehensive income (salary, labour remuneration, author's remuneration, royalties), 5%–35% on business income, and a flat 20% on interest, dividends, property rental, property transfer and incidental income S17. The law contains no tax on converting RMB into foreign currency. Decree 532 states that current international payments and transfers are not restricted S01; what it requires is a genuine, lawful transaction basis, which the bank checks.

If you cancel PRC household registration to emigrate, the law requires a tax settlement before the cancellation S17 — that is the point at which a PRC tax bill on pre-emigration property becomes concrete.

2 · The bank margin is the largest published cost

At the board read today the customer buys USD at 668.86 and the midpoint is 667.46. That is 0.210% one way. On $100,000 it is $209.75; on $400,000 it is $839.00. It is not a tax, it is not itemised on the tariff, and it is unavoidable on any conversion S07.

3 · The published fees are small by comparison

On $100,000: BOC $61.30, HSBC Premier/Advance $92.70, HSBC individual Private Banking $0.00 S08 S09. Both tariffs cap the percentage fee in renminbi, so the fee stops growing while the margin does not.

4 · The U.S. side is reporting

  • §988. Exchange gain on an individual's personal transaction is ignored unless it exceeds $200 S12. Above that the exception does not apply.
  • FBAR. Required above $10,000 aggregate at any time in the year; due 15 April with an automatic extension to 15 October S10. Current civil maximums: $16,536 non-willful, $165,353 willful S11.
  • Form 3520. A foreign gift or bequest above $100,000 aggregate from a nonresident alien or foreign estate must be reported S15.
  • FEIE. $132,900 for tax year 2026, $130,000 for 2025 — for qualifying foreign earned income, not for principal S14.
  • Treaty. PRC withholding on dividends (Art. 9), interest (Art. 10) and royalties (Art. 11) is capped at 10% of the gross amount S18.

Form 8938 thresholds and the foreign tax credit mechanics were not re-read in this pass and are left blank rather than paraphrased from memory (F23).

5 · Penalties apply to unlawful routes, not to converting

Evasion-type conduct (Arts. 39–40) and private or disguised FX trading (Art. 45) carry a fine of up to 30% of the amount, or 30% up to the equivalent amount in serious cases, plus possible criminal liability S01. Buying foreign exchange with false documents falls under Art. 40. Virtual-currency exchange is an illegal financial activity, the civil act is void and the loss falls on the investor S05. None of these is a cost of a lawful conversion.
