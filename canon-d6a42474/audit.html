A file count is not an error count: a section may quote two rates deliberately, to show a discrepancy. What the scan establishes is that a reader will meet more than one version of the same number.

The findings that follow from it

  • The CNY 620 HSBC cap appears as both $86.97 and $92.43 (and now $92.70 here). The first is the cap converted at the IRS 2025 yearly average (7.129); the second at a 2026-11-10 board rate; the third at the board read for this page. All three are arithmetic, but only one matches the rate a customer would pay on the day (F04, F21).
  • Same-day Bank of China snapshots differ. The 10:25:02 board (666.30 / 669.11) and the 15:30:04 board (666.06 / 668.86) both appear. The tables here use the later one and say so.
  • The 2026 exclusion appears as $132,900 and $137,300. The first is published in IR-2025-103; the second is not in any IRS page read for this pass and is therefore not used (F09).
  • Staged emigrant remittance survives in older text. Files still containing 分次汇出 describe a requirement cancelled by Hui Fa [2014] No. 2 §VI(2) (F10).
  • The HSBC tariff carries two effective dates (English cover 31 October 2026, Chinese header 12 September 2024), and the scan finds the Chinese date in the file itself (F05).
  • Root-hub counts mix sections. The landing page combines method counts, row counts and source counts that come from different research runs; they are all individually sourced but do not describe one dataset.
