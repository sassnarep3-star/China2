M01 · Documented current-account income: HSBC Premier/Advance, one telegraphic transfer lawful

All-in at $100,000: $302.45 ($92.70 fee + $209.75 margin) — all 40 tiers

Who can use it

An overseas individual (境外个人) converting legitimate current-account RMB income in mainland China, or a domestic individual with the documented-income file.

Limit

No annual cap published for this document-based conversion. The USD 50,000 annual total in S02 Art. 2 applies to settlement and to a domestic individual's purchase, not to an overseas individual's documented current-account income (S06).

Documents

Valid ID + source-of-funds documents including the tax voucher (S02 Art. 13(1); S06). Contract or payslips matching the credits.

PRC tax

No tax on the exchange itself. Tax was due when the income arose: 3%–45% comprehensive, 5%–35% business, 20% flat on property transfer, interest, dividends and royalties (S17 Art. 3).

U.S. side

Principal is not income. Exchange gain on a personal transaction is ignored only up to $200 (S12). FBAR if the account exceeded $10,000 at any time (S10).

Step by step

  1. Obtain the tax record for every year the balance represents (个人所得税 App / 完税证明) and the contracts or payslips that match each large credit.
  2. Ask the bank, in writing, for the conversion rate it will apply, the fee in CNY, the cable fee, the correspondent charge and the receiving-bank charge. The tariff publishes only the first three (S09).
  3. Convert CNY to USD at the bank's selling rate (board: 668.86 per 100 USD on 2026-11-11 15:30:04, S07) and keep the exchange memo (兑换水单) — it is the only way to reverse an unspent remainder, within 24 months (S02 Art. 13(2)).
  4. Send one telegraphic transfer to your own U.S. account. Premier/Advance: 0.1% min CNY 100 max CNY 500 + CNY 120 cable (S09 2.2.4.1–2.2.4.2).
  5. File Form 114 (FBAR) by 15 April with automatic extension to 15 October if the aggregate balance crossed $10,000 (S10).

Pros

  • Published fee schedule; no quota; one wire for the whole amount.
  • Current-account payments are not restricted by statute (S01 Art. 5).

Cons

  • The bank decides whether the documents are sufficient; no published checklist binds it.
  • Correspondent and receiving-bank charges are not published (F13).

Sources: S01 S02 S06 S07 S09

Limit

No annual cap published for the document-based conversion; the banking relationship itself is the gate.

Documents

PRC tax

As M01.

U.S. side

As M01.

Step by step

  1. Confirm in writing that the account is an individual Private Banking account (the tariff's individual columns are what make the fee zero).
  2. Obtain the tax records and source documents (S02 Art. 13(1)).
  3. Convert at the bank's selling rate and keep the exchange memo.
  4. Remit: telegraphic transfer issuance and cable are listed as Free for an individual Private Banking customer (S09 2.2.4.1, 2.2.4.2).

  • Zero fee still leaves the exchange margin, which is the larger number.

M03 · Documented income: Bank of China, one cross-border wire lawful

All-in at $100,000: $271.05 ($61.30 fee + $209.75 margin) — all 40 tiers

Who can use it

Any individual with a Bank of China account and the documented-income file.

Limit

No annual cap published for the document-based conversion; the same documentary gate as M01.

Documents

As M01, plus the bank's own cross-border remittance form.

PRC tax

As M01.

U.S. side

As M01.

Step by step

  1. Assemble the tax records and source documents (S02 Art. 13(1); S06).
  2. Ask for the executed rate in writing: the board is indicative and the bank's own note says the executed rate governs (S07).
  3. Convert and remit in one instruction; the published fee is 0.1% min CNY 50 max CNY 260 plus CNY 150 cable outside Hong Kong/Macau/Taiwan (S08).
  4. Keep the exchange memo and the remittance advice.
  5. File the U.S. reports the balance triggers (S10).

Pros

  • Lower published fee ceiling than HSBC (CNY 410 all-in vs CNY 620).
  • Same board, same statutory basis.

Cons

  • Fee schedule is headed 1 November 2014; no newer published version was located in this pass (F06).
  • Branch-level practice and documentation demands are not published.

not published ⚑F14

Who can use it

HSBC China Premier or Private Banking customer with a self-named HSBC account in a country where Global Transfer to Self is offered.

Limit

Not published. Currency and destination limits are set by the receiving country's HSBC entity.

Documents

Same documentary gate as M01; the receiving account must be in your name.

PRC tax

As M01.

U.S. side

As M01; the receiving HSBC entity may also report locally.

Step by step

  1. Confirm both accounts are eligible for Global Transfer to Self (S09 remark).
  2. Complete the same source-of-funds file as M01.
  3. Convert and transfer; the tariff lists the fee as Free for Private Banking and Premier (S09 2.2.6.1).
  4. Obtain the exchange rate applied, in writing — the tariff does not publish it (F14).

Pros

  • Published fee of zero for Premier and Private Banking.
  • Avoids a correspondent chain.

Cons

  • Exchange rate applied is not published, so the all-in cost cannot be computed from official sources (F14).
  • Requires an HSBC account on both ends.

M06 · USD 50,000 annual purchase total, one wire per year (domestic individual) conditional

All-in at $100,000: $345.63 ($135.88 fee + $209.75 margin) — all 40 tiers

Who can use it

A domestic individual (境内个人): a PRC citizen, or a foreign national continuously resident in China for one year or more (S01 Art. 52(2)). A U.S. citizen living abroad is normally an overseas individual, not this category.

Limit

USD 50,000 per person per calendar year (S02 Art. 2).

Documents

Valid ID within the annual total (S02 Art. 2).

PRC tax

As M01; the tax on the income that created the balance is unchanged.

U.S. side

As M01.

Step by step

  1. Establish whether the bank classifies you as a domestic individual; get the answer in writing (see F12 for the two official definitions).
  2. Use at most USD 50,000 of your own annual purchase total per calendar year.
  3. Convert and remit in that year; do not use another person's total to accelerate this (see U01).
  4. Repeat in later years only within your own total.
  5. File the U.S. reports the balance triggers (S10).

Pros

  • Simple documentary test within the annual total.
  • Predictable.

Cons

  • Eight calendar years for USD 400,000 at USD 50,000 a year.
  • Currency exposure and bank-policy change over that period are unpriced.

M07 · Emigrant property transfer (移民财产转移) after cancelling PRC household registration conditional

All-in at $100,000: $271.05 ($61.30 fee + $209.75 margin) — all 40 tiers

Who can use it

A natural person who emigrated from mainland China (or settled in Hong Kong/Macau) and is converting lawful pre-emigration property.

Limit

No USD cap published. One application for the whole amount; SAFE approval at the original household-registration location (S03 Art. 6; S04 VI).

Documents

Written application, status form, ID, cancelled household-registration certificate, proof of settlement abroad, property-rights documents, and a PRC tax certificate or payment voucher (S03 Art. 8). Notarisation of the property documents and the agency agreement is no longer required (S04 VI(4)).

PRC tax

Pre-departure tax settlement before cancelling household registration (S17 Art. 10(5)); property-transfer IIT at 20% of the gain (S17 Art. 3).

U.S. side

Principal is not income; the property gain is a PRC-taxed event and may need U.S. reporting and a foreign tax credit — not quantified here.

Step by step

  1. Complete the pre-departure tax settlement before the household registration is cancelled (S17 Art. 10(5)).
  2. File one application with the SAFE branch where the household was registered, with the S03 Art. 8 documents.
  3. Obtain the approval letter and the capital-account approval document (核准件).
  4. Take the approval to the bank, convert and remit; the bank fee is the ordinary wire fee (S08 or S09).
  5. Keep the approval, the tax certificate and the exchange memo.

Pros

  • Designed for exactly this fact pattern: lawful pre-emigration property.
  • No USD cap; staged remittance no longer required (S04).

Cons

  • Requires cancellation of PRC household registration — a status change, not a paperwork step.
  • SAFE approval is discretionary on the documents; no published processing time or SAFE fee.

Sources: S01 S03 S04 S08 S17

M08 · Inheritance property transfer (继承财产转移) conditional

All-in at $100,000: $271.05 ($61.30 fee + $209.75 margin) — all 40 tiers

Who can use it

A foreign citizen (or Hong Kong/Macau resident) who has lawfully inherited mainland property.

Limit

No USD cap published; SAFE approval at the deceased's former household-registration location (S03 Art. 5; S04 VI).

Documents

Application, status form, ID, proof of settlement abroad, inheritance documents, the deceased's property documents and a tax certificate or payment voucher (S03 Art. 9).

PRC tax

PRC tax on the inheritance and on the sale of the inherited property must be evidenced by a tax certificate (S03 Art. 8(6), 9(5)). The rate is not computed here.

U.S. side

A bequest from a foreign person above $100,000 is reportable on Form 3520 (S15).

Step by step

  1. Obtain the inheritance documents and the PRC tax certificate for the estate and the property.
  2. File one application with SAFE where the deceased was registered (S03 Art. 5).
  3. Obtain the approval and the 核准件.
  4. Convert and remit at the bank; ordinary wire fee (S08).
  5. File Form 3520 if the bequest exceeds $100,000 (S15).

Pros

  • Lawful route for inherited property.
  • One application; no staging (S04).

Cons

  • Probate and tax documentation can take longer than the transfer itself.
  • SAFE approval is not published as automatic.

Sources: S03 S04 S08 S15

M09 · Carry foreign-currency cash (with the CBP declaration) conditional

All-in at $100,000: $209.75 ($0.00 fee + $209.75 margin) — all 40 tiers

Who can use it

Anyone able to withdraw foreign-currency notes in China and carry them.

Limit

Withdrawal above USD 10,000 equivalent in one day needs prior SAFE filing (S02 Art. 30). Entry to or exit from the U.S. above USD 10,000 must be reported to CBP (S16).

Documents

ID for the withdrawal; FinCEN Form 105 for the U.S. leg (S16).

PRC tax

As M01; carrying cash does not change the tax on the income.

U.S. side

Report above USD 10,000 at one time; failure risks forfeiture and seizure (S16).

Step by step

  1. If withdrawing more than USD 10,000 equivalent in a day, file with SAFE in advance (S02 Art. 30).
  2. Convert to notes at the bank; the notes rate on the board equals the exchange rate (S07).
  3. Declare at the U.S. border above USD 10,000 using FinCEN Form 105 (S16).
  4. Do not split the amount among travellers to stay under the line (see U05).

Pros

  • No wire fee; only the exchange margin.
  • Settles instantly.

Cons

  • 40 trips for USD 400,000 at USD 10,000 a trip, with the associated risk and cost unpriced.
  • Physical loss and seizure risk.

Sources: S02 S07 S16

M10 · Remit from an existing foreign-currency savings account (no new conversion) conditional

All-in at $100,000: $92.70 ($92.70 fee + $0.00 margin) — all 40 tiers

Who can use it

Someone whose money is already USD (or another foreign currency) inside a Chinese bank.

Limit

Overseas individual: remit from the FX savings account on ID (S02 Art. 15(1)). Domestic individual: up to USD 50,000 a day on ID, above that with authenticity documents (S02 Art. 14).

Documents

Valid ID; authenticity documents above the daily line for a domestic individual.

PRC tax

None on the remittance itself; the tax on the income is unchanged.

U.S. side

As M01.

Step by step

  1. Confirm the balance is already foreign currency, not RMB.
  2. Remit from the FX savings account with ID (S02 Art. 15(1)).
  3. Pay the wire fee; there is no new exchange margin because no new conversion happens.

Pros

  • Cheapest published arithmetic: the wire fee only, no exchange margin.
  • No conversion decision.

Cons

  • Only works if the money is already in foreign currency — the conversion already happened at some earlier rate.
  • Bank may still ask for source documents.

Sources: S02 S09

M12 · Keep the RMB in China and convert later lawful

not published ⚑F23

Who can use it

Anyone not forced to move the money now.

Limit

Not applicable.

Documents

None.

PRC tax

The tax on the income is unchanged by waiting.

U.S. side

U.S. reporting follows the balance, not the transfer (S10).

Step by step

  1. Keep the balance where it is.
  2. Continue to file FBAR and any other U.S. reports the balance triggers (S10).
  3. Re-read the PRC rules before acting; they change (S04 and S02 both carry amendment notes).

Pros

  • Costs nothing today.
  • Keeps every route open.

Cons

  • Currency exposure is unpriced and can exceed every fee discussed here.
  • Rules can tighten; the balance stays exposed to PRC enforcement.
