A verified, source-linked answer to one question: a US citizen has CNY stuck in a mainland bank account and needs USD. What are the lawful routes, what does each cost at every USD 10,000 step up to USD 400,000, and what is the real penalty exposure?

The short answer

  1. There is no tax on the conversion itself. None of the 31 primary pages in the ledger imposes a tax on exchanging CNY for USD. The "considerable tax" is almost always one of three things: the bank's FX spread, PRC income tax that was already due on the income, or US tax on the currency gain.
  2. The real cost of a lawful conversion is small. At the board rates read on 11 November 2026 the cheapest priced route costs $49.79 on USD 10,000 and $898.75 on USD 400,000 — about 0.2247%.
  3. The expensive part is the paperwork ceiling, not the fee. The USD 50,000 annual facilitation quota [S08] is the binding constraint for a domestic individual; documented purchases and the emigrant transfer are the routes that go beyond it.
  4. Penalties dwarf costs. A single willful FBAR violation is USD 165,353 [S22]; an unlawful conversion treated as evasion is 30% of the amount, up to 100% in serious cases [S09]. At USD 400,000 that is a USD 120,000 exposure against a USD 899 lawful cost.
