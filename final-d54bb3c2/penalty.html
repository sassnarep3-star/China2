The conversion penalty, broken into its actual parts

"Penalty" in this problem means at least nine different things. They are separated here so each can be sized and, where possible, avoided. Layers P1, P2 and P6 are unavoidable in any lawful conversion. P4 is not a conversion cost at all. P7 and P8 are the layers that can ruin the outcome.

What the table says

  • At USD 400,000 the lawful conversion costs $898.75. The same amount treated as evasion carries a floor fine of $120,000.00 and a serious-case maximum of $400,000.00 [S09 Art.39].
  • One willful FBAR year ($165,353.00) costs more than the lawful conversion of USD 400,000 by a factor of 184 [S22]. A single non-willful year is $16,536.00.
  • The US currency gain of about 6.6082% is real but small next to either penalty layer; it is ordinary income, and only the first USD 200 of a personal-transaction gain is excluded [S27].
  • PRC income tax (P4) is not caused by converting. It is due on the income whether or not the money moves, with a 0.05% per day surcharge if late [S20, S31].

Worked example

Convert USD 400,000 through BOC. Spread 0.2094% and a capped fee of CNY 410 give a lawful cost of about USD 898.75, i.e. 0.225%. The same USD 400,000 routed through an unlawful channel and treated as evasion under Article 39 would face a fine of USD 120,000, or up to USD 400,000 in the serious band. One missed willful FBAR for one year would be USD 165,353, which is 184 times the lawful conversion cost.

Three ways the penalty gets triggered by accident

  1. Quota splitting. Using relatives' USD 50,000 quotas to move a large balance is expressly prohibited; it puts you on a watch list for two years [S15].
  2. Mislabeling. Describing capital as a service payment does not survive the tax filing review that applies at USD 50,000 and above [S19], and is treated as evasion [S09].
  3. Silence on the US side. Not filing the FBAR or Form 8938 does not become cheaper by moving the money; the penalty attaches to the account years [S21, S23, S22].
