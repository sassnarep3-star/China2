US currency gain and $200 exceptionThe 2024 US Code edition, section 988(e), distinguishes personal transactions from the preceding section 988 provisions. Its exclusion for exchange-rate gain on a personal disposition does not apply when gain otherwise recognized on that transaction exceeds $200. The example in the guide is a hypothetical, not this account’s tax or actual basis.Have a US cross-border tax professional determine functional currency, acquisition dates, basis, transaction classification and applicable gain treatment. Do not assume every conversion has ordinary gain or that a $200 allowance always applies.US Code §988(c), (e), 2024 edition

How to use the existing costs

The guide's mobile cost tables and route-by-origin playbook cover USD 10,000 through USD 400,000 in USD 10,000 increments. Their computed spread is a comparison to a stated reference rate, not an additional fee paid separately. They are dated scenarios, not quotes; blank or omitted taxes, beneficiary fees, intermediaries and route-specific charges must not be read as zero. Each table row links to its source, but a source link alone cannot establish individual eligibility. BOC itself states its board is for reference only: BOC board.

Decision-ready checklist

  1. Privately establish the legal owner, source, dates and composition of the balance; retrieve statements, contracts and tax records.
  2. Ask the holding bank for its precise classification, written document list and the legal basis and calculation for any tax it says is outstanding. Distinguish actual tax from proof of past tax payment.
  3. Obtain same-time written quotes for eligible routes: CNY debited, USD purchased, all sending, intermediary and receiving fees, and expected USD delivered.
  4. Review PRC tax history and US income, currency-gain and foreign-account reporting with qualified advisers before any transfer or corrective filing.
  5. Keep confirmations and tax records privately. Avoid false documentation, informal swaps or quota splitting to evade controls. SAFE on individual FX compliance.
