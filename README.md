# China Money: CNY-to-USD research dashboard

A static, source-led research brief on lawful ways to convert and move funds from China, the distinction between tax and FX administration, relevant U.S. reporting, and issues that need a case-specific review.

## Executive summary

The likely first route to investigate is a direct, documented transaction with the bank holding the CNY account. Ask its FX/compliance team to classify the account holder and each significant deposit, state the documents required, and provide an itemized net-USD quote. SAFE's annual personal FX convenience amount is a currency-administration facility, not a tax rate; access to the purchase amount depends on the transaction and account holder's classification. Chinese rules provide separate source-based processes for certain non-domestic individuals' lawful RMB income, qualifying property-sale proceeds, emigration asset transfers, and inheritance. None is a universal route for any account balance.

No facts were provided about the source or size of the balance, the account holder's Chinese FX status, prior taxes, or the alleged charge. Consequently, the research does not calculate a personal tax bill or bank fee. It flags those missing facts rather than guessing. U.S. citizens generally report worldwide income; FBAR and FATCA reporting may also apply independently of whether funds are transferred.

**Do not split conversions among people, borrow FX quotas, fabricate invoices, use informal exchangers, or misstate payment purpose.** These are not lawful tax-saving alternatives and may create foreign-exchange, AML, fraud, loss, and U.S. reporting problems.

## Running locally

Open `index.html` in a browser, or serve this directory with any static HTTP server. There is no package manager or third-party runtime dependency. The repository's existing GitHub Pages configuration publishes the root of `main` (legacy branch-based Pages); merging these static files to `main` updates the live site. `.nojekyll` disables unnecessary Jekyll processing.

## Research and limitations

Reviewed November 7, 2026. The dashboard links each table row to official sources from China's State Administration of Foreign Exchange, People's Bank of China, State Taxation Administration and General Administration of Customs, and the U.S. IRS/FinCEN. Older primary sources are identified where later amendments or current confirmation matter. Chinese-language sources are labeled. Unverified fees, exchange quotes and personal liabilities are left blank or flagged. See the [methodology and source list](sources.html).

This is general research, not legal, tax, accounting, or financial advice, and it cannot guarantee bank or regulator approval. Policies, bank practice and forms change; obtain written instructions from the account-holding bank/SAFE and advice from qualified China/U.S. tax professionals before acting.
