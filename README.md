## The headline finding

China's **USD 50,000 per person per year** individual FX quota is a **documentation
threshold, not a legal ceiling**. SAFE states explicitly that conversion beyond the quota is
permitted with valid ID plus documents proving the transaction is genuine. Separately, SAFE
confirms that outward remittance of company profits is a **current-account** item handled
directly at the bank with evidencing documents and **no quota**.

**If the money is personal, lawfully-earned, and already taxed in China, converting it carries
no Chinese income tax at all.** The only costs are the bank's FX spread and, on the US side,
any currency gain above USD 200 under 26 U.S.C. § 988(e).

The "considerable amount of taxes" comes from one of three specific places:

| Source of the tax | Size | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Company-level EIT + dividend WHT on untaxed WFOE profit | **32.5%** | Claim the treaty rate (10%, not the 20% statutory rate) and the US foreign tax credit |
| US currency gain under § 988(e) on converting appreciated CNY | Up to 100% of the gain | Model it before converting; above USD 200 the *entire* gain is ordinary income with no Chinese credit |
| Chinese VAT on service fees and royalties | *not verified* | Prefer routes with no VAT — VAT is not creditable against US tax |

that could not be confirmed from an official source are left **blank** and flagged on the
[Flagged for Review](docs/flagged.html) page. No value was inferred, estimated, or back-filled
from memory.
