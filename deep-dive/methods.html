A1 · Direct bank remittance of personal, already-taxed CNY

Convert CNY at a mainland bank and wire USD to a US account under the current account, presenting documents proving the money was lawfully obtained and already taxed.

How it works

The bank sells you USD — within the USD 50,000 annual quota on ID alone. Beyond the quota, you present ID plus documents proving the transaction is genuine.

China tax

0% on the principal if the money was already taxed in China when earned. No Chinese tax on the conversion itself.

US tax

Only the § 988(e) currency gain, if the CNY has appreciated against your acquisition cost by more than USD 200.

Pros

  • Lowest total fee leakage of the lawful routes compared here — conditional on documentation, account status and eligibility; not a personalized ranking (integration review 2026-11-10).
  • No additional Chinese tax.
  • No approval process; money arrives in days.
  • Keeps the FBAR / Form 8938 history clean.

Cons

  • Requires the bank to accept your proof of lawful source.
  • The USD 50,000 quota is a documentation threshold — a single large transfer needs a strong paper trail.
  • The bank may ask for tax records going back years.

A2 · Remittance of after-tax salary while still employed in China

If you are a foreign national employed in China, your after-tax salary can be converted and remitted. IIT was withheld at source by the employer.

A3 · Outward remittance of WFOE dividends

A wholly foreign-owned enterprise in China pays EIT, appropriates the statutory surplus reserve, then remits the dividend. SAFE records outward profit remittance under the current account: handled directly at the bank with evidencing documents and no FX quota.

StepRateAmount on 100Source
Profit in China (starting assumption)—100.00EIT Law Art. 4
Enterprise Income Tax25%(25.00)EIT Law Art. 4
Statutory surplus reserve appropriated from after-tax profit Secondary10%(7.50)HKEX filing · translation 1 · translation 2

If the 20% statutory WHT applied instead of the treaty rate40.0% total leakage60.00Ann. 61/2018

Claiming the treaty rather than letting the 20% domestic rate apply saves 7.5 points of leakage — USD 7,500 on a USD 100,000 dividend.

Pros

  • Fully lawful and routine.
  • No FX approval or quota — the constraint is tax, not permission.
  • The treaty halves the dividend WHT.
  • The foreign tax credit largely eliminates US double tax.

Cons

  • 32.5% combined leakage is the "considerable tax" the question refers to.
  • Requires a real, profitable, audited WFOE.
  • 10% statutory surplus reserve must be appropriated first.
  • Tax recordal required for single outbound payments over USD 50,000.

A4 · Intra-group service fees

The WFOE pays a US affiliate for services actually rendered. EIT applies only if the US affiliate has a permanent establishment in China — and the treaty limits that.

Pros

  • Moves money without the 32.5% dividend leakage.
  • Deductible to the WFOE.

Cons

  • VAT is not creditable against US tax, so it is a real cost.
  • Transfer-pricing scrutiny if the services are not arm's-length.
  • The service must be genuine and documented.

Domestic statutory rates: SAT Announcement [2018] No. 61. Treaty caps: official IRS treaty text.

Pros

  • The treaty caps withholding at 10% — half the domestic rate.
  • The withholding is creditable in the US on Form 1116.

Cons

  • Requires genuine IP or a genuine loan.
  • Thin-capitalisation and transfer-pricing rules apply to interest.
  • VAT on royalties is not creditable in the US.

A7 · Capital reduction or liquidation of the Chinese entity

Reduce registered capital or wind up the WFOE and remit the residual to the shareholder. Recorded under the current account, handled at the bank with evidencing documents.

DetailValueSource
Outward remittance is a current-account item, no quotaYesSAFE
EIT on the gain over the original investment25%EIT Law Art. 4
Creditor notice, audit and SAFE registration requirementsnot verifiedSAFE · SAFE catalogue

Pros

  • Recovers capital without dividend WHT on the capital element.
  • Lower total leakage than a full dividend where the entity is being closed.

Cons

  • Slow — months.
  • Requires shareholder resolution and creditor notice.
  • EIT applies to the gain element.

A8 · Direct payment of overseas expenses from the CNY account

Pay foreign suppliers, tuition, insurance, mortgages and credit cards directly out of the Chinese account instead of moving the money. The only route that is tax-free on both sides.

DetailValueSource
China tax0% — no income is created by paying an expenseSAFE
US tax0% — no income, no currency gainIRS
Evidencing documents required at the bankYesSAFE
Bank document-review threshold for service-trade paymentsnot verified — secondary sources cite USD 50,000SAFE · SAFE catalogue

Pros

  • No tax event from spending per se (integration review 2026-11-10: prior wording “zero tax on both sides” was categorical — PRC tax status of the money was fixed when it was earned, and U.S. reporting duties continue while the balance sits in China).
  • Converts CNY into the economic benefit of the payment without creating a new taxable income event.
  • No FX gain (no conversion), but FBAR/Form 8938 filing duties on the account balance continue.

Cons

  • Only works to the extent you have genuine overseas obligations.
  • Requires contracts and invoices.
  • Cannot be used for pure investment.

A12 · Offshore CNH via Hong Kong

Convert CNY to HKD/USD in the offshore CNH market in Hong Kong and move it from there.

DetailValueSource
China tax0%PBOC
Penalty for unauthorised or disguised FX tradingWarning, confiscation of gains, fine up to 30% (30–100% if serious), criminal liabilityFX Regs art. 45

Verdict: offshore rates can be better, but this does not solve the export problem — it relocates it. The CNY still has to lawfully leave the mainland, and adding an offshore layer adds cost and compliance surface. High legal risk if done outside the banking system.
