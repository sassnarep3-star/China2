U.S. currency disposition · research update 2026-11-08

Know the tax before moving the money

A mobile-friendly, source-linked workbook for the U.S. tax layer of moving CNY to USD. Unknown values stay blank.

Most important finding.

A CNY→USD transfer amount alone does not determine U.S. tax. Separate the underlying income, any currency-disposition gain, execution fees and reporting penalties. For a qualifying personal currency disposition, exchange-rate gain up to $200 on the transaction is not recognized; above $200, IRS Publication 525 says report the gain as a capital gain. Business/investment transactions can follow different ordinary-income rules. S01 · S02

Executive summary

  1. Do not assume “conversion tax = 0%.” The U.S. may tax a currency-disposition gain or the underlying income; the amount depends on basis, use, year and filing facts. S01 · S02 · S14
  2. Reconstruct basis before converting. Identify each CNY lot, acquisition date, USD basis and the disposition rate. Current balance is not basis. S04 · S06 · S03
  3. Use the $200 rule narrowly. It is a per-transaction personal-gain cliff, not an annual transfer allowance and not a shelter for the whole balance. S01 · S02
  4. Compare all-in execution quotes separately. The Treasury rate used here is an official reporting reference, not a bank quote. Fees and spread remain blank. S12 · S13 · S09
  5. Check reporting before moving large sums. FBAR and Form 8938 are separate from income tax and depend on aggregate accounts/assets and residence/filing status. S10 · S11

Reference rate used in the workbook

The workbook computes a labeled CNY reference amount using Treasury’s September 30, 2026 reporting rate: 6.7050 CNY per USD. This is not an executable conversion quote, not necessarily the correct tax transaction-date rate, and the PDF excludes online amendments. S12 · S13
