The penalty

There is no conversion tax. There are six other layers.

The phrase "conversion penalty" bundles things that behave completely differently: a tax on income, a paperwork gate, a tax on a transfer, a commercial spread, reporting duties, and fines. Breaking them apart is the whole analysis.

The same layers in dollars

This is where the "$400,000 problem" becomes concrete. Layer 1 is zero at every amount. Layer 2 is the only large lawful number, and it depends on the source of the money. Layer 8 is the only number that can legally exceed the balance itself.

The arithmetic of the trap. At $400,000, an untaxed labour balance carries about $148,896 of PRC IIT — 37.2% of the amount. The prohibited routes are priced at up to 30% ($120,000) or, if serious, 30%–100% ($400,000). A lawful conversion costs $0 in tax plus a commercial spread nobody will publish. Paying a 30% fine to avoid a 3%–45% tax that you owe anyway is not a saving; it is the tax plus a penalty.

Why the bank refuses without a certificate

Article 13(1) of the implementing rules requires identity documents plus documents proving the amount and the source, expressly including tax vouchers. Article 7 of the 2006 rules prohibits fabricated vouchers and splitting. The bank is not taxing you at the counter; it is checking that the tax was paid where the law puts it — on the income.

What is genuinely unknown

  • The commercial spread and fees (L6) — no official publication exists (F01).
  • Whether the income was already taxed (F05) — decides whether L2 applies at all.
  • The cost basis on an asset transfer (F06) — decides 20% of what.
  • Whether prior US filings were complete (F07) — can outweigh every other number.
