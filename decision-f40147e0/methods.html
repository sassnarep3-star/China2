Bank conversion + SWIFT wire — Bank of China

Lawful

Authorised bank channel

Sell RMB for USD on the bank's published board and send a telegraphic transfer to your own U.S. account. This is the reference route: fully published price, published fee formula, published statutory basis.

$282.23 at $100,000 · 0.28%

Lawful where the RMB is documented current-account income; the bank must accept the source-of-funds file.

Pros

  • Published rate and published fee formula, so the cost is estimable before you commit.
  • No third-party intermediary, no counterparty risk beyond the banking system.
  • Statutory basis is explicit for documented current-account income.
  • Foreign tax credit and §988 treatment are ordinary, documented outcomes.

Cons

  • Requires a complete, consistent paper trail; the bank can decline an incomplete file.
  • The fee is capped but the spread is not, so the real cost is the bank's markup on the day.
  • Correspondent-bank deductions are not published (F05).
  • A tax recordal step may be required at USD 50,000 and above (S13).
Step by step
  1. Confirm which rulebook you fall under: a foreign national continuously resident in China for one year or more is a 'domestic individual' under Art. 52(2) of Decree 532, while the conversion of a non-domestic individual's China income is governed by Huifa [2007] No. 1 Art. 13 and Huifa [2020] No. 14 Art. 59 (S07, S09, S10).
  2. Assemble the source-of-funds file: passport, the bank card/account, employment contract or service agreement, payslips, and the China tax record (纳税记录 / 完税证明) covering the years the balance was earned (S10).
  3. Ask the branch whether a tax recordal (税务备案) is required for the payment; a single outward payment of USD 50,000 or more for listed service-trade and income items is within Announcement [2013] No. 40 (S13).
  4. Execute the FX sale at the counter or in the app; the trade rate is the bank's live selling rate, not the board snapshot used in these tables (S01).
  5. Send the wire with the beneficiary name exactly matching your U.S. account. Keep the FX slip and the wire confirmation.
  6. Report the account on FinCEN Form 114 if the aggregate exceeded USD 10,000 at any point in the year, and check Form 8938 thresholds (S20, S22).

Bank conversion + SWIFT wire — China Merchants Bank

Lawful

Authorised bank channel

Same route through China Merchants Bank. Higher fee ceiling (CNY 1,000 versus CNY 260) but a competitive board.

$356.62 at $100,000 · 0.36%

Same legal basis as M01; CMB's fee cap is higher and its published board is one day behind at retrieval.

Pros

  • Published 0.1% fee with a stated cap and a stated cable charge.
  • Explicit SHA/BEN/OUR disclosure, which most banks do not publish.
  • Board readable without a browser session.

Cons

  • Fee ceiling is CNY 1,000 — four times BOC's cap.
  • The OUR option can cost more than the published prepayment if the intermediary asks for more.
  • CMB's board carried the previous day's date at retrieval (F02).
Step by step
  1. Same document set as M01 (S10).
  2. Check whether the funding account holds RMB cash or RMB remittance funds: CMB charges 0.8% to convert USD cash into remittance funds, but nothing when the account already holds remittance funds (S04).
  3. Choose the charging method deliberately: SHA (you pay your bank's fees), BEN (beneficiary pays) or OUR (you prepay the equivalent of USD 25 and may be asked for more) (S04).
  4. Execute the sale, then the wire; keep the FX slip and the SWIFT confirmation.
  5. Complete the U.S. reporting checks in M01.

HSBC China outward telegraphic transfer (Premier / Advance)

Conditional

Authorised bank channel

HSBC China's published tariff and board, priced for a Premier/Advance customer. Fee is lower in the middle range than the peers; the spread is the widest of the three readable banks.

$565.63 at $100,000 · 0.57%

Lawful, but the Premier/Advance service tier and its eligibility must be held; the Board's latest tariff charges a wider spread than the mainland peers read here.

Pros

  • Published tariff effective 31 October 2026, including the cable charge.
  • Lowest published fee cap of the three readable banks (CNY 500).
  • Pay Exact makes the receiving amount deterministic for a fixed extra fee.

Cons

  • Widest measured spread: about 0.47% versus a 6.6764 reference mid, roughly double BOC's.
  • Tier requirements mean the price is not available to everyone.
  • Pay Exact is an extra 0.2% at USD 10,000 and is not refundable if the transfer fails.
Step by step
  1. Confirm the account tier and whether the monthly relationship-balance condition is met (S06).
  2. Assemble the same source-of-funds file (S10).
  3. Decide whether to buy 'Pay Exact': it adds CNY 200 so the beneficiary receives the full instructed amount (S06).
  4. Execute the sale and the transfer; retain the tariff reference and the SWIFT confirmation.
  5. Complete the U.S. reporting checks in M01.

Foreign-currency cash carry (bank cash purchase + hand carry)

Conditional

Cash channel

Buy USD cash at the bank and carry it out. The published cost is only the spread, but the legal ceiling is USD 5,000 per trip without a permit and USD 10,000 with one.

Priced only below its published ceiling

Lawful only within the cash-carry limits; unusable for a six-figure balance without repeated trips, each of which is separately regulated.

Pros

  • No wire fee at all.
  • The bank's cash selling price matched its remittance selling price on the date read, so the spread cost is the same as M01.
  • Statutory limits are explicit and easy to check.

Cons

  • Cannot move USD 400,000 this way: 40 permitted trips at USD 10,000 would each need a permit and a border crossing.
  • Physical loss, theft and seizure risk; CBP reporting is mandatory above USD 10,000.
  • Repeated same-day and short-interval trips are capped at USD 500 and USD 1,000 (S16).
  • Declared cash is visible to both customs authorities.
Step by step
  1. Buy USD cash at a bank; BOC's board shows the same selling price for cash and remittance on the date read (S01).
  2. Up to USD 5,000 equivalent: no carrying permit needed (S16).
  3. USD 5,000-10,000: obtain a 《携带外汇出境许可证》 from the bank; the bank may only issue up to the deposit or purchase amount (S16).
  4. Above USD 10,000: in principle not permitted; only listed special cases can apply to SAFE (S16).
  5. On arrival in the United States, file FinCEN Form 105 for more than USD 10,000 (S17).
  6. Deposit the cash: U.S. banks may apply their own cash-deposit rules and ask for source documentation.

Wise (transfer from a China bank app or Alipay)

Conditional

Licensed payment provider

Wise routes the payment through a PBOC-licensed partner and applies its own limit equal to after-tax salary. Its CNY fee is not published as a table, so no all-in figure is stated.

No published price — left blank

Published caps: CNY 300,000 per transfer from a China bank app; CNY 50,000 per transfer from Alipay; Overall limit = salary earned in China minus tax paid in China

Available only to a non-mainland-Chinese customer who worked in China and holds a China tax record showing tax paid within the last three years.

Pros

  • Publishes its eligibility rules and limits, so a reader can rule themselves in or out quickly.
  • Runs through a PBOC-licensed partner, so it is not an underground channel.
  • Handles the FX and the payment in one instruction.

Cons

  • The fee and the applied rate are only shown inside the transfer flow, so no pre-computed all-in cost can be published (F07).
  • The limit is after-tax salary, which may be far below the account balance.
  • Only a non-mainland-Chinese customer qualifies, and only for an account the sender owns.
  • Tax records older than three years are not accepted.
Step by step
  1. Check the eligibility list on Wise's own help page: not mainland Chinese; has worked in China; valid China tax record showing tax paid in the last three years (S24).
  2. Create the transfer and complete verification; Wise says this takes 1-2 working days the first time (S24).
  3. The recipient must be an account you own outside China, or your Wise account (S24).
  4. Observe the single-transfer caps: CNY 300,000 from the bank app, CNY 50,000 from Alipay (S24).
  5. Keep the tax records: Wise may request new records when the limit is reached (S24).
  6. Complete the U.S. reporting checks in M01.

Annual USD 50,000 quota purchase (domestic individual)

Conditional

Quota channel

The familiar USD 50,000 figure is an annual aggregate quota, not a tax and not a price. Priced here on BOC's tariff, it is the same bank route with a hard annual ceiling.

Priced only below its published ceiling

The quota is a documented annual aggregate for an individual's FX settlement and a domestic individual's FX purchase. Whether a U.S. citizen falls inside it depends on classification under Decree 532 Art. 52(2) and must be confirmed with the bank.

Pros

  • Simple documentation inside the quota.
  • Same published bank pricing as M01.

Cons

  • Caps at USD 50,000 per person per year, so it cannot clear a USD 400,000 balance in one year.
  • Applicability to a foreign national is a classification question, not a certainty (F12).
  • Using relatives' quotas to move your own money is the prohibited 'splitting into small batches' pattern (S08 Art. 7).
Step by step
  1. Establish the classification first; the quota does not apply identically to a non-domestic individual (S07 Art. 52(2), S09 Art. 2, Art. 13).
  2. Within the quota, present valid ID at the bank (S09 Art. 2).
  3. Above the quota, a current-account payment proceeds on documents under Art. 12 or Art. 13, or a capital-account payment under the capital rules (S09).
  4. Keep the bank's quota record: the quota is an annual aggregate per person, not per transaction.
  5. Do not split a larger amount across people or days to stay under it (S08 Art. 7).

Emigration property transfer (SAFE-approved lump sum)

Conditional

Capital-account approval

A one-off capital-account transfer of pre-emigration domestic assets, approved by SAFE and then remitted by the bank in one sum or in batches.

$282.23 at $100,000 · 0.28%

Requires emigration and SAFE approval at the branch where the original permanent residence was registered, with the ceiling set by the approval document.

Pros

  • Designed for exactly this fact pattern: a large lawful balance leaving after emigration.
  • The ceiling is set by an approval document rather than a generic annual quota.
  • Batching within one approval is expressly allowed.

Cons

  • Requires the emigration facts and the correct SAFE branch; not a walk-in bank product.
  • The application document list is not restated here because the underlying property-transfer measures were not re-read (F10).
  • SAFE approval is discretionary and can take time.
Step by step
  1. Confirm the pre-emigration asset and emigration facts that the regime requires (S09 Art. 20, S11).
  2. Apply to the SAFE branch in the place of the original registered permanent residence (S11).
  3. Obtain the approval document, which sets the amount that may be remitted (S11).
  4. Take the approval to the bank; it may remit in one lump sum or in several batches within that limit (S11).
  5. Complete the U.S. reporting and §988 checks (S19, S20).

Entrusted conversion by a direct relative

Conditional

Quota channel

The rules do allow a direct relative to handle a quota-level conversion for you. They do not allow a crowd of friends to each use their quota for your money.

Priced only below its published ceiling

Permitted for quota-level settlement and purchase by a direct relative (parent, child, spouse) with identity, authorisation and relationship documents; above the quota the documents behind the transaction are also required.

Pros

  • Explicitly permitted for direct relatives.
  • Useful where the account holder cannot attend a branch.

Cons

  • Limited to parent, child or spouse — not friends, not cousins.
  • Still capped by the quota.
  • Easy to drift into the prohibited splitting pattern if repeated.
Step by step
  1. Confirm the relationship is within the definition: parent, child or spouse (S09 Art. 38).
  2. Provide both parties' ID, your written authorisation and the relationship document (S09 Art. 38).
  3. Handle the conversion within the annual quota; above it, add the transaction documents (S09 Art. 4, Art. 12).
  4. Do not extend the arrangement to non-relatives or repeat it to defeat the quota (S08 Art. 7).

Unverified

Offshore RMB

Send RMB rather than USD, then convert offshore. The appeal is avoiding the onshore USD purchase; the problem is that no published fee was found and the offshore spread is a second cost.

No published price — left blank

A cross-border RMB current-account remittance to an account you hold offshore is contemplated by the rules, but no bank read this pass publishes the fee for it.

Pros

  • Two spreads do not necessarily cost more than one if the offshore market is tighter.

Cons

  2. Obtain the sending and receiving banks' fee schedules and the applicable CNH spread in writing.

Unlawful

Prohibited channel

Listed because it is the route most often suggested online. It is prohibited in China, and the statutory penalty attaches to the amount moved.

No published price — left blank

Converting between legal tender and virtual currency inside China is prohibited as illegal financial activity; no instructions are given.

Pros

  • None that survive contact with the rules: the activity is characterised as illegal financial activity in China.

  • Illegal financial activity under Yinfa [2026] No. 42 (S15).

  • Counterparty and seizure risk, and a U.S. tax basis problem on top.

  1. No execution steps are provided. The route is prohibited under Yinfa [2026] No. 42 (S15), which repealed the 2021 notice.
