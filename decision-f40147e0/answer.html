The answer

Least tax penalties, lawfully

The brief asks for the cheapest way out with the least tax penalty. The honest answer has an order: fix the filing position first, document the source second, choose the cheapest published bank third, and never take a route whose statutory fine is a percentage of the principal.

The sequence that minimises the lawful total

  1. Establish the classification before anything else. A foreign national continuously resident in China for a year or more is a “domestic individual” under Decree 532 Art. 52(2); a non-domestic individual’s China-source income follows a separate route. The two rulebooks produce different document sets and different ceilings. S07 · safe.gov.cn S09 · safe.gov.cn
  2. Pull the China tax record for every year the balance was earned. Banks and licensed providers ask for it, and Wise will not send at all without tax paid in the last three years. S10 · safe.gov.cn S24 · wise.com
  3. Fix the U.S. filing position before the money moves. An FBAR is due for any year the aggregate exceeded USD 10,000; Form 8938 depends on where you live. Late filing is a separate, cheaper problem than the transfer. S20 · irs.gov S22 · irs.gov
  4. Establish the RMB cost basis. §988 gain is measured from the booking date to the payment date, so the acquisition rate decides whether the conversion produces ordinary income. S19 · govinfo.gov S18 · irs.gov
  5. Get two bank quotes in writing, then transfer in as few wires as the file allows. The fee is capped, the spread is not; batching removes repeated caps but does not change the spread. S02 · boc.cn S04 · cmbchina.com
  6. Keep the paper. FX slip, wire confirmation, tax record and the source documents are the audit file for both jurisdictions.

The one-line answer. The least-tax lawful path is a documented bank conversion at the bank with the tightest published spread, executed in as few wires as the file allows, with the FBAR/8938 position fixed first and the RMB cost basis recorded. On today’s published numbers that is roughly $54.55 at USD 10,000 and $944.70 at USD 400,000, before unknown correspondent deductions — about 0.55% down to 0.24%.
