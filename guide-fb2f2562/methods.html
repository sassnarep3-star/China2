M01 · Bank of China: buy USD online, then online cross-border wire to your own US account

Priced from published inputs · Speed: Usually days; not stated by the bank page. · Cost table

Pros
  • Lowest published bank fee read today: 0.1%, min CNY 50, max CNY 260, plus CNY 150 cable — at most CNY 410 per wire [S03].
  • Lowest published exchange spread read today: BOC sells USD at 668.86 and buys at 666.06 per 100, a 0.21% gap to the midpoint [S01].
  • Clean paper trail on both sides: a wire from your own named account is the route the rules describe [S08 Art. 13, S13 Art. 11].
Cons
  • The bank applies the documentary test first: a foreigner buys USD for lawful RMB income with ID plus income/tax proof [S08 Art. 13, S10].
  • Intermediary and receiving-bank charges are not published — blank in every cost row (flag F-02).
  • The board rate moves during the day; the rate you get is the one at execution [S01].
Step by step
  1. Collect: passport, employment contract or other proof of where the RMB came from, and PRC tax records (完税证明 / IIT records) [S08 Art. 13, S10].
  2. Log in to BOC online/mobile banking (or visit a branch if online purchase is refused for your ID type) and request a USD purchase (购汇) for the documented purpose.
  3. Submit the documents the bank asks for; the bank checks genuineness before selling USD [S10].
  4. Once USD sits in your BOC USD account, send a cross-border wire (跨境汇款) to a US account in your own name; fee 0.1% (CNY 50–260) + CNY 150 cable [S03].
  5. Keep the purchase slip, wire confirmation and tax records together — they support both the PRC file and your US records.
  6. On the US side: report the PRC account on FinCEN 114 for any year the total of your foreign accounts exceeded USD 10,000 [S21]; check Form 8938 thresholds [S23]; work out any FX gain above USD 200 per conversion [S27].

M02 · HSBC China: buy USD, then outward telegraphic transfer to your own US account

Priced from published inputs · Speed: Not stated in the tariff. · Cost table

Pros
  • Published fee schedule: 0.1%, min CNY 100, max CNY 500, plus CNY 120 cable — at most CNY 620 [S04].
  • English-language bank with US affiliates; same documentary route as M01 [S08 Art. 13].
Cons
  • Wider spread than BOC today: HSBC sells USD at 6.7053746 and buys at 6.6386339, about 0.50% to the midpoint [S02].
  • The tariff header carries two dates (31 Oct 2026 and 2024年9月12日) — confirm the current version with the bank (flag F-06) [S04].
  • Intermediary/receiving fees not published (flag F-02).
Step by step
  1. Prepare the same documents as M01 (ID, source-of-funds and tax proof) [S08 Art. 13, S10].
  2. Buy USD in HSBC China online banking or at a branch; check the board rate on the day [S02].
  3. Send an outward TT to your own US account; fee 0.1% (CNY 100–500) + CNY 120 cable [S04].
  4. File the US reports as in M01 [S21, S23, S27].

Sources: S02S04S08S10S21S23S27

M03 · HSBC China Premier: Global Transfer to your own HSBC account abroad

Priced from published inputs · Speed: Not stated in the tariff. · Cost table

Pros
  • Transfer fee for Premier: free [S04 2.2.6.1].
  • Same documentary route as M01/M02 [S08 Art. 13].
Cons
  • Premier carries a CNY 300 monthly fee unless your relationship balance is at least CNY 500,000 [S04] — not included in the cost rows.
  • New Advance accounts are restricted since 18 Mar 2026 (Advance Global Transfer is USD 20) [S04].
  • Same 0.50% HSBC spread as M02 [S02]; receiving-side HSBC US terms were not read (flag F-16).
Step by step
  1. Confirm you hold Premier status in China and an HSBC account in the US in the same name [S04].
  2. Buy USD with source-of-funds documents [S08 Art. 13, S10].
  3. Use Global Transfer to your own HSBC account; Premier fee: free [S04].
  4. File the US reports as in M01 [S21, S23, S27].

Sources: S02S04S08S10S21S23S27

M04 · Wise: send CNY out of China to your own account

Partly priced — some inputs unpublished · Speed: Not read. · Cost table

Pros
  • Designed for foreigners' after-tax China salary [S05].
  • Money can go only to your own account, which keeps the trail clean [S05].
Cons
  • Only for income with a recent China tax record — not for old savings, property sale proceeds or gifts [S05].
  • Total is limited to roughly salary minus tax [S05].
  • Wise's CNY→USD fee and rate were not on the page read — blank in every row (flag F-01).
Step by step
  1. Check eligibility on the Wise CNY guide: non-mainland ID and a China tax record within 3 years [S05].
  2. Set up the transfer to your own USD account; pay from your China bank app (≤ CNY 300,000 each) or Alipay (≤ CNY 50,000 each) [S05].
  3. Upload the salary/tax documents Wise asks for.
  4. File the US reports as in M01 [S21, S23, S27].

Sources: S05S21S23S27

M05 · Emigrant property transfer (former PRC citizen with a cancelled hukou)

Partly priced — some inputs unpublished · Speed: SAFE: 5 working days to accept, 20 to decide [S14]. · Cost table

Pros
  • SAFE charges nothing for the approval [S14].
  • Staged remittance was abolished in 2014 — one or several remittances are allowed [S12].
  • Covers savings and property sale proceeds that a salary route cannot [S12, S14].
Cons
  • Only for emigrants: needs hukou cancellation and proof of settlement abroad [S14].
  • Needs proof of source and title and a tax certificate [S14]; unproven-source property is refused [S13 Art. 12].
  • Bank conversion and wire fees for this route were not published on the pages read — blank (flag F-05).
  • Public officials applying for more than CNY 1 million may be referred to supervisory authorities [S14].
Step by step
  1. Collect: hukou cancellation proof, foreign settlement proof (e.g. US naturalisation), proof of source and title of the property, tax certificate [S14].
  2. Apply to the SAFE office where your original hukou was registered [S12].
  3. Wait for acceptance (≤ 5 working days) and decision (≤ 20 working days) [S14].
  4. Take the single-use 《资本项目外汇业务核准件》 to the bank; buy USD and remit to your own overseas account (no cash) [S13 Art. 11, S14].
  5. File the US reports as in M01 [S21, S23, S27]; gifts or bequests from others may need Form 3520 [S24].

Sources: S12S13S14S21S23S24S27

M06 · Withdraw cash abroad with a China-issued bank card

Partly priced — some inputs unpublished · Speed: Daily, capped. · Cost table

Pros
  • No paperwork per withdrawal [S16].
  • Useful for small amounts or travel money.
Cons
  • Hard cap: RMB 100,000 a calendar year; going over suspends overseas withdrawals for this year and next [S16].
  • Lending or borrowing cards to get around the cap is prohibited [S16].
  • ATM, card-issuer and FX fees are not published on the pages read — blank (flag F-03).
Step by step
  1. Check the card works abroad and ask the issuer for its overseas withdrawal fee.
  2. Withdraw up to RMB 10,000 per card per day; keep the yearly total under RMB 100,000 [S16].
  3. Deposit in your own US account; keep statements.

Sources: S16

M07 · Carry US dollar cash out of China

Partly priced — some inputs unpublished · Speed: One trip at a time. · Cost table

Pros
  • Uses the same bank cash-sell rate as a wire at BOC (668.86 per 100 today) [S01].
Cons
  • Many trips: at USD 5,000 a trip, USD 400,000 needs 80 exits [S17].
  • A foreigner's cash withdrawal from FX savings is limited to USD 10,000 a day [S08 Art. 15].
  • Over USD 10,000 entering the US must be declared on FinCEN 105 [S28].
  • Loss, theft and bank cash-withdrawal fees (not published — flag F-04).
Step by step
  1. Buy USD cash at the bank against your documents [S08 Art. 13, Art. 15].
  2. For USD 5,000–10,000 get the bank's 携带证 (valid 30 days) [S17].
  3. Declare on arrival in the US if over USD 10,000 (FinCEN 105) [S28].
  4. Deposit in your own US account with the bank and permit paperwork.

Sources: S01S08S17S28
