Step-by-step: every identified route (R01–R18)

For each route: who qualifies, the exact steps in order, the pros, the cons, and the cost references to page 2. Unlawful routes are listed so you can recognise and refuse them — they are never cost alternatives.

R01 — Bank wire inside the USD 50,000 annual individual FX quota Lawful

Limit / headroom: USD 50,000 / person / calendar year   SAFE-1 HSBC-1

Steps

  1. Valid ID (passport/PRC ID) at any Chinese bank counter or FX app
  2. State the purpose (e.g., personal remittance)
  3. Bank buys USD for you from CNY within your remaining annual quota (SAFE system checks)
  4. Funds wired to your US account; keep the remittance advice for FBAR basis/booking

Pros

  • Simplest lawful route — ID only, no transaction documents inside the quota
  • Clean audit trail for SAFE and the IRS
  • Per-person, per-year only — $400,000 means 8 calendar years via this route alone

Cons / risks

  • Hard USD 50,000/yr ceiling
  • Authenticity review still applies; bank may ask purpose questions
  • Spread and fees as in Table A (page 2); spread unpublished

R02 — Above-quota bank wire with authenticity documents Lawful · conditional

Limit / headroom: Not capped by the quota; conditioned on documentary support   SAFE-1 SAFE-2 HSBC-2

Steps

  1. Identify the underlying genuine transaction (employment income, family maintenance, inheritance, donation, services)
  2. Collect supporting documents: tax payment certificates, employment contract + payslips, notarised relationship/donation/inheritance instruments, income proof of overseas payer
  3. Apply at bank for FX purchase/remittance above quota; bank reviews authenticity via SAFE-linked system
  4. Wire executes if documents support the full amount; keep everything for 7+ years

Pros

  • The only lawful >USD 50,000 fast route for personal balances with documentary basis
  • No annual ceiling where documents prove the transaction
  • Defence-grade documentation trail

Cons / risks

  • Bank discretion on document sufficiency; can be slow
  • Notarisation/translation costs unverified (blank)
  • Family maintenance requires notarised relationship + overseas payer’s income & tax certificates

R03 — Emigrant asset transfer (property proceeds transferred abroad on emigration) Lawful · conditional

Limit / headroom: Governed by SAFE approval document; lump sum or batches   SAFE-1

Steps

  1. Emigrate with visa/permission first (PRC no longer uses the old hukou cancellation certificate in all cities — check the SAFE approver for your locale)
  2. Apply to SAFE with identity, emigration approval, asset transfer contract & tax settlement proof
  3. SAFE issues an approval for a capped amount
  4. Remit within the approved limit; batch if needed

Pros

  • Moves property-sale proceeds that would otherwise take many quota-years
  • One approval; no quota consumption

Cons / risks

  • Emigration is a life decision, not a fee choice
  • Approval-specific cap; adviser/legal costs blank (unverified)
  • Time-consuming; tight documentary scrutiny

R04 — Inheritor asset transfer (inherited China assets) Lawful · conditional

Limit / headroom: Governed by SAFE approval; one application per decedent   SAFE-1

Steps

  1. Obtain decedent’s death certificate + your inheritance right document (notarised will / statutory inheritance agreement)
  2. Apply to SAFE with identity, decedent proof, estate contract and tax settlement where applicable
  3. SAFE issues approval capped to the estate
  4. Remit lump sum or batches

Pros

  • Lawful route for inherited balances
  • No quota consumption

Cons / risks

  • Only for genuine inheritances
  • Notarisation costs blank (unverified)
  • False documents → criminal exposure (R17)

R05 — Overseas cash withdrawals with a Chinese bank card Lawful

Limit / headroom: RMB 100,000 per person per civil year (all cards); RMB 10,000 per card per day   SAFE-3 CBP-1

Steps

  1. Withdraw cash at overseas ATMs/counters with domestic debit card — never above daily/annual caps
  2. Arrival in US: >$10,000 in currency must be reported on FinCEN Form 105

Pros

  • No quota consumption, no bank interview
  • Genuinely useful for travel float

Cons / risks

  • ≈ USD 15,028/yr ceiling at 6.6538 — hopeless for $400k
  • ATM & FX markup fees blank (unverified)
  • Excess → cards blocked in current + next 2 years

R06 — Purchase FX and carry banknotes out of China Lawful · conditional

Limit / headroom: Bounded by USD 50,000 purchase quota; >USD 10,000 cash → customs declaration forms   SAFE-1 CBP-1

Steps

  1. Within quota, buy USD banknotes at the bank (cash buy rate — worse than telegraphic)
  2. If carrying >USD 10,000 equivalent: obtain bank/customs-signed declaration forms
  3. Declare on both borders (CN exit; US entry CMIR >$10,000)

Pros

  • Works without any wire intermediary
  • No remittance fee at all

Cons / risks

  • Cash notes rate is worse than spot — spread penalty highest here
  • Physical risk; limited to USD 50k/yr
  • Two declarations required

R07 — Individual FX settlement account for genuine trade Lawful · conditional

Limit / headroom: No quota — bounded only by genuine trading documents   SAFE-1

Steps

  1. Prove you are an individual trader (contract for goods/services, customs declarations, tax filings)
  2. Open a foreign exchange settlement account (treated as institutional account)
  3. FX settlement/payment against trade documents regardless of amount

Pros

  • No annual cap; scalable to any size
  • Institutional-style processing

Cons / risks

  • Only if you genuinely trade
  • Fabricated trade documents = R16 (criminal)
  • Admin overhead; account thresholds for customs practice blank (unverified)

R08 — Corporate profit repatriation (your Chinese company) Lawful · conditional

Limit / headroom: Not governed by the individual quota; current-account   TREATY-1 EIT-1 IIT-1 SAFE-1

Steps

  1. Company declares profits & pays EIT 25% (Art. 4) and any surcharges
  2. Allocate dividends with board resolution; withhold treaty WHT (10% for US beneficial owner, Art. 9/10)
  3. File tax certificates; bank remits dividend — no quota consumed
  4. US: report dividend; claim FTC for Chinese WHT (mechanics unverified)

Pros

  • Scales to $400k+ without quota or personal drama
  • 10% WHT is capped and usually creditable in the US

Cons / risks

  • Requires a real company, real profits, audited books
  • EIT 25% + 10% WHT leak (≈32.5% of pre-EIT profit)
  • FTC mechanics for your case: blank (unverified)

R09 — After-tax employment income — foreign individuals working in China Lawful · conditional

Limit / headroom: Current-account with documents; NOT the USD 50,000 purchase quota route   SAFE-2 HSBC-2 FEIE-1 IIT-1

Steps

  1. Keep every payslip + annual IIT final settlement (汇算清缴) certificate
  2. Apply at bank to purchase FX & remit with valid ID + source materials incl. tax payment certificates (Huifa 2020 No. 14, Art. 59)
  3. US: salary taxable in US; FEIE (2026: $132,900) may exclude it if you qualify (bona fide residence / physical presence)
  4. FTC alternative if not FEIE-eligible

Pros

  • Handles exactly the “money earned in China” fact pattern
  • No quota games; authenticity proven by tax receipts
  • Direct answer to “least tax” for salary balances

Cons / risks

  • Only the after-tax salary portion qualifies — never principal/deposits
  • Wise’s parallel product requires same proof and caps per transfer
  • FEIE ≠ automatic; bona fide residence test is fact-heavy

R10 — Remit foreign exchange already held in a Chinese FX account Lawful · conditional

Limit / headroom: Quota covers FX purchases/sales — not transfers of FX you already hold, subject to authenticity review   SAFE-1

Steps

  1. If the account already holds USD/EUR: instruct the bank to outward-remit
  2. Bank authenticates the source; no purchase occurs, so no quota consumption
  3. Your CNY question doesn’t apply directly — but converting CNY→FX first consumes quota (R01)

Pros

  • Bypasses quota for pre-existing FX balances
  • Clean, documented

Cons / risks

  • Irrelevant if your balance is CNY (conversion itself is the FX purchase)
  • FX account rules differ by bank; terms blank (unverified)

R11 — Transfers between your own FX accounts / direct relatives Lawful · conditional

Limit / headroom: Bounded by recipient’s own quota position   SAFE-1

Steps

  1. Same-name or direct-relative FX account transfer
  2. Bank verifies same nature & relationship
  3. No purchase occurs; receiver’s quota untouched for transfers of existing FX

Pros

  • Useful repositioning within family FX accounts
  • No fees beyond wire (if wired externally)

Cons / risks

  • Does not convert CNY→USD; only moves existing FX
  • Gift/contract docs needed cross-border

R12 — Leave it in CNY and spend abroad (card consumption) Lawful

Limit / headroom: No cap on overseas consumption itself   SAFE-3

Steps

  1. Use UnionPay/international card for overseas spending — consumption is excluded from the RMB 100,000 cash-withdrawal cap
  2. Merchant/acquirer converts at purchase time; spread embedded in the purchase rate

Pros

  • Zero FX paperwork; best for genuine living costs abroad
  • No remittance fee; no quota

Cons / risks

  • Doesn’t put USD in a US bank — unusable for a US balance or US bills
  • Card purchase FX markup: blank (unverified)
  • Not a strategy for idle $400k

The one-line rule

If someone offers you a CNY→USD conversion cheaper than the bank route (R01/R02/R09), it is either subsidised by a real document (R02/R07/R09) or it is a crime wearing a fee schedule. Ask for the written tariff item and the FX quote behind any number you are quoted; if they hesitate, walk away.
