Getting yuan out of China as dollars

If you hold a US passport and can show where the yuan came from, the cheapest lawful route found is an ordinary bank conversion and wire. At the rates and tariffs published on 10 November 2026, $400,000 costs $881.47 at Agricultural Bank of China or $900.77 at Bank of China; $10,000 costs $42.96 or $53.42.

Three things decide the outcome

  1. Papers, not price. A foreign-passport holder has no USD 50,000 yearly quota. The bank sells dollars against your passport plus documents showing the source and amount of the money, including tax vouchers. L1 L3 L4
  2. The route follows the source of the money. Pay earned in China, pre-emigration savings, an inheritance and a housing sale each have their own rule. Find your row in the table below. L1 L8 L9
  3. The largest tax exposure found is in the US. China taxes the income behind the money, not the transfer. The US treats a gain from exchange-rate movement above $200 as capital gain. At 6.6851 yuan per dollar that gain is 7.01% of the dollars received for yuan acquired at the 2024 average rate, and 6.23% at the 2025 average. T1 A2 R6

What to do next

  1. Find your row in the situation table. The route depends on the source of the money and on the ID the bank holds for you.
  2. Collect the papers for that route before you visit the bank, starting with PRC tax payment records.
  3. Ask the bank for its selling rate and wire fee that day and compare them with the Costs page.
  4. Wire to an account in your own name. At Bank of China, USD 25 buys full-amount arrival.
  5. Before converting, work out what each tranche of yuan was worth in dollars when you received it. A gain above $200 goes on your US return.
  6. Check FBAR, Form 8938 and Form 3520 for every year the account was open.
  7. Stay away from the five routes marked unlawful on the Methods page.
