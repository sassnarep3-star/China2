Costs

What it costs, $10,000 to $400,000

Forty amounts, ten thousand apart. The first money column is the verified statutory cost of converting your own already-taxed money. The next columns show where a real tax appears. The commercial column is deliberately empty.

How to read column A. $$0. At every amount. Not because a provider is cheap, but because no PRC statute imposes an FX-conversion tax and no US statute taxes moving your own funds between your own accounts. If that number looks too good, the catch is in column B: China taxes the income, not the wire.

Scenario definitions

A — Your own already-taxed money

Documented lawful income on which PRC tax was withheld or paid, evidenced by a 完税证明 (tax payment certificate) or withholding records. PRC conversion tax: 0% (S04 Art. 2 lists no such category). US tax on the transfer: 0% (§988(e), S06). Statutory total: $0.

B — Untaxed labour / service income

The whole amount is treated as PRC comprehensive income for a resident with no other income: annual progressive brackets after the RMB 60,000 deduction, converted at CNY 6.7168/USD (S04, S05, S12). This is an upper-bound illustration, not a determination.

C — Asset gain, dividend or interest

The whole amount is treated as falling in the 20% flat category (S04 Art. 3(3)). On a real asset transfer the 20% applies to the gain, not the gross; the gross version is used only because the cost basis is unknown (F06).

US residual

US federal tax on the same amount (single filer, 2026 brackets, $16,100 standard deduction, no other income) minus the credit for the PRC tax, floored at zero (S07, S13). State tax is excluded (F11).

What the table says, in words

  • Converting already-taxed money is free of tax at every amount. The only missing number is commercial.
  • At $400,000, an untaxed labour balance would carry about $148,896 of PRC IIT in this model, while the US residual after the credit is $0.
  • In the 20% scenario the picture changes at the top end: PRC tax of $80,000 against US tax of $103,134 leaves about $23,134 of US tax after the credit. That is a US-bracket effect, not a conversion penalty.
  • The $50,000 quota is a speed limit, not a tax. At $400,000 it needs 8 calendar years if it applies to you at all — which for a US citizen is exactly the open question in F02.
  • The ATM channel is capped at RMB 100,000 a year, about $14,888 at this rate, so it needs 27 years to move $400,000. It is a spending channel, not a repatriation plan.

Method notes

M01 · Documented after-tax RMB income → bank conversion → SWIFT wire

This is the route the research points to first for documented, already-taxed money.

No annual USD cap is stated for this route; one documented operation can cover the whole amount. S01S02S04

M02 · Re-conversion of unused RMB from an earlier FX conversion (original exchange memo)

Amounts on the memo are personal facts and are not published anywhere; left unverified.

No verified cap, or the cap is a personal fact not published anywhere. S02

M03 · Walk-in small-amount re-conversion with ID only

A safety valve, not a repatriation plan.

Daily ceiling applies. S02

M04 · Remit an existing FX savings-account balance abroad

The cheapest lawful path when it applies: no conversion spread, because no conversion is needed.

A single documented event; no published annual cap. S02

M05 · Overseas ATM cash withdrawals with a PRC bank card

Also note the US CMIR rule if physical cash crosses the border (S14).

Annual ceiling applies. S03

M06 · Overseas merchant / POS spending with a PRC card

Counts as a legitimate consumption channel only.

This channel does not turn the balance into USD you can hold. S03S01

M07 · Carry physical cash across the border

A last resort for small amounts.

No verified cap, or the cap is a personal fact not published anywhere. S22 (prior)S14

M08 · Sale of PRC real estate by a foreign individual, then remit after-tax proceeds

The 'conversion penalty' people feel is usually this 20% on the gain, already owed before any conversion.

A single documented event; no published annual cap. S01S02S04

M09 · Inheritance outbound transfer (继承财产转移 (inheritance asset transfer))

Documentation and approvals, not a tax rate, are the binding constraint.

A single documented event; no published annual cap. S02S17 (prior)

M10 · Emigrant personal-asset transfer (移民财产转移 (emigrant asset transfer)) — former PRC residents only

Not available to a US citizen who never emigrated from mainland China.

A single documented event; no published annual cap. S02S17 (prior)

M11 · PRC-citizen USD 50,000/year facilitated FX purchase quota

S11 Art. 52(2) defines 境内个人 (domestic individual) as including foreigners resident in China for one full year, which conflicts with the narrower reading used by an earlier session. Flagged, not resolved.

Annual ceiling applies. S01S02S11

M12 · Transfer between FX savings accounts of immediate relatives

Lawful for genuine family support; unlawful if used to evade limits.

No verified cap, or the cap is a personal fact not published anywhere. S02
