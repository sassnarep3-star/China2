US & compliance.

Moving funds does not erase past filing obligations. US and China rules are separate; consult qualified cross-border professionals.

RequirementWhat official guidance saysPractical noteOfficial source
Foreign-account report (FBAR)Generally file FinCEN 114 if aggregate foreign financial accounts exceed $10,000 at any time in the calendar year, subject to applicable rules.Separate from income tax; file electronically.Official source ↗
Form 8938Specified foreign financial assets may require Form 8938 if applicable thresholds are exceeded.Thresholds vary by filing status and residence; separate from FBAR.Official source ↗
US worldwide incomeUS citizens generally report worldwide income regardless of residence.Moving previously taxed principal is distinct from reporting underlying income; currency gains require separate review.Official source ↗
Foreign tax credit / earned-income exclusionForeign tax credits are subject to limits; qualifying earned income may be excluded under applicable conditions.No credit for taxes allocable to excluded income. Compare outcomes with a qualified adviser.Official source ↗
TreatyThe US–China income tax treaty has a saving clause; some treaty provisions have exceptions.Do not assume the treaty exempts a US citizen’s income.Official source ↗
US cash reportingCBP requires reporting currency and monetary instruments over $10,000 in aggregate when entering the US.FinCEN Form 105 is a reporting requirement, not itself a tax.Official source ↗
Avoid splitting and false documentsPBOC rules prohibit splitting to evade quota administration or using fabricated documentation.Do not use friends’ quotas, sham gifts, or repeated transactions to circumvent controls.Official source ↗
