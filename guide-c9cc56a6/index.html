Getting money out of China with the least tax penalty

A verified, source-linked guide to converting CNY into USD: 24 routes, a USD 10,000-400,000 cost ladder, the actual tax and penalty exposure, and every gap marked as a gap.

Executive summary

There is no tax on converting CNY into USD. Both governments tax the income, not the conversion - and the income was already taxed when you earned it.

Do the slow, documented thing. Get the PRC tax record that proves the balance is after-tax income, convert it at your bank with documents rather than within the quota, and file the US reports on time. That costs about 0.02%-0.31% in published bank fees, plus an FX margin the bank will not publish in advance, plus - possibly - a small section 988 exchange gain. Every shortcut converts a fee of tens of dollars into a fine of tens of thousands.

Six things that decide the answer

China does not tax a conversion

The Individual Income Tax Law taxes nine listed categories of income - wages, services, author's remuneration, royalties, business income, interest, dividends, property leasing, property transfer, incidental income. Converting your own after-tax renminbi is not one of them. Nothing in the nine categories is triggered by moving an existing balance to USD.

G09 · National People's Congress / State Taxation Administration

The US does not tax a conversion either - only the exchange-rate difference

US citizens are taxed on worldwide income, and foreign income is translated to dollars at the rate in effect when it is earned. Converting later can therefore create only a section 988 foreign-currency gain or loss measured from the rate on the day you acquired the currency. For an individual that is a personal transaction: no gain is recognised if the exchange gain is USD 200 or less.

G13 · IRS G18 · IRS G17 · US Code

At official IRS rates, the exchange difference on recent CNY has been a loss, not a gain

The IRS yearly average for the yuan is 6.452 (2021), 6.730 (2022), 7.075 (2023), 7.189 (2024) and 7.129 (2025). Yuan acquired in 2021-2023 and converted at the 2025 average is worth fewer dollars than its basis, so there is an exchange loss, not a taxable gain. Only a 2024-acquired balance shows a gain, and it is 0.84% of the amount.

G19 · IRS

The real cost of lawful conversion is small and published

The one itemised personal tariff verified for this study charges 0.1% of the remittance with a minimum of CNY 100 and a maximum of CNY 500, plus a CNY 120 cable fee. That is USD 30.99 on USD 10,000 (0.31%) and USD 87.32 on USD 400,000 (0.022%) - the fee cap stops growing while the amount does.

G08 · HSBC Bank

The real obstacle is documentation, not tax

A non-domestic individual may convert lawful renminbi current-account income on presenting identity documents plus source-of-funds materials, including tax vouchers. The USD 50,000 annual quota is a facilitation figure that binds quota-based purchases and domestic individuals; the documented-income route is not capped by it. What blocks a stuck balance is the absence of tax records, not a tax rate.

G01 · People's Bank of China G03 · SAFE Tianjin branch G02 · State Administration of Foreign Exchange

The expensive mistakes are the shortcuts

Splitting a large amount into small batches, borrowing other people's quotas, using an underground bank, or presenting fabricated documents are statutory violations with fines of 30%-100% of the amount involved - orders of magnitude more than any lawful route costs.

G01 · People's Bank of China G05 · State Council of the PRC

The conversion cost, as published

The only dollar figure a bank will commit to in advance is its published tariff. For the documented-income wire (C01) that is 0.1% of the amount, minimum CNY 100, maximum CNY 500, plus a CNY 120 cable fee (G08). The cap is why the percentage falls as the amount rises: the fee stops growing at USD 70,423 of principal.

If it were your money: the order of operations

  1. Get the tax record first. Download your PRC individual income tax payment record for the years the balance was earned. It is both the thing that proves the money is after-tax and the document the bank will ask for G03.
  2. Fix any unpaid PRC tax. If the income was never taxed, that is income tax on the income — 3%-45% comprehensive or 20% flat depending on the category G09 — and it is owed whether or not you move the money.
  3. Convert with documents, not with the quota. C01 has no annual ceiling in the published rules; the quota route C21 stops at USD 50,000 a year G01 G02.
  4. Get an executable all-in quote before signing. Ask for the USD selling rate, the commission with its floor and cap, and the cable fee, in one number.
  5. File the US reports on time. FBAR by 15 April (automatic to 15 October) above USD 10,000 aggregate G10; Form 8938 above the FATCA thresholds G11; Form 1116 to credit the PRC tax G12.
  6. Compute the section 988 difference, don't fear it. At official IRS yearly averages the yuan has weakened since 2021, so a 2021-2023 acquisition shows an exchange loss on conversion G19 G17. See Tax & penalties.

What this page will not do

It will not quote you a rate, because no bank publishes the customer rate it will deal on — that gap is flag F01. It will not put a number on your PRC tax, because that depends on facts this repo does not have (F08). And it will not tell you whether a section 988 gain above USD 200 is capital or ordinary, because the statute does not say and the IRS publication that is usually quoted could not be verified verbatim here (F04). Those three blanks are deliberate.
