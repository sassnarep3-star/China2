Quotations relied on

Art. 7 prohibits evading limit administration by splitting a large amount into small batches or tampering with authenticity administration using fabricated commercial bills or vouchers. Art. 13 permits a non-domestic individual to convert lawful RMB current-account income and remit it abroad on presenting valid identity documents and relevant documents.

G01 · Arts. 2, 7, 9, 13, 14 · used for: Legal basis of the documented-income route (M01); the anti-splitting rule that makes M15 unlawful.

Individuals may purchase/sell foreign exchange up to the equivalent of USD 50,000 per person per year; purchases beyond the quota are handled after an authenticity review of the supporting documents; funds in a trade settlement account are exempt from the quota on presentation of valid documents.

G02 · Quota paragraph; authenticity-review paragraph; trade settlement account paragraph · used for: The USD 50,000/year quota figure used in M02 and in the 'quota years' column of the cost ladder.

An overseas individual in China converting lawful RMB income must present identity documents plus source-of-funds materials for the transaction amount, including tax vouchers.

G03 · Q&A on an overseas individual converting lawful RMB income; cites Huifa [2020] No. 14 Art. 59 · used for: The document set (identity + source materials + tax vouchers) required by M01.

Art. 39: evasion of foreign exchange administration - a fine of up to 30% of the amount involved, or between 30% and the full amount if serious. Art. 42: illegal carriage of foreign exchange across the border - warning plus a fine of up to 20%. Art. 45: private (illegal) foreign exchange trading - confiscation of the funds plus a fine of up to 30%, or 30%-100% if serious. Art. 52: a foreign national who has resided continuously in China for one year or more is a 'domestic individual'.

G05 · Arts. 39, 42, 45, 52 · used for: Every penalty percentage in the penalty tables, and the residence test in M02.

Aggregate overseas cash withdrawals on a person's domestic bank cards may not exceed the equivalent of RMB 100,000 per calendar year; each card is capped at the equivalent of RMB 10,000 per day.

G06 · Arts. 1 and 4 · used for: The hard caps on M06 (ATM) and the 'years / days needed' columns for card and cash routes.

Art. 6: an emigration transfer must be applied for once for the whole amount and remitted in stages - the first remittance may not exceed half of the property, after one year no more than half of the remainder, and after two years the whole balance; amounts up to RMB 200,000 may be remitted in one go after approval. Art. 8(4): an applicant settling abroad must provide the police-issued cancellation of PRC household registration plus a certificate of overseas residence issued or authenticated by a Chinese embassy or consulate. Art. 8(6): a tax certificate or tax payment voucher issued by the competent tax authority is required. Art. 10: up to RMB 500,000 is approved by the local SAFE branch; above that it goes to SAFE head office. Art. 11: the bank must remit directly to the applicant's own overseas account and may not pay out foreign cash domestically.

G07 · Arts. 6, 8(4), 8(6), 10, 11 · used for: Staging of the emigration and inheritance routes (C07, C08), the document set, and the approval threshold.

2.2.4.1 Telegraphic Transfer Issuance - individual customer: 0.1% of remittance amount, minimum CNY 100, maximum CNY 500 per transaction, plus cable charge (Private Banking individual: free). 2.2.4.2 Cable Charges: CNY 120 per cable. 2.2.6.1 Global Transfer to your own overseas HSBC account: Free (Private Banking), Free (Premier), Advance CNY 145 indication charge - actual charge USD 20 or equivalent per transaction. Important Note 3: Premier requires last month's Total Relationship Balance of at least CNY 500,000 or equivalent.

G08 · Items 2.2.4.1 (telegraphic transfer issuance), 2.2.4.2 (cable charge), 2.2.6.1 (Global Transfer), 2.2.7 (International Education Payment); Important Note 3 (Premier eligibility, CNY 500,000 TRB) · used for: Every published fixed fee in the cost ladder. This is a commercial price list, not a regulator's figure and not a quote for any specific account.

Art. 2 lists the nine categories of income subject to IIT (wages and salaries, remuneration for personal services, author's remuneration, royalties, business income, interest, dividends, property leasing, property transfer, incidental income). Art. 3: comprehensive income 3%-45% progressive; business income 5%-35%; interest, dividends, property leasing, property transfer and incidental income 20%. Art. 6: RMB 60,000 annual deduction. Art. 11: annual reconciliation window 1 March - 30 June.

G09 · Arts. 2, 3, 6, 11 · used for: The PRC income tax rates in the penalty/tax breakdown. A bare currency conversion appears in none of the nine categories.

A US person must file an FBAR if the aggregate value of foreign financial accounts exceeds USD 10,000 at any time during the calendar year. Due 15 April, with an automatic extension to 15 October; filed as FinCEN Form 114 through BSA E-Filing.

G10 · Filing requirement; due date; automatic extension; FinCEN 114 / BSA E-Filing · used for: The USD 10,000 FBAR trigger and the filing calendar used in the tax section and the step-by-step.

Unmarried taxpayer living in the US: USD 50,000 on the last day of the year or USD 75,000 at any time. Married filing jointly living in the US: USD 100,000 / USD 150,000. Unmarried living abroad: USD 200,000 / USD 300,000. Married filing jointly living abroad: USD 400,000 / USD 600,000.

G11 · Form 8938 thresholds table · used for: Form 8938 thresholds in the tax section.

A taxpayer may choose to take the foreign tax credit (Form 1116) or an itemized deduction; the credit reduces US tax dollar for dollar. Foreign taxes on income excluded under the foreign earned income exclusion cannot be credited.

G12 · Choice between credit and deduction; Form 1116; interaction with the foreign earned income exclusion · used for: Why the PRC tax that is already paid does not stack on top of the US tax (the credit), and the FEIE interaction.

US citizens and resident aliens are taxed on their worldwide income regardless of where they live, and are subject to the same filing rules; the page lists the FBAR, Form 8938, foreign tax credit and foreign earned income exclusion as the relief and reporting provisions.

G13 · Worldwide income paragraph; filing and reporting overview · used for: The starting premise that the US taxes the income, not the conversion.

The exclusion is available to a US citizen who has a tax home in a foreign country and meets either the bona fide residence test or the physical presence test (330 full days in a 12-month period). The exclusion amount is inflation-adjusted annually and is claimed on Form 2555.

G14 · Eligibility tests; inflation-adjusted maximum exclusion; Form 2555 · used for: The FEIE tests named in the step-by-step, and the warning that excluded income cannot also generate a foreign tax credit.

Foreign financial agency transaction - non-willful violation of transaction: maximum USD 16,536 (statute USD 10,000). Foreign financial agency transaction - willful violation: maximum USD 165,353 (statute USD 100,000). Also 31 U.S.C. 5321(a)(1) general civil penalty for willful BSA violations: USD 71,545 - 286,184.

G15 · Table 1, rows for 31 U.S.C. 5321(a)(5)(B)(i) and 5321(a)(5)(C)(i)(I) · used for: The only FBAR penalty dollar figures on this site. Anything else about FBAR penalties is left blank or dated to its own source.

988(a)(1)(A): any foreign currency gain or loss attributable to a section 988 transaction is computed separately and treated as ordinary income or loss. 988(e)(1): the preceding provisions of the section do not apply to a section 988 transaction entered into by an individual which is a personal transaction. 988(e)(2): where nonfunctional currency is disposed of by an individual in a personal transaction, no gain is recognized by reason of exchange-rate changes after acquisition - but that sentence does not apply if the gain would exceed USD 200. 988(e)(3): a personal transaction is any transaction entered into by an individual, except to the extent allocable expenses meet section 162 or section 212.

G17 · 988(a)(1)(A); 988(b)(1); 988(e)(1), (e)(2), (e)(3) · used for: The only US tax that can attach to the conversion itself. The character question (capital vs ordinary once the USD 200 line is crossed) is flagged for review - see F04.

If your functional currency is the US dollar you must immediately translate into dollars all items of income, expense and taxes that you receive, pay or accrue in a foreign currency, using the exchange rate prevailing when you receive, pay or accrue the item.

G18 · Functional currency; translation at the rate prevailing when the item is received, paid or accrued · used for: Why the income is fixed in USD when it is earned, so a later conversion can only produce an exchange-rate difference - not a second tax on the income.

China, Yuan: 7.129 (2025), 7.189 (2024), 7.075 (2023), 6.730 (2022), 6.452 (2021). The IRS states that it has no official exchange rate and generally accepts any posted rate used consistently.

Anyone who brings, sends or receives currency or monetary instruments totalling more than USD 10,000 at one time, into or out of the United States, must report it on FinCEN Form 105 (electronic filing at fincen105.cbp.dhs.gov). Failure to file or submitting false information risks forfeiture and seizure, and civil or criminal penalties under 31 U.S.C. 5321, 5322, 5317 and 5332.
