The conversion penalty, broken down

There is no conversion tax. There are four separate exposures — PRC income tax, the section 988 exchange difference, US reporting penalties and PRC foreign-exchange fines — and they are usually confused with each other.

Where the "considerable tax" actually comes from

Four different things are being confused with each other

1. The conversion itself

China: nothing. The Individual Income Tax Law lists nine categories of taxable income; converting your own renminbi is not one of them G09.

United States: only the exchange-rate difference since you acquired the currency — section 988. For an individual's personal transaction, no gain is recognised at all if the exchange gain is USD 200 or less G17. The income itself was translated to dollars when it was earned G18, so it cannot be taxed a second time by moving it.

2. The income behind the balance

This is the large number, and it is not caused by the conversion. If the balance has already borne PRC tax, there is nothing left to pay in China. If it was never taxed, the exposure is the PRC rate on the income, plus the US rate on the same income net of the foreign tax credit.

Negative means an exchange loss — renminbi acquired in 2021-2023 and converted at the 2025 average buys fewer dollars than its basis. Only a 2024 acquisition shows a gain, at 0.83% of the amount, which crosses the USD 200 line at roughly $23,963 converted. All of this is arithmetic on the published IRS rates; your own dates are the missing input.

Flagged (F04): the statute does not say whether a section 988 gain above USD 200 is capital or ordinary. Section 988(e)(1) switches the ordinary-income rule off for an individual's personal transaction, and 988(e)(2) then says no gain is recognised on the disposition of nonfunctional currency by reason of exchange-rate changes - unless the gain would exceed USD 200. The statute does not state the character of the gain once that USD 200 line is crossed. The frequently quoted statement that it is reported as a capital gain could not be verified verbatim from the publication this session (F04), so this site does not choose a character. What it can say with certainty is the amount: only the exchange-rate movement since acquisition.

Relief, not penalty: the credit and the exclusion

PRC tax already paid on income that the US also taxes can be claimed as a credit on Form 1116 — a dollar-for-dollar reduction of US tax, not a deduction G12. Separately, a US citizen with a tax home abroad who meets the bona fide residence test or the 330-day physical presence test may exclude foreign earned income G14. The two interact: foreign tax on excluded income cannot also be credited.
