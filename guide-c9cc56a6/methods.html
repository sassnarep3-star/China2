Every route, with pros, cons and steps

C01 Documented after-tax income: bank FX conversion + SWIFT wire

lawful

A US citizen who can prove the RMB came from lawful current-account income on which PRC individual income tax was already paid or withheld.

Advantages

  • No USD 50,000 ceiling: the cap is the size of the documented lawful income.
  • Zero incremental PRC tax once the underlying tax is paid; PRC tax already paid is creditable in the US on Form 1116.
  • Clean audit trail for both SAFE and the receiving US bank.

Disadvantages

  • Requires tax and income paperwork that many people have never collected.
  • Counter review is discretionary; a thin file is refused.
  • You bear the bank's spread, which is not published in advance.

Step by step

  1. Download your personal IIT payment record from the PRC individual income tax app or request a 完税证明 at the tax service hall for the years the balance was earned.
  2. Assemble the income file: contract or invoices, payslips, bank statements showing the credits, and the tax record. Amounts must tie to the balance.
  3. Book a counter appointment at the account-holding branch (foreign-exchange business is not reliably transacted at a machine for this category).
  4. Ask for an executable all-in quote: the USD selling rate applied, the 0.1% commission with its CNY 100/500 floor and cap, and the CNY 120 cable fee.
  5. Complete the FX purchase; keep the exchange memo (水单) — it is the document that proves the conversion was authorised and is needed for any later re-conversion.
  6. Instruct the wire to your own US account. Keep the SWIFT MT103 / remittance advice.
  7. File FBAR (FinCEN 114) by 15 April and Form 8938 with your return if thresholds are met; claim PRC tax on Form 1116.

Documents the bank will want

  • Passport
  • Employment contract or service invoice/payment proof
  • PRC IIT payment record / tax certificate (完税证明 / 纳税记录)
  • Bank card and account opening documents
  • Source-of-funds statement for the receiving US bank

Timing and tax

Time: 1-10 business days

No PRC tax arises from converting money that has already borne PRC tax. Additional PRC tax is due only if the underlying income was never taxed.

⚑ The tariff quotes the issuance fee as free for Private Banking individual customers and 0.1% (min CNY 100, max CNY 500) plus CNY 120 cable for Premier and Advance. The model applies the Premier/Advance formula to everyone, so if anything it overstates the fee rather than understates it.

C21 Bank FX purchase inside the USD 50,000/year facilitation quota

lawful

A PRC-identity individual or a foreign national holding the PRC Foreign Permanent Resident ID Card. For most foreign passport holders the documented-income route (C01) is the one that applies.

Advantages

  • Least paperwork of any lawful route - identity only inside the quota.
  • Same published tariff as C01, so the fee is known in advance.

Disadvantages

  • USD 50,000 per person per year: USD 400,000 needs eight calendar years.
  • Not available to most foreign passport holders without the permanent resident card.
  • Using other people's quotas to move one balance is prohibited splitting (see C18).

Step by step

  1. Confirm you hold a PRC ID or the Foreign Permanent Resident ID Card; without one this route is generally not open to you.
  2. At the bank, purchase USD with renminbi inside the remaining annual quota, quoting your ID number - no transaction documents are required inside the quota.
  3. Remit to your own US account; keep the exchange memo and the wire advice.
  4. If the balance exceeds USD 50,000, repeat in later calendar years, or use C01 for the excess in the same year.
  5. File FBAR and Form 8938 if the thresholds are met; claim PRC tax on Form 1116.

Documents the bank will want

  • Passport plus PRC ID or Foreign Permanent Resident ID Card
  • Bank card and account documents

Timing and tax

Time: 1-10 business days per tranche

No PRC tax arises on converting money that has already borne PRC tax. The quota is an administrative limit, not a tax.

⚑ One wire per calendar-year tranche of up to USD 50,000.

C02 Documented income routed through same-name HSBC Global Transfer

lawful

As R01, but you already hold, or can open, an HSBC account in the US and an HSBC China Premier relationship.

Advantages

  • Cheapest published fixed fee of any fully documented route (0 to USD 7).
  • Same-bank transfer reduces correspondent-bank deductions and intermediary questions.

Disadvantages

  • Requires an eligible HSBC relationship on both sides; Premier has balance conditions.
  • The spread on the CNY→USD leg is unchanged and remains the dominant cost.
  • Account opening for a US person at a Chinese bank is subject to enhanced due diligence.

Step by step

  1. Confirm both accounts are in exactly the same name and that Global Transfer is enabled for the China-US corridor.
  2. Complete the R01 documentation and FX conversion inside HSBC China.
  3. Use Global Transfer to move the USD to your own HSBC US account: free for Premier, reference price USD 7 per transfer otherwise (S06 item 5.3.1).
  4. Keep the transfer confirmation as the audit trail; the receiving bank still needs a source-of-funds note.
  5. File FBAR / Form 8938 / Form 1116 as in R01.

Documents the bank will want

  • Everything in R01
  • HSBC US account in the same name
  • Premier status confirmed on both sides

Timing and tax

Time: 1-5 business days

Same tax analysis as R01: conversion of already-taxed income adds no PRC tax.

⚑ Published fee is zero for Private Banking and Premier customers and USD 20 per transaction for Advance, so the range's lower edge assumes the free tier. Premier requires a total relationship balance of CNY 500,000 or equivalent.

C03 Re-conversion of unused RMB bought with foreign currency (original exchange memo)

lawful

A US citizen whose RMB came from converting USD or another currency inside China, and who still holds the original exchange memo, within 24 months.

Advantages

  • No tax certificate needed — the cleanest documentary path when it applies.
  • No cap other than the amount of the original conversion.

Disadvantages

  • Only works to the extent you converted FX into RMB in the first place.
  • Strict 24-month window; expired memos push you back to R01.
  • You pay a round-trip spread, so this is a recovery route, not a way to move earned income.

Step by step

  1. Locate the original exchange memo for each conversion you now want to reverse.
  2. Check the date: the memo is valid for 24 months from the exchange date.
  3. Present the passport and memo at the counter and request conversion back to the original currency, then remit.
  4. Keep the new memo; it is the receipt for the outbound leg.

Documents the bank will want

  • Passport
  • Original exchange memo (原兑换水单), within 24 months
  • Bank statements showing the original conversion

Timing and tax

Time: 1-10 business days

Return of previously settled principal; no PRC income tax and no tax voucher is required for this category.

C07 Emigration asset transfer (移民财产转移)

lawful

A natural person who emigrated from mainland China (or moved to Hong Kong/Macao) and is transferring lawful pre-emigration assets, including a US citizen who formerly resided in China.

Advantages

  • The only route designed to move a whole lifetime balance, with no annual quota.
  • Explicit statutory staging, so the plan is predictable.
  • Highest-value route for a former PRC resident with a large balance.

Disadvantages

  • Requires emigration status and asset realisation, not just an RMB balance.
  • Two years to complete for anything material.
  • SAFE head-office approval above RMB 500,000; documentary burden is heavy.

Step by step

  1. Apply once, to the SAFE branch where your pre-emigration household registration was, for the whole amount you intend to move.
  2. Settle the PRC tax position on the realised assets and obtain the tax certificate.
  3. Obtain approval: up to RMB 500,000 equivalent is decided by the local SAFE branch; above that it goes to SAFE head office.
  4. Remit in the staged pattern: up to half first, up to half of the remainder after one year, the balance after two years. Amounts up to RMB 200,000 may go in one remittance once approved.
  5. The bank must pay directly to your own overseas account; cash payment inside China is not permitted for this category.
  6. Keep every approval and remittance advice for the US side.

Documents the bank will want

  • Written application stating the reason and a full account of source and realisation
  • Approval/record of emigration status
  • Proof of ownership and realisation of the assets
  • PRC tax clearance certificate for the assets
  • Notarised documents where required

Timing and tax

Time: up to 2 years of staged remittances

The transfer itself is not a tax; the tax authority's clearance on the realised assets is required and the applicable rate depends on the asset (for property, 20% of the gain under S07 Art. 3(3)).

C08 Inheritance asset transfer (继承财产转移)

lawful

A foreign citizen (including a US citizen) who has inherited assets in mainland China.

Advantages

  • No annual quota; one application per decedent.
  • Can be remitted in one transfer.

Disadvantages

  • Notarisation and consular authentication are slow.
  • Requires a tax certificate for the estate.
  • Requires a foreign-citizen residence certificate from a Chinese mission.

Step by step

  1. Obtain and notarise the inheritance documents and the deceased's title documents.
  2. Obtain the tax certificate from the tax authority where the assets are located.
  3. File one application at the SAFE branch where the deceased was registered; one application covers the whole estate from that decedent.
  4. Remit in one go or in instalments once approved.
  5. Report the bequest in the US on Form 3520 if the threshold is met (not verified in this study - see F07).

Documents the bank will want

  • Written application describing the relationship and the estate
  • Application form signed by you or your agent
  • Passport plus a certificate of overseas residence issued or authenticated by a Chinese embassy/consulate
  • Notarised proof of inheritance
  • Notarised proof of the deceased's title to the assets
  • Tax certificate or payment proof issued by the tax authority where the assets are
  • Notarised power of attorney if an agent acts

Timing and tax

Time: 1-6 months

China has no inheritance tax in the instruments verified here; the estate's own tax clearance is required and is checked before conversion.

G07 · PBOC

C09 Sale proceeds of PRC residential property

lawful

A US citizen selling a property they own in mainland China.

Advantages

  • Moves a large sum in one transaction once the tax is settled.
  • Tax is on the gain, not the price.

Disadvantages

  • The 20% gain tax is the largest single number in this study.
  • Specific FX paperwork for a non-domestic vendor's property proceeds was not verified in this session (F06).
  • Months of process.

Step by step

  1. Complete the sale and settle PRC IIT: the registration authority will not transfer title without the tax clearance certificate (S07 Art. 15).
  2. Apply to the bank/SAFE for conversion of the net proceeds under the property rules.
  3. Convert and remit to your own US account.
  4. Report the disposition in the US and claim the PRC tax on Form 1116.

Documents the bank will want

  • Title certificate
  • Sale contract and completion documents
  • PRC tax payment certificate for the transfer (checked at registration under S07 Art. 15)
  • Proof the property was self-use where an exemption is claimed

Timing and tax

Time: 3-12 months

Property transfer income is taxed at 20% of the gain (proceeds less original value and reasonable costs) under S07 Arts. 3(3) and 6(5). Whether a exemption applies to a particular flat was not verified (F06).

C10 Dividends from a PRC company you own

lawful

A US citizen shareholder of a PRC company with distributable after-tax profits.

Advantages

  • Efficient once the company has already paid EIT.
  • Treaty caps the dividend tax at 10%.

Disadvantages

  • 25% EIT plus 10% withholding is a combined burden on the profits.
  • Excess withholding above the treaty rate is not creditable in the US.
  • Requires a company, audited accounts and a board process.

Step by step

  1. Confirm distributable profits after 25% EIT and statutory reserves.
  2. Pass the distribution resolution and withhold dividend tax at the treaty rate with the residence certificate in place.
  3. Obtain the withholding payment certificate.
  4. Convert and remit through the company's bank with the resolution and tax documents.
  5. Claim the 10% withholding on Form 1116 in the US.

Documents the bank will want

  • Audited financial statements and profit distribution resolution
  • EIT payment proof
  • Treaty residence certification for the 10% rate
  • Tax withholding payment certificate

Timing and tax

Time: 3-12 months

Company-level EIT at 25% comes first (S08 Art. 4). The dividend then bears 20% domestic IIT, reduced to a 10% treaty ceiling for a US resident (S09 Arts. 9-11, S07 Art. 3(3)).

C24 Individual foreign-trade settlement account (genuine trade)

conditional

An individual carrying on genuine foreign trade through a settlement account. SAFE states such funds are exempt from the quota on presentation of valid documents (G02).

Advantages

  • Quota-exempt on valid documents (G02).
  • Designed for exactly this pattern - goods invoiced in foreign currency.

Disadvantages

  • Only genuine trade; using it for a personal balance is a misrepresentation.
  • Whether an individual (rather than an entity) can hold one was not established - F10.
  • Bank fees for this account type were not retrieved - blank and flagged.

Step by step

  1. Establish the trade account with the bank under the individual foreign trade rules.
  2. Assemble the customs declarations, contracts and invoices for the shipment.
  3. Convert and remit from the settlement account, which is quota-exempt on valid documents.
  4. Keep the file; report the business income and file the US reports.

Documents the bank will want

  • Business licence or registration
  • Customs and contract documents
  • Tax records

Timing and tax

Time: 1-10 business days

Trade income is business income; PRC tax is assessed on the income itself (5%-35%).

⚑ Cost cells blank: no published tariff for this account type was retrieved (F10).

G02 · State Administration of Foreign Exchange

C04 Passport-only small-amount conversion at a bank counter

lawful

Any non-domestic individual; documentary exemption is in the re-conversion rule.

Advantages

  • No paperwork beyond a passport.
  • Immediate settlement.

Disadvantages

  • USD 500 per day: 20 banking days for USD 10,000 and 800 for USD 400,000.
  • Cash spreads are the widest in this study.
  • Using it as a staging device for a large balance is unlawful, not merely slow.

Step by step

  1. Attend a branch counter with your passport.
  2. Request conversion of no more than the equivalent of USD 500 in one day.
  3. Take USD cash or instruct a wire from the resulting FX balance.
  4. Do not repeat this pattern to move a large balance: that is the conduct S01 Art. 7 and S18 prohibit, and it is the classic trigger for a watch-list referral.

Documents the bank will want

  • Passport

Timing and tax

Time: one banking day per USD 500

No tax at the counter, but the exemption is documentary only: it does not convert untaxed income into lawful money.

⚑ The counter's own commission on a small-amount conversion is not in any tariff retrieved, so the published-fee column is blank and the route is priced on the FX margin only (F03).

C05 Airside pre-departure conversion at a port-side counter

lawful

A departing non-domestic individual, at a port-side counter before departure (境内关外).

Advantages

  • Higher daily exemption than an onshore counter.

Disadvantages

  • USD 1,000 per day of travel; 400 trips for USD 400,000.
  • Requires physically moving cash, with CBP reporting and seizure risk for errors.

Step by step

  1. Convert no more than the equivalent of USD 1,000 in a single day at a port-side counter before departure.
  2. Declare the cash to US CBP on FinCEN Form 105 if you arrive with more than USD 10,000 in total (S13).
  3. Do not treat repeated trips as a transfer mechanism.

Documents the bank will want

  • Passport
  • boarding pass

Timing and tax

Time: one departure per USD 1,000

Same documentary-only exemption as R04.

⚑ Port-side counters publish no tariff in any source retrieved; blank fee, FX margin only (F03).

G03 · SAFE Tianjin branch

C06 Withdraw USD cash in China and carry it out

lawful

Any individual, up to the cash-carrying ceiling.

Advantages

  • No tax certificate for the cash itself.
  • Works today, with no application.

Disadvantages

  • USD 5,000 per trip without a certificate: 80 trips for USD 400,000.
  • Physical risk plus forfeiture exposure for a reporting failure (S13).
  • Widest spread of any channel.

Step by step

  1. Convert or withdraw USD cash at the counter. On the verified tariff a foreign-cash withdrawal costs 0.25%-0.4% with a CNY 40 minimum on non-Premier accounts and is waived for Premier (S06 item 3.1.1).
  2. Above USD 5,000 and up to USD 10,000, obtain a bank carrying certificate before departure.
  3. Above USD 10,000 a SAFE-issued certificate is required and is granted only in listed special cases.
  4. Declare to CBP on arrival above USD 10,000 in aggregate.
  5. Deposit in the US and keep the withdrawal and declaration records.

Documents the bank will want

  • Passport
  • Bank withdrawal slip
  • Carrying certificate for USD 5,000-10,000
  • FinCEN Form 105 on arrival above USD 10,000

Timing and tax

Time: one trip per USD 5,000

Carrying cash is not a taxable event in itself; it also does not discharge any unpaid PRC or US tax.

⚑ Cash carriage has no bank fee but a hard physical limit per trip, plus a US declaration duty above USD 10,000 (G20). The band below prices the bank's cash margin only.

C11 Overseas ATM withdrawals on a PRC bank card

lawful

Anyone holding a PRC-issued bank card.

Advantages

  • No application and no tax certificate.
  • Useful for living costs, not for a balance.

Disadvantages

  • RMB 100,000 a year is about USD 14,000: 29 years for USD 400,000.
  • Exceeding the cap suspends overseas cash access for the rest of that year and the next.
  • ATM operator fees were not verified (F08).

Step by step

  1. Withdraw abroad within RMB 10,000 equivalent per card per day.
  2. Stay within RMB 100,000 equivalent per person per calendar year.
  3. Deposit the USD and keep the ATM receipts.

Documents the bank will want

  • PRC bank card
  • Passport for the receiving bank's KYC

Timing and tax

Time: about 14,000 USD per calendar year per person

No PRC tax at withdrawal; it does not discharge unpaid tax on the underlying income.

⚑ Issuer and ATM-operator fees are not published in any source retrieved - blank, flagged F03. The real constraint is the RMB 100,000/year and RMB 10,000/day caps (G06).

C12 Spend the RMB directly on overseas purchases with a PRC card

lawful

Anyone holding a PRC-issued card, for genuine current-account spending.

Advantages

  • No quota and no application.
  • Converts at the card scheme rate as you spend.

Disadvantages

  • Only works if you have a matching expense stream.
  • Not a way to obtain USD savings.
  • Round-tripping purchases to extract cash can breach the cash-withdrawal rules.

Step by step

  1. Pay genuine overseas expenses (tuition, medical, travel, suppliers) directly with the card.
  2. Keep invoices: the merchant is being paid, so the underlying purpose is visible.

Documents the bank will want

  • PRC bank card
  • Merchant receipts

Timing and tax

Time: as you spend

Spending is not conversion income; it also does not settle unpaid tax on the source.

⚑ Card-scheme settlement margin and issuer foreign-transaction fees are not published - blank, flagged F03.

C13 Pay US obligations directly from the China account

lawful

Anyone with a genuine US invoice or obligation (tuition, hospital, tax, supplier).

Advantages

  • No quota and low friction when the document is genuine.
  • Reduces the balance you ever need to repatriate.

Disadvantages

  • Does not put money in your own account.
  • Only limited by how much genuine US spending you actually have.

Step by step

  1. Obtain the genuine invoice from the US payee.
  2. Present it at the counter with your passport; current-account payments are convertible against the underlying document (S04 Art. 12, S02 Art. 11).
  3. Instruct the wire to the payee, not to yourself.
  4. Keep the invoice and payment advice together.

Documents the bank will want

  • Invoice or tuition statement
  • Proof of the obligation
  • Passport
  • The bank's current-account payment form

Timing and tax

Time: 1-10 business days

A current-account payment, not a transfer of your own savings; the tax sits with the underlying income, not with the payment.

conditional

Potentially an individual eligible for cross-border RMB remittance. Eligibility for a non-resident US citizen was NOT verified in this session.

Advantages

Documents the bank will want

  • Not established in this session - see F05

Timing and tax

Time: unknown

Conversion offshore does not create PRC tax, and does not cure an untaxed onshore balance. Moving RMB out still requires a compliant onshore basis.

⚑ The onshore leg is priced from the tariff; the offshore conversion and its own fees are not published, so the total is a floor.

C22 Wise (from-CNY) transfer to your own US account

conditional

Non-mainland-Chinese individuals with PRC tax records for the last three years, sending their own funds to their own account. Provider terms, not a regulator's rule (G24).

Advantages

  • Fee shown before you send, unlike a bank counter.
  • No calendar-year staging if the documented-income limit is large enough.

Disadvantages

  • Provider eligibility and limits are commercial terms, not published law - flagged (G24).
  • Own accounts only; no third-party recipients.
  • No statically published fee, so no cost cell can be filled (F03).

Step by step

  1. Create a Wise personal account and complete verification.
  2. Upload PRC tax records so the provider can set your sending limit.
  3. Set up a CNY to USD transfer to your own US account and read the in-app fee and rate before sending.
  4. Pay from your own Chinese bank app to the pay-in account the provider names, or by Alipay within its lower limit.
  5. Keep the receipt; file FBAR and Form 8938 if the thresholds are met.

Documents the bank will want

  • Passport
  • PRC tax records (tax paid within the last three years)
  • Own-name US account

Timing and tax

Time: 1-3 business days after pay-in

Same analysis as C01: the income was taxed when earned; conversion adds no PRC tax.

⚑ Every cost cell is blank and flagged: the provider publishes no static fee schedule (F03).

C23 Western Union from China via a partner bank

conditional

Residents of China sending through a partner bank's app or counter, with cash pickup abroad. Provider terms (G25).

Advantages

  • Fast and available in cash where a bank wire is not.
  • Useful for small amounts.

Disadvantages

  • Fees and per-transfer limits are not published statically - blank and flagged (F03).
  • Cash pickup creates a physical-carry reporting problem above USD 10,000 (G20).
  • The agent bank's own foreign exchange controls still apply.

Step by step

  1. Register a profile with a partner bank, or visit a branch with your passport.
  2. Quote the purpose and the recipient; pay from your account or card plus the quoted fee.
  3. Send the control number to the recipient only.
  4. The recipient collects cash with identity documents, then deposits it in the US.
  5. Keep the receipt; carrying more than USD 10,000 across the border requires FinCEN Form 105 (G20).

Documents the bank will want

  • Passport
  • Partner-bank account or card
  • Recipient details

Timing and tax

Time: Minutes to a few business days

The agent bank remains subject to PRC foreign exchange rules; the conversion basis still has to be lawful.

⚑ Every cost cell is blank and flagged: no published static fee (F03).

G01 · People's Bank of China G05 · State Council of the PRC

C20 Keep the money in China and use it as a spending account

lawful

Anyone — this is the baseline every other route must beat.

Advantages

  • Zero conversion cost and zero spread.
  • Reversible at any time.

Disadvantages

  • Currency and country risk stay with you.
  • US reporting continues regardless.
  • The problem is deferred, not solved.

Step by step

  1. Keep the balance and spend it on trips, cards and obligations paid from China.
  2. File FBAR and Form 8938 every year the thresholds are met.
  3. Revisit only if you need the capital in USD.

Documents the bank will want

  • Passport for account maintenance
  • FBAR / Form 8938 filings

Timing and tax

Time: immediate

No conversion, no PRC conversion cost. US reporting duties (FBAR/8938) still apply.

⚑ Nothing is converted, so nothing is paid - and nothing leaves China. The balance stays exposed to future exchange-rate and policy risk instead.
