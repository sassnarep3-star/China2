Executive summary

No trustworthy cheapest method or total cost is established. The account's ownership, source, Chinese tax history, U.S. basis, bank's eligibility decision and executable exchange rates and fees are unknown. A government rule is not a bank price quote.

For a non-domestic individual with documented, lawful Chinese current-account RMB income, ask the bank about conversion and outward remittance under PBOC rules article 13 and SAFE rules article 13. This is a starting point, not proof that this account qualifies or that the bank will accept particular documents. Capital-account proceeds require separate analysis.

Separate tax on underlying income or gains, any U.S. foreign-currency gain, bank conversion spread, sending/intermediary/receiving fees, and compliance penalties. Prior tax paid does not alone prove all taxes are settled or establish a zero tax outcome. Compare actual quotes, and seek cross-border legal and tax advice before execution.

The existing route comparison, costs, tax and reporting and existing review flags provide further background. The separate portal contains statements flagged below; do not rely on these statements without correction.

Practical sequence

  1. Establish the actual owner, how funds were acquired, whether each amount is current-account income, capital proceeds or reconversion of unused exchanged RMB, and the relevant filing and tax history.
  2. Ask the originating bank for the current written requirements and a transaction-specific explanation of any requested tax, documentation, refusal or restriction. SAFE's 2021 guidance identifies examples of tax documentation for certain non-domestic individual current-account income; a tax-exemption certificate is one example.
  3. For routes the bank confirms are eligible, obtain contemporaneous written, like-for-like quotes: CNY debited, USD delivered, rate, all sender/intermediary/recipient fees, validity and any charges deducted from proceeds. Compare totals on the same amount and date. Verify that any provider is authorized and will accept the transaction.
  4. Have qualified advisers review Chinese tax, U.S. worldwide income and currency gain, foreign tax credit limitations, and past or current account reporting before any transfer. An FBAR may be required if the aggregate value of reportable foreign accounts exceeded $10,000 at any time in a calendar year.
  5. Use the bank-approved procedure, accurately describe ownership and purpose, retain documentation and the actual settlement confirmation. Do not use borrowed quotas, sham invoices or split transactions to evade controls: PBOC article 7.
