Tax and reporting rules

The rules that apply, with the rate and the article

Two systems apply at once. The PRC taxes the income and controls the conversion; the US taxes the citizen on worldwide income and requires the account to be reported. Neither read here taxes the act of converting.

How the two interact on a single conversion

  1. The conversion. No PRC tax arises from the exchange; no US income arises from exchanging principal. A section 988 gain or loss may arise from the currency movement, measured against the rate at which the CNY was acquired. S19 S22
  2. The income behind it. If the CNY is PRC-source salary, PRC tax was withheld when it was paid. On the US return the same income is taxable, with a foreign tax credit for the PRC tax actually paid — only the treaty-rate tax qualifies, and no credit is available against excluded income. S21 S27
  3. The account. FBAR and Form 8938 are triggered by balances, not by transfers, and are due for every year the threshold was crossed. S15 S16
  4. The gift question. Money received from a foreign relative is not automatically income, but a genuine gift above USD 100,000 from a nonresident alien must be reported on Form 3520, and the IRS may recharacterise a purported gift. S18
No personalised conclusion is offered. Whether a particular transfer is income, a gift, a return of capital or a property transfer determines the tax; that fact is not known here. The question register is the route to it.
