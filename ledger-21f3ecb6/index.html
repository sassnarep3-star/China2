Getting money out of China: what it costs, and what actually blocks it

The money is not stuck because of tax. On the rules read here, converting CNY to USD is not a taxable event at all; the blocks are a USD 50,000 annual purchase total, a documentary review above it, and the fees and spread a bank charges on the way through. This dashboard prices every route it can from published terms, from USD 10,000 to USD 400,000 in USD 10,000 steps, and publishes every value it could not verify as a blank.

The short answer, in four lines.
  1. No PRC tax is levied on the conversion itself. The income tax law taxes listed categories of income; a currency exchange is not one of them. Whether tax is owed on the money is a separate question about where the money came from, and that is still open. S19
  2. One person can buy at most USD 50,000 of foreign exchange per calendar year on ID alone. Above that the bank requires documents. USD 400,000 is eight calendar years at one person's total. S01
  3. The published cost of the transfer itself is small: $900.50 on USD 400,000 (0.225%) through Bank of China online banking, and $50.03 on USD 10,000 (0.500%). Those are fees plus the bank's own spread, not tax. S09 S10
  4. The expensive mistakes are the shortcuts. Splitting the amount across family totals is named in the rules and puts you on a watch list for three years; private FX dealing and crypto conversion are prohibited, with fines up to 30-100 percent of the amount. S02 S03 S08

Method in one paragraph

Each route was priced only from published inputs: the bank's own rate board, the bank's own fee schedule or tariff, and the regulator's rule. The spread is measured against the bank's own midpoint, so it isolates the bank's charge from market movement; the Federal Reserve H.10 is shown alongside as an external benchmark. Where a charge is not published — intermediary bank deductions, Wise's fee and rate, HSBC Premier's monthly fee — the cell is blank and carries a link to its entry on the Flagged for review page. Nothing was inferred, and no value was filled in from a secondary source.

What this is. A source-linked working file, not advice. Every figure is either quoted from a linked official page or computed from one by model.py; every blank is published as a blank and listed on the Flagged for review page. No tax liability, route eligibility or all-in quote is asserted for any person.
