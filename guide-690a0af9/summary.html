Executive summary

The short answer, with the official page behind each point.

#PointSources
1There is no tax on converting CNY to USD in any of the official pages read today. The real "conversion penalty" has four parts: the bank's exchange spread (0.21% at Bank of China, 0.50% at HSBC China to the board midpoint today), a capped wire fee (at most CNY 410 at BOC, CNY 620 at HSBC), PRC income tax that should already have been paid on how the money was earned, and US reporting duties.S09S01S02S03S04S20S21
2The lawful path for a US citizen is a documented purchase of USD at the bank holding the money, followed by a wire to a US account in your own name. The bank's gate is paperwork: passport plus proof of where the RMB came from and that PRC tax was paid (SAFE 汇发[2007]1号 Art. 13; SAFE Tianjin 2026 Q&A).S08S10S13
3Cheapest published route for most amounts: Bank of China online purchase + online cross-border wire (an existing HSBC Premier customer pays less with Global Transfer at $10,000, before the CNY 300 monthly Premier fee). Known cost (fee + spread to the board midpoint) is $53.37 at USD 10,000 and $898.75 at USD 400,000 (0.225%). The same transfer through HSBC China costs $2,083.91 at USD 400,000. Correspondent and receiving fees are not published and are left blank.S01S03S02S04
4If you emigrated from China and the money is pre-emigration property, use SAFE's emigrant property transfer: free, 25 working days at most, and staged remittance was abolished in 2014.S12S14
5Avoid: splitting across other people's quotas (watch list for 3 years), underground swaps (fine up to 30%), disguised transfers (30%–100%), and any crypto/stablecoin bridge (illegal since 银发〔2026〕42号).S15S09S18
6US side: none of the IRS, FinCEN or CBP pages read taxes the wire itself; what can be taxable is FX gain, which is not excluded above USD 200 per conversion (26 USC 988(e)(2)) — RMB acquired when the 2025 IRS average rate was 7.129 and converted at the BOC sell rate used here shows about a 6.20% USD gain. File FBAR if your foreign accounts exceeded USD 10,000 at any time, and check Form 8938. FEIE is USD 132,900 for 2026 and USD 137,300 for 2027 (Rev. Proc. 2026-35 §3.41, verified in the official PDF today).S27S07S01S21S23S25S26
7A separate 1% US excise tax now exists on remittance transfers (from 1 Jan 2026), but the IRS limits it to transfers the sender funds with cash, a money order or a cashier's check; paying from a bank account or card keeps a transfer out of it, and a China→US wire is not a remittance sent from the US. Budget 1% only for a cash-funded US-side leg (flag F-19).S31S32
8Using a commercial platform instead of a bank wire is priced, not blank: Wise's own panel quotes 0.1500 USD per CNY and a CNY 1,224.27 fee on CNY 200,000 — about 0.61%, or USD 61 at USD 10,000 and USD 2,448.54 at USD 400,000 on a straight-line extension of that single quote (flag F-20) — with eligibility limited to people who have worked in China (flag F-21).S05
9This repository's earlier sections still contain lines that conflict with the official text — emigrant transfers described as staged, a "dollar-for-dollar" foreign tax credit — and crypto mentioned in places without its illegal status (to check). Every line is listed with file and line number on review.html.S01S09S12S18S29

Recommended order of work

  1. Answer the decision questions — origin of the money, tax paid, residence status, emigrant or not.
  2. Collect passport, source-of-funds documents and PRC tax records S08S10S14.
  3. Bring US reporting up to date before moving large sums (FBAR, Form 8938; streamlined procedures if non-wilful) S21S23S30.
  4. Ask the bank for its current rate, fee and online limits; compare with the cost tables S01S03.
  5. Convert and wire to your own US account; keep every slip S13.
  6. Record the CNY acquisition rate and the conversion rate for each transaction to work out any gain above $200 S27.
