Flagged for review: tax and cost claims

Reviewed 10 November 2026. This is a targeted source review, not a complete legal audit of every repository entry. No transfer was made. Not tax or legal advice.

Most important: Exact tax, eligibility and total cost are not established by the supplied facts. The earlier briefing used categorical “$0 tax” and assumed spreads; PR #34 corrected those statements after this review. Do not treat remaining estimates as personalized outcomes. Exchange gains can be taxable. Consult qualified advisers in both countries and obtain an executable, itemized quote.

Decision in brief

First investigate a documented bank route if the balance is qualifying lawful current-account income. The bank must determine the correct category, documentation and transfer eligibility; a U.S. passport alone does not establish the applicable Chinese FX classification. Other source categories, such as capital transactions, need separate review.

For eligible overseas individuals' China-sourced lawful current-account income, SAFE describes purchase of foreign currency with ID and source documentation including tax vouchers. This is not a promise that the bank will accept any particular transaction. SAFE Tianjin, 13 July 2026 · 2020 guidance, articles 54–59, 64–65 and 68.

U.S. exchange gains		An earlier briefing summary said “$0 PRC or U.S. tax on the conversion act” and “None on the exchange.” PR #34 corrected this overly categorical wording. Currency disposal can produce exchange gain or loss; treatment depends on the transaction, basis, holding and purpose. The statute excludes gain on a qualifying personal transaction only if the otherwise recognizable gain does not exceed $200. Do not assume $200 is a deduction from a larger gain or that all bank-balance conversions qualify.
