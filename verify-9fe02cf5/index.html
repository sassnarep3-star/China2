What it actually costs to get CNY out of China, and what is still unknown

The balance is not expensive to convert because of a tax on converting. On the law read here it is expensive only if the underlying income was never taxed in China, and otherwise the real cost is a bank spread plus a small, capped wire fee. Every figure below is traced to a document re-fetched on 2026-11-11; everything that could not be traced is blank.

Short answer

Cheapest lawful shape: convert documented current-account income at a bank that accepts the file, in one instruction, and send it with the published sender fee. At the bank whose tariff was read today that fee is capped at $92.66 on $400,000 (Premier/Advance) or $0.00 on Private Banking. The benchmark spread on $400,000 is $1,679.84. So a benchmark all-in of about $1,772.50 (0.44%) on $400,000 and $74.88 (0.75%) on $10,000. Those two numbers are arithmetic on published prices, not a quote.

What would change the answer: if the money is untaxed PRC income, the PRC income tax is the cost and it is measured in tens of percent, not basis points. That fact is unknown (F10).

What the evidence supports

  • 26 of 27 load-bearing statements were re-read verbatim in an official or bank-published document on 2026-11-11; 1 are partial. The ledger is on Verification.
  • The sender-side fee is small and capped on the routes where a tariff was published (S20). The spread, not the fee, is the cost that scales.
  • Cash is not a shortcut: overseas ATM withdrawals are capped at RMB 100,000 per person per civil year (S07).
  • The US side does not double-tax the same income if the PRC tax is paid and documented, because a credit is available on Form 1116 with a one-year-back / ten-year-forward carry (S15 S16).

What the evidence does not support

  • Any claim that this particular balance is tax-free, or that the bank's request is unlawful. The origin and tax history are unknown.
  • Any specific all-in cost in dollars for this account. The spread and third-party charges are unpublished (F03, F04).
  • Any use of a crypto/USDT bridge: RMB-to-virtual-currency business is classified as illegal financial activity (S10).
  • Any use of relatives' quotas, underground swaps or 'agents' to defeat the limits; those carry fines of up to 30% or more of the amount (S01).
