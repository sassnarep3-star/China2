Methods: 15 ways to move CNY out of mainland China

Every method I could find, with its legal basis, its published caps, its real pros and cons, and the documents it demands. Costs are on the cost page; nothing here is priced unless a source prices it.

The two unlawful routes are included because the brief asked for every way, including the ones that look attractive. They are not recommendations and they are not priced as if they were.

R01 Documented current-account conversion, then wire the USD out

Lawful

Primary route

Who it fits
US citizen with lawful RMB current-account income in a mainland bank (salary, fees, etc.)
Legal basis
Current Account FX Business Guidelines 2020, 汇发〔2020〕14号 Art. 59 (non-domestic individual) or Art. 56 (domestic individual); FX Regulations 条例 Art. 5 and 12; PBOC Order No. 3 Art. 13 S01 S02 S03 S07 S14 S15 S16
Caps / limits
No USD 50,000 cap is stated for this documented conversion. Art. 54 puts the USD 50,000 annual facilitation quota on individuals' FX SALES and DOMESTIC individuals' FX PURCHASES; Art. 59 handles a non-domestic individual's documented current-account purchase separately. A holder of a PRC Foreign Permanent Resident ID may instead use the quota (Art. 59 second sentence).

Pros

  • Lawful and expressly provided for in the current rule text
  • No published quota cap for documented current-account income
  • Once the money is in your own FX account, Art. 65(1) lets a non-domestic individual remit it abroad on ID alone

Cons

  • The bank, not you, decides whether the paper is sufficient (Art. 68 lets it refuse)
  • Requires an unbroken tax and employment paper trail for the whole balance
  • Bank fee is capped but the FX spread scales with the amount
  • Chinese banks may require the receiving account name to match exactly
Documents required
  • Passport (valid ID)
  • Source-of-funds documents for the amount: employment contract, payslips, and the PRC individual income tax payment record (税务凭证 / 完税凭证) — Art. 59 expressly includes tax vouchers
  • Bank's own authenticity review under 条例 Art. 12 and Art. 68
Exact steps
  1. Ask the bank for its written list of required documents for a non-resident/foreign-individual current-account conversion of this amount.
  2. Assemble the tax payment record for the same period as the funds (税务凭证/完税凭证) plus contracts and payslips.
  3. Convert CNY→USD at the bank counter or in online banking; keep the 结汇/购汇 slip (水单).
  4. Either remit directly (step 5) or park the USD in your own 外汇储蓄账户, then remit under Art. 65(1).
  5. Send the wire with your name and address exactly as on the US account; choose SHA/OUR consciously (OUR + 'Pay Exact' costs CNY 200 at HSBC).
  6. Keep the SWIFT confirmation, the Chinese conversion slip and the tax vouchers — they are the evidence for both FBAR/8938 and any later audit.

Not verified:

  • The FX spread your particular bank will apply (commercial, not published as a rule)
  • Any intermediary/correspondent bank deduction
  • Whether your specific documents will be accepted

R02 Remit a foreign-currency balance you already hold

Lawful

Cheapest if the account is already in USD

Who it fits
Only if the balance is already foreign currency in your own FX savings account
Legal basis
Current Account FX Business Guidelines 2020 Art. 65(1); PBOC Order No. 3 Art. 14 S01 S03 S14 S15
Caps / limits
No amount cap stated for a non-domestic individual remitting from an FX account (Art. 65(1)). Cash is different: USD 10,000/day with documents above that (Art. 65(2)).

Pros

  • No conversion spread at this step — the CNY→USD conversion already happened earlier
  • Only the wire fee applies
  • The rule text requires ID only, not a document bundle

Cons

  • Only works to the extent the money is already in FX — does not solve a CNY balance
  • The original CNY→FX conversion would itself have carried a spread
Documents required
  • Funds already in a foreign-currency account in your own name
  • Valid ID at the counter (Art. 65(1) requires no amount cap and no transaction documents)
Exact steps
  1. Confirm with the bank whether the balance is CNY or a foreign currency, and in which account type.
  2. If FX: instruct the outward remittance under Art. 65(1) with your passport.
  3. If the bank asks for source documents anyway, supply them; the rule permits a bank to refuse a suspicious transaction (Art. 68).
  4. Reconcile the amount received against the amount sent to measure the true all-in deduction.

Not verified:

  • Whether your balance is CNY or FX — a private fact I must not assume

R03 HSBC Global Transfer to your own HSBC account abroad

Lawful

Lowest published fee, but only HSBC-to-HSBC

Who it fits
HSBC China customer who also holds an HSBC account in the US
Legal basis
HSBC China tariff 2.2.6.1; the underlying conversion still needs 汇发〔2020〕14号 compliance S14 S01 S15
Caps / limits
Tariff states no amount cap. The FX-conversion rules still apply before any transfer.

Pros

  • Premier: free; Advance: USD 20 per transaction (tariff 2.2.6.1)
  • One institution on both ends can shorten the correspondent chain

Cons

  • Requires HSBC on both ends
  • The free/discounted tier requires a CNY 500,000 relationship balance or equivalent (tariff Important Note 3)
  • Still needs the Art. 59 documentation for the conversion itself
Documents required
  • HSBC China account at Advance/Premier/Private Banking tier
  • HSBC account in your own name in the US (2.2.6.1 covers self-name transfers)
  • The same conversion documentation as R01 for the CNY→USD leg
Exact steps
  1. Confirm your HSBC China tier and whether a US HSBC account exists in your name.
  2. Complete the documented conversion (R01 steps 1–3).
  3. Use Global Transfer to Self; the tariff price applies per transaction, not per dollar.
  4. Confirm receipt and record any deduction.

Not verified:

  • HSBC US incoming-wire fee (not verified this session)

R04 Withdraw USD cash abroad on a mainland bank card (ATM)

Lawful, capped

Small amounts only — hard annual cap

Who it fits
Anyone holding a mainland-issued RMB or foreign-currency bank card
Legal basis
SAFE Notice 汇发〔2017〕29号 Arts. 1 and 4 S06 S16
Caps / limits
CNY 100,000 equivalent per person per calendar year across all your cards (supplementary cards included); CNY 10,000 equivalent per card per day. Exceeding the annual cap suspends overseas cash withdrawals for the rest of that year AND the following year (Art. 1).

Pros

  • No up-front document bundle
  • Useful for living costs rather than repatriation

Cons

  • Cannot move USD 400,000 — the annual ceiling is about USD 14,958 at the 2026-11-10 BOC rate
  • Breaching the cap triggers a suspension, and card-lending to evade is expressly prohibited (Art. 1)
  • ATM operator fees and card FX pricing are set commercially and were not verifiable
Documents required
  • Mainland-issued bank card
  • Overseas ATM that accepts the card network
Exact steps
  1. Check the card's daily and annual limits with the issuing bank.
  2. Withdraw at an ATM in the US; keep every receipt.
  3. Track cumulative withdrawals against the CNY 100,000 annual ceiling.
  4. Do not use anyone else's card and do not lend yours (Art. 1).

Not verified:

  • Issuing-bank ATM fee
  • Card-network FX markup

R05 Spend the CNY directly on US expenses with the card

Lawful

Avoids conversion, but you never receive USD

Who it fits
Anyone who can pay US merchants with a mainland card
Legal basis
Current Account FX Business Guidelines 2020 Art. 76(5) (domestic cards used abroad, repaid by FX purchase) S01 S16
Caps / limits
Merchant and card limits apply; no regulator cap stated for purchases.

Pros

  • No conversion paperwork
  • Buys goods and services at the point of need

Cons

  • Does not produce a USD balance you control
  • Card FX markup and any foreign-transaction fee apply to every purchase
  • Merchant acceptance of UnionPay in the US is uneven
Documents required
  • Merchant that accepts UnionPay or the card network
Exact steps
  1. Test a small purchase to see the posted rate and any fee.
  2. Compare the posted CNY cost against the BOC published rate to measure the markup.
  3. Use only for expenses you would incur anyway.

Not verified:

  • Card FX markup
  • Foreign transaction fee

R06 Emigration asset transfer (移民财产转移)

Lawful if eligible

For pre-emigration assets — needs cancelled hukou

Who it fits
A person who emigrated from the mainland (or settled in HK/Macau) and is moving assets owned BEFORE acquiring emigrant status
Legal basis
Provisional Measures on Individuals' Outward Asset Transfers (个人财产对外转移售付汇管理暂行办法) Arts. 2, 8, 11, as changed by SAFE Huifa [2014] No. 2 Section 6 S04 S17 S01
Caps / limits
No instalment schedule since 10 February 2014: once the SAFE branch of your original household registration approves, the bank may send the approved amount in one remittance or several. The same notice removed the head-office step for totals above RMB 500,000. The 2004 text (half, then half of the remainder after a year, then the balance after two years) no longer applies.

Pros

  • Designed exactly for moving a whole lawful estate abroad
  • Explicit legal pathway with defined documents

Cons

  • Requires having given up PRC household registration — a one-way step
  • Needs an approval document (核准件) from the SAFE branch of your original household registration before the bank will act
  • Bank must remit to your own account abroad; no cash payout in China
Documents required
  • Cancelled PRC household registration certificate (户籍注销证明) from the police
  • Proof of settling abroad issued or authenticated by a Chinese embassy/consulate
  • Property-rights documents and the tax certificate / 完税凭证 from the tax authority
  • One application for the entire amount
Exact steps
  1. Confirm you hold the 户籍注销证明 and embassy-authenticated settlement proof.
  2. Obtain the tax certificate for the assets from the competent tax authority.
  3. File one application with the SAFE branch where your household was registered.
  4. After approval, take the 核准件 to the designated bank.
  5. Have the bank remit the approved amount to your own account abroad, in one transfer or several — your choice within the approved total.

Not verified:

  • Eligibility depends on private facts (whether you ever held PRC hukou)

Correction. Corrected 11 November 2026. This card previously gave the 2004 instalment schedule (half, half of the remainder after one year, the balance after two) and SAFE head-office approval above RMB 500,000 as current rules. SAFE Huifa [2014] No. 2 Section 6 removed both with effect from 10 February 2014. S17

R07 Inheritance asset transfer (继承财产转移)

Lawful if eligible

Only for a lawful inheritance of a mainland estate

Who it fits
A foreign citizen (or HK/Macau resident) who has lawfully inherited a mainland estate
Legal basis
Provisional Measures on Individuals' Outward Asset Transfers Arts. 2, 9, 11, as changed by SAFE Huifa [2014] No. 2 Section 6 S04 S17
Caps / limits
Approved by the SAFE branch where the deceased was registered; the head-office step for totals above RMB 500,000 was removed in 2014. Property inherited from different deceased persons may be put in one combined application. After approval the bank may remit once or in several payments.

Pros

  • Clear statutory route for inherited funds
  • Can be remitted in one or several tranches

Cons

  • Only applies to inherited property, not your own savings
  • Notarisation and tax certificates take time
Documents required
  • Proof of inheritance (will/probate/bank records), notarised if not already
  • Deceased's property-rights documents and the tax certificate from the tax authority where the estate is
  • Proof of settling abroad authenticated by a Chinese embassy/consulate
  • One application for all property inherited from the same person
Exact steps
  1. Collect the inheritance documents and the deceased's property documents.
  2. Obtain the tax certificate / 完税凭证.
  3. Apply to the SAFE branch where the deceased was registered.
  4. Remit through the designated bank to your own account abroad.

Not verified:

  • Eligibility depends on private facts

Correction. Corrected 11 November 2026. This card previously said approval above RMB 500,000 goes to SAFE headquarters and that estates of different deceased persons cannot be combined. SAFE Huifa [2014] No. 2 Section 6 removed both requirements with effect from 10 February 2014. S17

R08 Sell mainland property, then repatriate the proceeds

Lawful if eligible

Capital-account route with its own approvals

Who it fits
Owner of mainland real estate selling as a foreign individual
Legal basis
PBOC Order No. 3 Art. 22 (non-domestic individual buying commercial housing, own-use principle) and the property FX rules it cross-refers to S03 S02 S04
Caps / limits
Not determined by the documents I verified this session — the specific property-repatriation rules were not re-read here.

Pros

  • Converts an illiquid asset into a remittable balance
  • Tax on the gain is assessed on the sale, separately

Cons

  • Property-specific approvals on top of everything else
  • Market and transaction risk; selling is slow
  • The precise repatriation document list is unverified in this session
Documents required
  • Completed sale with title transfer and tax paid
  • Bank/SAFE review of the capital-account documentation
  • Proof the property was acquired lawfully
Exact steps
  1. Obtain the property-specific document list from the bank and the local SAFE branch before listing.
  2. Complete the sale and settle all taxes.
  3. Apply for the outward transfer of the net proceeds.
  4. Remit to your own account abroad.

Not verified:

  • Property repatriation document list
  • Any property-specific cap

R09 Entrust a near relative to act for you within their own quota

Lawful if eligible

Lawful for genuine family support; not a quota pool

Who it fits
Where genuine family support or a documented transaction exists
Legal basis
Current Account FX Business Guidelines 2020 Arts. 61 and 62 S01 S02
Caps / limits
Within-quota agency is limited to the relative's own USD 50,000 facilitation quota, and only for genuine purposes. Using relatives' quotas to move YOUR money is the prohibited 分拆 (Art. 62).

Pros

  • Practical for genuine support payments to family
  • Expressly permitted with the right paperwork

Cons

  • Cannot be used to multiply your own quota — that is the exact conduct Art. 62 targets
  • Both sides risk the '关注名单' watchlist and a 3-year enhanced review
Documents required
  • Entrustment with both IDs, a power of attorney and a near-relative relationship document (household register, marriage certificate, birth certificate)
  • A genuine underlying transaction or support obligation
Exact steps
  1. Confirm the transfer is genuine support, not a split of your own balance.
  2. Prepare the power of attorney and relationship proof.
  3. The relative applies within their own quota for the genuine purpose.
  4. Keep the support rationale documented.

  • Nothing recorded

R12 Carry USD cash out in person

Lawful, capped

Legal only in small amounts; impractical

Who it fits
Traveller carrying their own converted cash
Legal basis
Provisional Measures on Carrying FX Cash Across the Border 汇发〔2003〕102号 Arts. 3, 5, 7; 条例 Art. 42; CBP currency reporting S05 S02 S13
Caps / limits
China: ≤ USD 5,000 no permit; USD 5,000–10,000 bank 携带证; above USD 10,000 a SAFE-branch permit, and in principle not above USD 10,000 at all (Art. 7). US: report amounts over USD 10,000 on FinCEN 105. China penalty for illegal carriage: warning plus fine up to 20% (条例 Art. 42).

Pros

  • No wire fee
  • Immediate

Cons

  • Hard limit of about USD 10,000 per trip
  • Physical loss/theft risk
  • US declaration obligation and seizure risk if not filed
  • Carrying USD 400,000 is not lawful in one trip and is unsafe in any case
Documents required
  • Conversion and cash withdrawal inside China (bank 携带证 above USD 5,000)
  • US FinCEN Form 105 above USD 10,000
Exact steps
  1. Convert at the bank and request cash with a 携带证 if between USD 5,000 and 10,000.
  2. Declare to CBP on FinCEN 105 if bringing in more than USD 10,000.
  3. Never split cash among travellers to stay under thresholds — CBP aggregates family/group amounts (S13).

Not verified:

  • Whether any bank will issue a 携带证 for your amount
