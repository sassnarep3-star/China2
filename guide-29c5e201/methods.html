All transfer / conversion routes

Routes are categorized by what they actually authorize, who may qualify and whether the path fits ordinary personal savings. A listed method is not a recommendation or guaranteed bank acceptance.

Most likely for a U.S. citizen: documented-income bank conversion/remittance (M01) when the balance is traceable to lawful current-account income and tax vouchers exist. A U.S. citizen is not presumed eligible for the PRC ¥50,000 equivalent purchase facilitation quota. Every route below has source-linked steps and cost detail.

M01 · Bank SWIFT wire of documented after-tax China income (foreign individual) lawful-conditional

Who / eligibility: Non-mainland-Chinese individual (e.g., US citizen) with lawful current-account RMB income earned in China (salary, services) and tax vouchers. The key route for 'money stuck in a China bank account'.

Pros
  • No annual numeric cap stated by SAFE for documented lawful income - the USD 50,000 facilitation quota is not the ceiling for this route (S03/S05).
  • Bank handles conversion + remittance in one process; fully documented and auditable.
  • HSBC published tariff caps the issuance fee at CNY 500 + CNY 120 cable per wire regardless of size (S29) - large wires are cheap in fixed-fee terms.
  • Current international payments are not restricted in principle (S13 Art. 5).
Cons / cautions
  • Requires ID + source-of-funds materials including tax vouchers for the transaction amount (S03); undocumented balances cannot use it.
  • If Chinese tax was underpaid on the source income, it must be resolved first - that is income tax, not a conversion tax (S15).
  • FX spread and correspondent-bank charges are not published (F03) - the all-in cost cannot be verified in advance.
  • Bank authenticity review and processing time; bank may request additional documents.
Step-by-step (conditional on bank/provider approval)
  1. 1. Assemble passport/ID, employment contract or income proof, and PRC tax records (tax vouchers) covering the amount.
  2. 2. If tax on the source income is unpaid/underpaid, resolve it with the tax authority first (S15); obtain tax records.
  3. 3. At your China bank, apply to purchase USD with the documented RMB income (S03; Guidelines Art. 59).
  4. 4. Instruct an outward SWIFT remittance to your own US account; provide receiving bank details (SWIFT/BIC, account, address).
  5. 5. Pay the published tariff (e.g., HSBC Premier/Advance: 0.1% min CNY 100 max CNY 500 + CNY 120 cable; optional Pay Exact CNY 200) (S29).
  6. 6. Keep the remittance receipt and tax records for US reporting (FBAR/8938 if thresholds met; S17/S18).

M02 · Bank wire within the USD 50,000/year facilitation quota (PRC ID or Foreign Permanent Resident ID holders) lawful-conditional

Who / eligibility: PRC domestic individuals; foreign individuals ONLY with the PRC Foreign Permanent Resident ID Card (S05). A US citizen without that card generally uses M01 instead.

Pros
  • ID-only formalities within the quota - no transaction documents (S01 Art. 2).
  • Same bank infrastructure as M01; published tariff applies.
  • Spreading over calendar years is lawful for the quota holder's own funds (one person, one quota).
Cons / cautions
  • USD 50,000 equivalent per person per year (S01 Art. 2): USD 400,000 needs 8 calendar years on this route alone.
  • Not available to most foreign passport holders (S05).
  • Using other people's quotas for one beneficiary is prohibited splitting (S06 Art. 7; see M15).
  • Spread still applies and is unpublished (F03).
Step-by-step (conditional on bank/provider approval)
  1. 1. Confirm you hold a PRC ID or Foreign Permanent Resident ID Card (S05).
  2. 2. At the bank, purchase USD within the remaining annual quota with ID (S01 Art. 2).
  3. 3. Remit abroad: domestic individuals <=USD 50,000/day from FX savings account with ID; above with authenticity documents (S01 Art. 14).
  4. 4. Repeat in later calendar years for amounts above the quota, or use document-based routes for the excess.
  5. 5. Keep receipts; comply with US reporting (S17/S18).

M03 · HSBC Global Transfer to your own overseas HSBC account lawful-conditional

Who / eligibility: Customers with HSBC China accounts AND own-name HSBC accounts in the destination country. Conversion of CNY still requires a lawful basis (M01/M02).

Pros
  • Published fee: Free for Private Banking and Premier; Advance USD 20 per transaction (S29 2.2.6.1).
  • Own-name transfer; simple audit trail.
  • No correspondent-fee uncertainty for the HSBC-internal leg (tariff note 7 still excludes other institutions/government charges).
Cons / cautions
  • Requires an HSBC account in both countries; Premier eligibility conditions (e.g., TRB >=CNY 500,000) apply (S29 Note 3).
  • Does not bypass SAFE conversion rules - the CNY->USD purchase still needs quota or documents (S01/S03).
  • FX spread unpublished (F03); per-transaction limits not in the tariff (F03).
Step-by-step (conditional on bank/provider approval)
  1. 1. Open/confirm own-name HSBC accounts in China and the US (eligibility per S29).
  2. 2. Convert CNY to USD inside HSBC China on a lawful basis (M01 documents or M02 quota).
  3. 3. Send via Global Transfer to your own US HSBC account (S29 2.2.6.1).
  4. 4. Keep records; US reporting applies (S17/S18).

M04 · Wise (from-CNY) to your own foreign account lawful-conditional

Who / eligibility: Non-mainland-Chinese individuals who work/worked in China with PRC tax records showing tax paid within the last 3 years; personal accounts; funds from your own Chinese account to another account you own (S30).

Pros
  • Provider states overall limit = documented after-China-tax salary, does not expire (S30) - no USD 50k/year staging needed within that limit.
  • Single transfer up to CNY 300,000 via bank-app pay-in (S30).
  • Fully regulated partner (PBOC-licensed) pay-in flow; transparent in-app quote before sending.
Cons / cautions
  • Only your own accounts as recipient; no third-party payments (S30).
  • Requires recent tax records; balances without documented taxed income do not qualify (S30).
  • Fees are shown only at setup - not statically published, so no verified cost table is possible (F03).
  • Mainland Chinese nationals cannot use from-CNY sending (S30).
Step-by-step (conditional on bank/provider approval)
  1. 1. Create a Wise personal account; complete first-time verification (1-2 working days per S30).
  2. 2. Upload PRC tax records (tax paid within last 3 years); Wise assigns an overall limit from them (S30).
  3. 3. Set up CNY -> USD transfer to your own US account; review the in-app fee and rate quote.
  4. 4. Pay from your own Chinese bank app to the provided PBOC-regulated partner ACS account (<=CNY 300,000/transfer), or via Alipay (<=CNY 50,000).
  5. 5. Wise converts and pays out; keep records for US reporting.
Primary sources: S30 · Wise (wise.com) - provider primary source

M05 · Western Union from China (partner-bank online or in-person, cash pickup abroad) lawful-conditional

Who / eligibility: Residents of China sending via WU partner banks (CEB, SPDB, PSBC, BOC mobile-only online; walk-in at partner banks). Passport accepted; bank account may be required (S31).

Pros
  • Cash pickup abroad in minutes; 525,000+ locations (S31).
  • Online sending via several partner-bank apps after profile registration (S31).
  • US is on PSBC's 84-country whitelist (S31).
Cons / cautions
  • At retrieval, transfers to recipient bank accounts were 'temporarily unavailable' - cash pickup only (S31 banner; F07).
  • Fees and per-transfer limits not statically published (F03).
  • Agents are banks subject to local FX rules and risk controls - SAFE conversion basis still applies (S31).
  • Cash pickup of >USD 10,000 then deposited in the US may trigger bank reporting; carrying cash across the US border >USD 10,000 requires FinCEN 105 (S28).
Step-by-step (conditional on bank/provider approval)
  1. 1. Register a WU profile with a partner bank (online) or visit a partner-bank branch with passport (S31).
  2. 2. Provide receiver details and purpose; pay from your debit card/account plus the quoted fee.
  3. 3. Receive MTCN; share only with your intended receiver.
  4. 4. Receiver picks up cash with ID at a WU location.
  5. 5. Keep records; US reporting applies to the underlying account/income (S17/S18).
Primary sources: S31 · Western Union (westernunion.com) - provider primary source

M06 · Overseas ATM cash withdrawals with a China-issued bank card lawful-conditional

Who / eligibility: Holders of domestic (mainland-issued) bank cards withdrawing cash abroad. Separate from the USD 50k quota; a parallel cash channel (S07).

Pros
  • No tax documents needed at the ATM; card network converts automatically.
  • Useful for small amounts; RMB 10,000/card/day ceiling (S07).
Cons / cautions
  • Hard cap RMB 100,000/person/calendar year across ALL your cards (~USD 14,027 at the S23 reference rate) (S07).
  • Exceeding it suspends overseas withdrawals for the current AND following year (S07).
  • ATM operator + issuer fees and card-network FX margin are not published here (F03).
  • USD 400,000 would take ~29 calendar years - impractical (computed).
  • Borrowing others' cards to evade is prohibited (S07).
Step-by-step (conditional on bank/provider approval)
  1. 1. Use your own mainland-issued card at an overseas ATM.
  2. 2. Track cumulative withdrawals across all your cards: <=RMB 100,000/year, <=RMB 10,000/card/day (S07).
  3. 3. Deposit withdrawn cash into your US account; keep records.
  4. 4. For physical carry into the US >USD 10,000 at one time, file FinCEN 105 (S28).
Primary sources: S07 · SAFE (safe.gov.cn)S13 · SAFE Beijing (safe.gov.cn)

M07 · Buy USD cash in China and carry it out (within customs permit rules) lawful-conditional

Who / eligibility: Travelers exiting China. <=USD 5,000 equivalent no permit; USD 5,000-10,000 with bank-issued carrying permit; >USD 10,000 in principle not allowed (S10).

Pros
  • No permit needed <=USD 5,000/trip (S10).
  • HSBC counter banknote withdrawal is published Free (S29 2.2.2.1).
  • US entry with <=USD 10,000 needs no FinCEN 105; above is legal WITH reporting (S28).
Cons / cautions
  • USD 400,000 = 80 permit-free trips (computed) - travel costs and risk dominate; repeat-traveler rules cut limits to USD 500/1,000 for same-day/15-day repeats (S10 Art. 6).
  • Notes buy/sell board rates apply (spread unpublished, F03); >=USD 5,000 withdrawal needs 3 working days notice at HSBC (S29).
  • Unlawful carrying: warning + fine up to 20% (S13 Art. 42); US non-declaration risks forfeiture (S28).
  • Physical loss/theft risk; no audit trail unless documented.
Step-by-step (conditional on bank/provider approval)
  1. 1. Purchase USD cash on a lawful basis (M01/M02) and withdraw at the counter (S29; >=USD 5,000: reserve 3 working days ahead).
  2. 2. <=USD 5,000: exit without permit. USD 5,000-10,000: obtain bank carrying permit (valid 30 days, single use) (S10).
  3. 3. Do not exceed USD 10,000 per exit without a SAFE special-case permit (S10 Art. 7).
  4. 4. On US arrival, if carrying >USD 10,000 (family/group combined), file FinCEN 105 (S28).
  5. 5. Deposit in your US bank; keep China withdrawal/purchase receipts.
Primary sources: S10 · gov.cn (State Council Gazette)S02 · SAFE (safe.gov.cn)S28 · US Customs and Border Protection (cbp.gov)S29 · HSBC China (hsbc.com.cn) - provider primary sourceS13 · SAFE Beijing (safe.gov.cn)

M08 · Carry RMB banknotes out (<=CNY 20,000/trip) and convert abroad lawful-conditional

Who / eligibility: Travelers; RMB cash limit CNY 20,000 per person per trip (S11/S12). Conversion then happens OUTSIDE China at offshore rates.

Pros
  • Avoids onshore conversion paperwork for small amounts.
  • Lawful within the per-trip limit.
Cons / cautions
  • ~USD 2,806/trip at the S23 reference rate; USD 400,000 = ~143 trips (computed) - impractical.
  • Offshore RMB conversion rates/fees not published here (F03).
  • Excess RMB is returned by customs; penalties for non-declaration (S12); unlawful carrying fine up to 20% (S13 Art. 42).
Step-by-step (conditional on bank/provider approval)
  1. 1. Withdraw <=CNY 20,000 cash.
  2. 2. Exit China within the limit; declare if required.
  3. 3. Convert RMB to USD at a bank/exchange abroad (offshore rate).
  4. 4. US entry >USD 10,000 equivalent: FinCEN 105 (S28).

M09 · Reconvert unused RMB previously exchanged from foreign currency lawful-conditional

Who / eligibility: Foreign individuals holding RMB that was previously converted FROM foreign currency and left unused, with the original exchange memo (24-month validity) (S01 Art. 13(2), S04).

Pros
  • With the original memo: reconversion up to the memo amount, no tax documents (S04).
  • ID-only small reconversions: <=USD 500/day onshore, <=USD 1,000/day airside before departure (S04).
Cons / cautions
  • ONLY for previously exchanged unused RMB - not a general route for salary/savings (S04; fixes the overbroad 'USD 500/day for anyone' claim, see audit A06).
  • Memo expires 24 months after the original exchange (S04).
  • USD 400,000 via ID-only USD 500/day = 800 bank days (computed) - impractical; memo route limited to original amount.
Step-by-step (conditional on bank/provider approval)
  1. 1. Locate the original exchange memo (water slip) from when you converted USD->CNY.
  2. 2. Within 24 months, present ID + memo at the bank; reconvert up to the unused memo amount (S04).
  3. 3. Without a memo, only the ID-only daily small-amount reconversion applies (<=USD 500 onshore / <=USD 1,000 airside).
  4. 4. Remit the reconvened FX abroad with ID (S01 Art. 15).
Primary sources: S04 · SAFE Tianjin (safe.gov.cn)S01 · SAFE Tianjin (safe.gov.cn)

M10 · Emigrant personal asset transfer (移民财产转移) - SAFE-approved capital transfer lawful-conditional

Who / eligibility: Individuals who EMIGRATED FROM Mainland China (assets obtained before immigration permission) - typically former PRC residents, not US-born citizens (S09). Inheritor variant covers foreign citizens inheriting China assets (M11).

Pros
  • SAFE approval allows lump-sum remittance of the approved amount - no USD 50k/year staging (S08 Section VI).
  • Covers assets beyond salary (property, savings) if lawful, owned and undisputed (S09).
Cons / cautions
  • Eligibility is narrow: emigrants from Mainland China with hukou cancellation/immigration proofs (S09); most US citizens born abroad do not qualify (analysis, F05).
  • SAFE application at original registered residence; documentary burden; approval amount limits the transfer (S09).
  • Tax clearance on the underlying assets required; emigration triggers a PRC tax return (S15 Art. 10(5)).
  • No published fixed fee schedule for the approval process (F03).
Step-by-step (conditional on bank/provider approval)
  1. 1. Confirm emigrant status (immigration permission + PRC hukou cancellation proofs) (S09).
  2. 2. Settle PRC tax on the assets; emigration tax return (S15 Art. 10(5)).
  3. 3. Apply to SAFE at the original registered-residence branch with written application, ID, asset-source proofs (S09).
  4. 4. After approval, bank purchases FX and remits lump sum or batches within the approved limit (S08).
  5. 5. US reporting: FBAR/8938 for the China account;移民 transfer itself is not US-taxable income (moving own funds; S16 worldwide-income rules still apply to any income).

M11 · Inheritor asset transfer (继承财产转移) - foreign citizen inheriting China assets lawful-conditional

Who / eligibility: Foreign citizens (and HK/Macau residents) inheriting assets located in China from a decedent (S09).

Pros
  • One application per decedent; lump sum or installments within SAFE approval (S08/S09).
  • No USD 50k quota staging for the approved inheritance (S08).
Cons / cautions
  • Requires inheritance legal documents/notarization and tax clearance; SAFE approval at decedent's original registered residence (S09).
  • Only inherited assets - not a route for your own savings.
  • Process fees/timeline not published (F03).
Step-by-step (conditional on bank/provider approval)
  1. 1. Obtain inheritance legal documents or notarial deeds (S01 Art. 10(3) context; S09).
  2. 2. Settle applicable PRC taxes on the estate/assets.
  3. 3. Apply to SAFE where the decedent's residence was registered (S09).
  4. 4. Bank remits the approved amount lump sum or in batches (S08).
  5. 5. US: inheritance from a nonresident alien >USD 100,000 triggers Form 3520 reporting (S16); FBAR/8938 may apply to the China account while held.

M12 · Remit proceeds of selling China commercial property (foreign individual) lawful-conditional

Who / eligibility: Non-domestic individuals who owned and sold commercial housing in China (own-use principle at purchase) (S06 Art. 22).

Pros
  • PBOC rules explicitly allow converting and remitting sale proceeds (S06 Art. 22).
  • No USD 50k quota staging for documented sale proceeds.
Cons / cautions
  • PRC IIT 20% on the GAIN (proceeds minus original value and reasonable expenses) plus other transaction taxes/fees not quantified here (S15 Art. 3(3), 6(5); F03).
  • Requires sale contract, tax vouchers, property documents; bank authenticity review.
  • Only if you actually own sellable property - not applicable to a plain bank balance.
Step-by-step (conditional on bank/provider approval)
  1. 1. Complete the property sale; pay PRC IIT on the gain and transaction taxes (S15).
  2. 2. Obtain tax vouchers and sale documents.
  3. 3. At the bank, convert the after-tax RMB proceeds to USD and remit with ID + documents (S06 Art. 22).
  4. 4. US: report the sale and any gain on your US return; FTC for PRC tax may apply subject to limits (S20); FBAR/8938 while funds are in China.

M13 · Individual foreign-trade settlement account (genuine trade, quota-exempt) lawful-conditional

Who / eligibility: Registered individual foreign-trade operators / 个体工商户 with genuine trade activity (S01 Art. 9; S02).

Pros
  • Purchases/sales under the settlement account are NOT subject to the annual quota, regardless of amount, with valid trading documents (S02).
Cons / cautions
  • Requires genuine registered trade - fabricating contracts to move savings is prohibited (S06 Art. 7; S13 Art. 40; see M17).
  • Not a route for an existing personal bank balance with no trade background.
  • Trade compliance, customs and tax obligations attach.
Step-by-step (conditional on bank/provider approval)
  1. 1. Register as an individual foreign-trade operator (S01 Art. 9).
  2. 2. Open a foreign-exchange settlement account (managed as an institutional account).
  3. 3. Conduct genuine trade; settle FX with valid trading documents regardless of amount (S02).
  4. 4. Pay applicable trade taxes; keep documents.
Primary sources: S01 · SAFE Tianjin (safe.gov.cn)S02 · SAFE (safe.gov.cn)S06 · People's Bank of China (pbc.gov.cn)

M14 · Crypto / stablecoin bridge (CNY -> USDT -> USD) rejected-unlawful

Who / eligibility: Not recommended. Documented because the brief asks for as many ways as possible.

Pros
  • (None endorsed.) Technically attempted by some; speed is the only claimed advantage - unverified and not endorsed.
Cons / cautions
  • Virtual-currency-related business is illegal financial activity in China; fiat-crypto exchange strictly prohibited (S14).
  • Financial institutions may not provide accounts/transfers/settlement for it (S14).
  • Overseas providers may not illegally serve domestic subjects; assisting is punishable (S14).
  • Civil acts contrary to public order are void - losses borne by the investor (S14 item 19).
  • If it constitutes illegal FX trading/evasion: fines up to 30%, serious 30-100%, criminal liability (S13 Arts. 39/45).
Step-by-step (conditional on bank/provider approval)
  1. Do not use. The lawful alternatives are M01-M13.

M15 · Quota splitting / pooling friends' and relatives' USD 50k quotas ('smurfing') rejected-unlawful

Who / eligibility: Not recommended.

Pros
  • (None endorsed.)
Cons / cautions
  • Explicitly prohibited: no evading limit administration by splitting large amounts into small batches (S06 Art. 7).
  • SAFE's bank-SAFE information system is designed to detect repeated installment purchases (S02).
  • Evasion/fraudulent purchase fines up to 30%; serious cases 30-100% + criminal liability (S13 Arts. 39-40).
  • Participants' own quotas and banking access can be affected.
Step-by-step (conditional on bank/provider approval)
  1. Do not use. Lawful staging is your OWN quota across calendar years (M02) or the documented-income route (M01).
