Executive summary · 8 November 2026

The answer, and its limits

For a US citizen with RMB in a mainland bank account, the cheapest lawful way out is almost never a clever structure. It is the documented route: prove the source, prove the tax was paid, convert at an authorised bank, and wire. That costs 0% in Chinese tax on the conversion and 0% in US tax on the movement. Everything else in this dossier is detail around that sentence.

1. The premise needs correcting

The starting assumption was that converting CNY to USD triggers a considerable tax. The statutes do not support that. Article 2 of the PRC Individual Income Tax Law taxes nine categories of income; a currency conversion is not one of them. On the US side, moving your own funds is not a realisation event, and §988(e)(1)-(2) excludes an individual's personal currency transactions from §988, with no gain recognised unless it would exceed $200. At $400,000 the verified statutory cost of converting already-taxed money is $0.

2. Where the money actually goes

  • Income tax on the source (3%–45% or a flat 20%) — owed whether or not the money leaves China. This is the real "penalty" in most cases.
  • The documentary gate — Article 13(1) of the implementing rules requires identity plus source documents including tax vouchers. A missing certificate is a refusal, not a charge.
  • The commercial spread and wire fees — real, unavoidable, and unpublished by any regulator. The only figure that requires your own bank's quote.
  • Reporting duties — FBAR over $10,000, Form 8938 from $50,000/$75,000, Form 3520 from $100,000 of gifts, FinCEN Form 105 over $10,000 in cash. Zero cost if filed.
  • Fines on prohibited channels — up to 30%, or 30%–100% if serious, plus confiscation. This is the only layer that can exceed the balance.

3. The route, in order

  1. Establish ownership and the lawful source of the balance, and locate the tax evidence.
  2. If the money came from an earlier FX conversion, use the original exchange memo (M02) — the only route needing no tax documents, valid 24 months.
  3. If the balance is already in foreign currency, remit it from the FX savings account (M04) — no conversion means no spread.
  4. Otherwise use M01: documented conversion plus wire, with the full document set.
  5. Ask the bank, in writing, for the all-in price and for which category it will record you under.
  6. File the US information returns for the years concerned.

No provider is named cheapest, because no official source publishes spreads. No personal tax amount is stated, because the personal facts are unknown. No commercial fee is filled in. No individual eligibility conclusion is drawn, and the question of whether a long-term US resident of China may use the USD 50,000 quota is flagged rather than resolved. 6. The one thing to do first

Before any bank visit: establish whether the PRC tax on the income was paid and obtain the certificate. That single document decides whether this is a 0% transaction or a 3%–45% one.
