Getting money out of China with the least lawful penalty

A US citizen's balance sits in a Chinese bank. This dashboard answers three questions in order: what is actually being taxed, which routes exist, and what each route costs from $10,000 to $400,000 — with a link to the primary document behind every number, and blanks where nothing official exists.

The answer in 60 seconds

  1. There is no "conversion tax" in either country. The PRC's FX rules impose document, quota and authenticity requirements plus evasion penalties — not a tax on exchanging currency (FX Regulations, PBOC Decree 3). The IRS taxes income, not conversions (26 USC §988).
  2. What feels like a penalty is five separate things — PRC income tax owed when the money was earned; the bank's FX spread; wire fees; US residual tax (with foreign tax credit / FEIE); and reporting penalties if FBAR/Form 8938/FinCEN 105 are missed. Each has its own fix. See Tax & reporting.
  3. Least-friction route: documented current-account income → bank conversion + wire (M01). You need your passport plus source-of-funds materials including PRC tax records for the amount (SAFE 2026 FAQ). If the underlying PRC tax was never paid, resolve that first — that is income tax, not a conversion charge.
  4. Cheapest published wire fee: HSBC Private Banking individual customers are published Free; Premier/Advance pay 0.1% (min ¥100, max ¥500) plus a ¥120 cable (tariff 2.2.4.1–2.2.4.2). At the IRS 2025 average rate of 7.129 that is $30.86 at $10,000, capped at $86.97 from about $80,000 upward. Same-name HSBC Global Transfer is free for Premier/Private Banking, USD 20 per transfer for Advance (2.2.6.1).
  5. The $50,000/year quota is not your route unless you hold a PRC Foreign Permanent Resident ID Card (SAFE 2024 FAQ); it is an annual total for domestic individuals and card holders (Implementing Rules Art. 2).
  6. Never split the quota, use crypto/stablecoin bridges, underground banks or fabricated documents. Conversion between RMB and virtual currency inside China is an illegal financial activity (Yinfa [2026] 42) and FX evasion carries fines of up to 30% — or 30% to 100% in serious cases (Arts. 39–45).

What to ask the bank, in writing

  1. Which tax, taxpayer, period and legal basis is the hold about — a tax already assessed, a tax certificate you must supply, an FX eligibility question, or a bank fee? Request the refusal in writing.
  2. Which FX category applies to my balance (documented current-account income under Huifa [2020] No. 14 Art. 59, or the annual facilitation quota)?
  3. List the source-of-funds documents you will accept, including the PRC tax records.
  4. Provide an executable quote: applied CNY/USD rate, sender fee, cable, correspondent/intermediary charges and the recipient bank's charge.
  5. Confirm the customer segment your tariff applies to — HSBC's published wire fee is free for Private Banking individuals and 0.1% (min ¥100 / max ¥500) + ¥120 cable for Premier/Advance.
