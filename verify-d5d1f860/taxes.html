Tax & reporting: breaking down the "conversion penalty"

No reviewed instrument imposes a tax on the act of converting CNY to USD. What people experience as a penalty decomposes into five layers. Four of them are avoidable or reducible with documents; one (unpaid PRC income tax) is not.

Currency gain: when a conversion can itself create US tax

For an individual's personal transaction, exchange-rate gain is not recognised if it is $200 or less; above that the exclusion does not apply (26 USC §988(e)(2)). The statute is the 2024 GPO compilation — later amendments are not reflected here, so confirm the current edition. Basis and holding history of the RMB are private facts and were not assumed.

The IRS has no official exchange rate and generally accepts any consistently used posted rate (IRS). A "conversion penalty" calculated by multiplying the balance by a tax rate is therefore not supported by any reviewed text.

What this page does not do

It does not compute your tax. The PRC tax already paid, the source and date of the balance, your residence days, other foreign accounts, filing history and the property/employment character of the money are all unknown. A number presented as "your tax" would be invented. The actionable substitute is the document list in M01 and the bank questions on the overview.

Scope and limits. Every source on this site was fetched and quoted on 2026-11-10; the quote shown is the text relied on. Values a provider or regulator does not publish are left blank and flagged — blank means unverified, not zero. No private account facts were used, no bank was contacted, no funds moved and no return filed. The 24 sources here are a re-verification of load-bearing claims, not a legal opinion or a personalised tax computation.
